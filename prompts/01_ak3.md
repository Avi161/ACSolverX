## The target, precisely

**AK(3)** is the Akbulut–Kirby presentation

```
AK(3) = ⟨ x, y | xyx = yxy,  x³ = y⁴ ⟩ = ⟨ x, y | x³y⁻⁴,  xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹ ⟩,   total length 13.
```

In this repo's string encoding (lowercase = generator, uppercase = inverse; each relator a cyclic word), the two relators are `r1 = xxxYYYY` (= x³y⁻⁴, length 7) and `r2 = xyxYXY` (= xyxy⁻¹x⁻¹y⁻¹, length 6). Its canonical representative in the unsolved-reps data is **`aca_115` = `13_1`**, stored in `data/ms_unsolved_reps/ak3_only.csv` as `r1 = YXYxyx`, `r2 = YYYYxxx`. AK(3) is Aut(F₂)-equivalent to Miller–Schupp rep `13_1`, which is a **singleton class** (class 116 of the 124) — it has no known equivalent among the 261 unsolved reps.

**Why this is *the* case.** AK(3) is the **unique minimal open case** of the Andrews–Curtis conjecture. Miasnikov (GA) and Myasnikov–Myasnikov (MM03) exhaustively certified that *every* balanced 2-generator presentation of the trivial group of total length ≤ 12 is AC-trivial. Havas–Ramsay showed every length-≤13 such presentation is AC-equivalent to the trivial one **or** to AK(3). So length 13 is exactly the boundary where the conjecture first becomes open, and AK(3) sits on it. Settling AK(3) is a primary goal of this project.

## What a complete resolution requires

Because the two directions are wildly asymmetric (see the contract), state at the outset which you are attempting, and hold yourself to the matching bar.

- **Affirmative — AK(3) is AC-trivial (unstable).** A move path of AC1–AC3 (equivalently Definition 2.1 supermoves) from AK(3) to `⟨x,y | x, y⟩`, that passes `experiments/stable_ac/verify_results.py` by independent replay. This is the strongest possible positive result: it settles the minimal open case in favour of the conjecture. Nothing weaker — a reduction to a shorter presentation, a stable trivialization, a heuristic — is this result.
- **Affirmative — AK(3) is stably AC-trivial.** A path using AC4/AC5 (stabilization) or a certified Lemma-11 / CoV chain, again independently replayed, with the automorphism steps certified as genuine F₂ automorphisms. This is a real result **and strictly weaker than the unstable one** — label it as stable, never conflate the two. Note: AK(3)'s stable status is currently **OPEN** (see traps), so a *verified* stable trivialization would itself be new.
- **Negative — AK(3) is a counterexample.** A rigorous, computable **AC-invariant obstruction**: a quantity provably constant on AC-classes taking different values on AK(3) and on `⟨x,y|x,y⟩`. This is the actual 60-year-open problem and is believed to require a genuinely new invariant. This project has *proved* the usual layers cannot supply one (`OBSTRUCTION_BARRIER.md`): abelianization is forced to `|det| = 1`, and the presentation 2-complex is contractible with trivial Whitehead group, so no homological / quotient / π₂-module / torsion invariant works. If you pursue this direction, your deliverable is an *obstruction with a proof*, or — absent that — an honest, bounded map of what was ruled out. **A stalled search is not this result and never will be.**

## What does NOT count (AK(3)-specific traps)

Beyond the contract's ten red lines, these have specifically burned prior work on AK(3):

1. **The broken MMS02 stable chain.** Do not build on, cite, or "re-verify" the claim that AK(3) is known stably AC-trivial via MMS02 Thm 1.4 → the length-25 presentation → AK(3). Shehper et al. found a misprint (MMS02's 13th Wirtinger relator, printed `x₅x₁₂x₅⁻¹`, must be `x₄x₁₂x₄⁻¹`); the presentation is not a genuine Wirtinger presentation, so the stable mechanism is void. Lisitsa (arXiv:2501.18601) re-proves **only** the unstable link `P ~AC AK(3)` and inherits the broken stable leg. Some in-repo notes still loosely say "AK(3) is known stably trivial" — that phrasing is **stale**; treat the stable status as OPEN and verify against `ac-advisor.md` Red Line 1.
2. **Reaching `Q` (or any stable-class sibling) is not progress.** MMS02 Prop 1.2 gives a fully-written chain from AK(3) to a longer presentation `Q` in the *same stable class*. By construction `Q` shares AK(3)'s wall — routing through it buys restart diversity at best, never a shortcut. Same for any presentation you reach that is stably-equivalent to AK(3).
3. **The length-13 floor holds two Aut-orbits.** `min_relator_length` cannot see an orbit switch at the same length. AK(3)'s own orbit and a second orbit — **orbit-2**, rep `YYXXyx | YYYxyXX` (relations `yx = x²y²`, `xy = y³x²`) — both sit at total length 13, and orbit-2 is *classically AC-equivalent to AK(3)* (they merge into one 1000-state component at ceiling 17; below ceiling 15 AK(3) is an isolated vertex). So "stuck at 13" is a statement about length, not about orbits — census the Aut-orbits (`aut_canon`) at the floor before concluding anything.
4. **CoV is near-inert on AK(3).** The whole subword-CoV family reaches only **2** Aut-orbits in one hop, 12 in two, 55 in three — and *every* reachable orbit still has total length ≥ 13. Iterated CoV buys more restart points, **none closer to trivial**. Do not expect the CoV levers that help the 124 residual to move AK(3); its real bets are non-CoV (below).
5. **"Unsolved at budget B" is not a counterexample** (contract Red Line 3, repeated because AK(3) is where the temptation is strongest). A 1M-node baseline sees zero progress from AK(3); that is a budget statement.

## The attack menu (with honest priors)

Grounded in `experiments/IDEAS.md`, `experiments/IMPLEMENTATION_IDEAS.md`, and `results/stable_ac/ak3/RESULTS.md`. Ranked by what could actually *end* AK(3)'s open status, not by ease:

1. **Thickenability certificate (the only lever that could *close* AK(3)).** Lackenby (arXiv:2606.06122): a *thickenable* balanced trivial-group presentation satisfies the unstable AC conjecture (Thm 1.3), and thickenability is decidable by Neuwirth's algorithm. For a 2-generator presentation this reduces to a finite check — whether the 4-vertex Whitehead-graph link admits an edge-consistent genus-0 rotation system. `NEUWIRTH_FEASIBILITY.md` gives this a **GO** verdict (~1 person-week for the certificate checker). **Critical safety rule:** a false positive here silently prints a proof that AK(3) is AC-trivial — the single riskiest failure mode in the whole project. Every "thickenable" verdict must be cross-checked against an independent tool (Regina, confirmed importable in the machine's Python) before it is believed. This leaves numba for 3-manifold topology; scope it accordingly.
2. **Production greedy from orbit-2 and its floor states, at scale.** Orbit-2 (`YYXXyx | YYYxyXX`) is classically AC-equivalent to AK(3) but has **6 height-15 exits** versus AK(3)'s **1** — a far better-connected seed. A solve from orbit-2 certifies AK(3) with **no stable caveat** (it is an unstable-AC-equivalent). This is the single most concretely recommended AK(3)-specific experiment and has never been run at 50k–1M nodes. (You may *prepare and validate* the pipeline locally at ≤1000 nodes; the production run is the user's, on Colab.)
3. **Exhaustive ceiling-18/19 component enumeration.** The closed AC component of AK(3) restricted to total length ≤ 17 is exactly 1000 states / 168 Aut-orbits, none below length 13 (`AC17_CERTIFICATE.json`, closure-verified). Push the ceiling to 18/19 (grows past 1243 states at 18): does a third Aut-orbit or a length-≤12 state appear? A closure proof here is real structure either way.
4. **Independent ATP oracle (Prover9).** Resolution ATP has no length-monotonicity constraint, so it explores differently from the greedy. Honest prior: on AK(3) it is **memory-bound before time-bound** (producer pruned, see [`PRUNED.md`](../PRUNED.md) — was `experiments/stable_ac/atp/`); an earlier "exhaustion" was a false artifact of weight-pruning silently dropping clauses, so treat any ATP null as unreliable. Route AK(3) *directly* to trivial, never through the broken MMS02 P-chain.
5. **Aut-quotient meet-in-the-middle vs the trivial presentation.** `run_mitm_aut.py` (ship-ready). A pilot found no merges at ceiling 26–30, but that is a *time-bounded* lower bound, not a disjointness proof — every TRIVIAL-connecting merge must be dual-stack verified before any "solve" language.
