# The flat depth-four obstruction is exhausted

## 1. The remaining dependent equation

Put

\[
A=x^3y^{-4},\qquad B=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1},
\]

and let

\[
T=\operatorname{Chr}(-4,7)=yyXyyXyyXyX.
\]

The signature \((8,3,5,-3,5)\) has a legal depth-four representative

\[
\begin{aligned}
R&=A h_0B^{-1}h_0^{-1},\\
S&=B h_1R^{-1}h_1^{-1},\\
U&=R h_2S^{-1}h_2^{-1},\\
Z&=U^{-1}h_3Sh_3^{-1},
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]

where the four conjugators are arbitrary words in \(F(x,y)\).
Abelianization gives

\[
[Z]=-3[A]+5[B]=(-4,7).
\]

The unresolved question is whether some choices in (1) make \(Z\)
conjugate to \(T\).

## 2. Exact planar fatgraph

Relax (1) by forgetting that repeated leaves descend from the same
intermediate rows. The relaxed boundary chain is

\[
T+3A+5B^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]

Use the nine cyclic boundary words

    T, A, A, A, B^-1, B^-1, B^-1, B^-1, B^-1

with

    T    = yyXyyXyyXyX
    A    = xxxYYYY
    B^-1 = yxyXYX.

Their concatenation has 62 letter positions. Pair the positions by

    (0,31) (1,30) (2,45) (3,23) (4,22) (5,57) (6,15)
    (7,14) (8,13) (9,42) (10,25) (11,37) (12,43) (16,56)
    (17,32) (18,49) (19,55) (20,59) (21,58) (24,44)
    (26,41) (27,53) (28,52) (29,46) (33,61) (34,60)
    (35,39) (36,38) (40,54) (47,51) (48,50).

Every pair contains inverse letters. Let \(\sigma\) advance along each
oriented boundary component and let \(\tau\) exchange paired positions.
The vertex permutation \(\sigma\tau\) has 24 cycles. Hence

\[
E=31,\qquad V=24,\qquad \chi=V-E=-7.
\]

The action generated by \(\sigma,\tau\) is transitive, so the fatgraph
is connected. It has nine boundary components, and

\[
2-2g-9=-7
\]

gives \(g=0\). Thus (2) bounds an exact connected planar surface over
the rank-two rose.

## 3. No-go theorem for flat invariants

The planar surface relation implies in \(F(x,y)\) that \(T\) is a
product, in some order, of three arbitrary conjugates of \(A^{-1}\) and
five arbitrary conjugates of \(B\). Consequently:

1. every quotient or representation which tests only membership in that
   flat product of eight conjugacy classes must answer yes;
2. every conjugacy-invariant bi-invariant norm satisfies

   \[
   \|T\|\le3\|A\|+5\|B\|;
   \]

3. finite class products, multiplicative Horn inequalities,
   Bass--Serre class products, and compact-group metric tests cannot
   obstruct this signature once they forget the shared intermediate
   rows.

This theorem explains the exact failure of the full-two-relator metric
search. It is stronger than a negative bounded computation: no target
group and no choice of representation can make a flat class-product
invariant succeed.

## 4. Target-adapted basis

The target supplies the next structure. Put

\[
c=yyX,\qquad d=yX.
\]

Then

\[
T=c^3d.
\]

The pair \(c,d\) is a free basis: \(c=yd\), while
\(d=yx^{-1}\), so

\[
y=cd^{-1},\qquad x=d^{-1}cd^{-1}.
\]

Replacing \(d\) by \(t=c^3d=T\) is a Nielsen move. Therefore
\((t,c)\) is also a free basis, with

\[
y=ct^{-1}c^3,\qquad
x=t^{-1}c^4t^{-1}c^3.
\tag{3}
\]

In the free-product splitting

\[
F(x,y)=\langle t\rangle*\langle c\rangle,
\]

the primitive target is the single syllable \(t\). Direct substitution
in (3) gives 12 alternating free-product syllables for \(A\) and 10 for
\(B\). Any solution of (1) must therefore cancel a decorated hierarchy
built from long hyperbolic words to a single target syllable.

The ordinary planar fatgraph cannot record this constraint. A closing
theorem must either produce explicit \(h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3\), or prove a
cancellation invariant for (1) which remembers that the same \(R\) and
\(S\) recur at several levels.

## 5. Scope

This is a no-go theorem about proof architecture, not a solution of
(1). The source depth-four problem and the Andrews--Curtis conjecture
remain open.
