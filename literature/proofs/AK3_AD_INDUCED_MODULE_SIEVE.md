# Induced-module sieve for arbitrary A--D relative products

Date: 2026-07-26

Status: **PROVEN** for every relative conjugator outside three exact
\(F(q,z)\)-projection fibers.

Put

\[
\begin{aligned}
A&=qx^3q^{-1}t^{-4},\\
D&=t^{-1}zxz^{-1},\\
P_\sigma(c)&=A\,cD^\sigma c^{-1},
\qquad \sigma=\pm1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.1}
\]

Let

\[
\pi:F(x,t,z,q)\longrightarrow F(q,z),
\qquad
\pi(x)=\pi(t)=1,
\tag{0.2}
\]

and set

\[
h=qz^{-1}.
\tag{0.3}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
P_\sigma(c)\text{ primitive}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
\pi(c)=h,&\sigma=+1,\\
\pi(c)\in\{1,h\},&\sigma=-1.
\end{cases}
}
\tag{0.4}
\]

There is no bound on the length, alphabet, cancellation pattern, or
commutator content of c. The three displayed fibers are not claimed to
contain primitive words.

## 1. The noncommutative Fox row

Let

\[
G=F(q,z),
\qquad
R=\mathbb Z[G].
\tag{1.1}
\]

Apply \(\pi\) to the coefficients of the left Fox derivatives. Since
\(\pi(A)=\pi(D)=1\), direct differentiation gives, in the coordinate
order \((x,t,z,q)\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_*(\nabla A)&=(3q,-4,0,0),\\
\pi_*(\nabla D)&=(z,-1,0,0).
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.2}
\]

Write \(g=\pi(c)\). In the product rule for \(P_\sigma(c)\), the two
derivatives of c cancel:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\pi_*(\nabla P_\sigma(c))
&=
\pi_*(\nabla A)
+\pi_*(\nabla c)
+g\,\pi_*(\nabla D^\sigma)
+g\,\pi(D^\sigma)\pi_*(\nabla c^{-1})\\
&=
\pi_*(\nabla A)+\sigma g\,\pi_*(\nabla D)\\
&=
\boxed{(3q+\sigma gz,-4-\sigma g,0,0)}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.3}
\]

The second line uses

\[
\pi_*(\nabla D^\sigma)=\sigma\pi_*(\nabla D),
\qquad
\pi_*(\nabla c^{-1})=-g^{-1}\pi_*(\nabla c).
\tag{1.4}
\]

If \(P_\sigma(c)\) is primitive, its Fox row is right-unimodular over
the integral group ring. Applying \(\pi_*\) preserves a right Bézout
identity, so the row (1.3) is right-unimodular over R.

## 2. A right-module obstruction

### Lemma 2.1

Let \(g\in G\). If \(g\notin\langle h\rangle\), the row

\[
(3q+\sigma gz,-4-\sigma g,0,0)
\tag{2.1}
\]

is not right-unimodular over R.

#### Proof

Work over \(k=\mathbb Q\), and put

\[
L=\langle h,g\rangle\le G.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The word \(h=qz^{-1}\) is primitive and hence is not a proper power.
If \(g\notin\langle h\rangle\), then L is noncyclic. It is generated by
two elements, so it is a free group of rank two. Moreover \((h,g)\) is
a free basis of L: the natural epimorphism from a rank-two free group
onto L becomes a surjective endomorphism after choosing an abstract
rank-two basis of L, and finitely generated free groups are Hopfian.

Define a one-dimensional right \(k[L]\)-module \(k_\chi\) by the
character

\[
\chi(h)=\frac43,
\qquad
\chi(g)=-4\sigma.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Induce it to a right \(k[G]\)-module:

\[
M=k_\chi\otimes_{k[L]}k[G].
\tag{2.4}
\]

The group algebra \(k[G]\) is free as a left \(k[L]\)-module on a set
of right-coset representatives, so M is nonzero. Let

\[
v=1\otimes1\ne0.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The tensor relation gives

\[
vh=\frac43v,
\qquad
vg=-4\sigma v.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Since \(h=qz^{-1}\), right-multiplying the first equality by z gives

\[
vq=\frac43vz.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
v(-4-\sigma g)
&=-4v-\sigma(-4\sigma v)=0,\\
v(3q+\sigma gz)
&=4vz+\sigma(-4\sigma v)z=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.8}
\]

If (2.1) had a right Bézout identity
\(\sum_i r_is_i=1\), then

\[
v=v\sum_i r_is_i=\sum_i(vr_i)s_i=0,
\tag{2.9}
\]

contradicting (2.5). Thus the row is not right-unimodular. \(\square\)

Combining Lemma 2.1 with Section 1 proves

\[
P_\sigma(c)\text{ primitive}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\pi(c)\in\langle qz^{-1}\rangle.
\tag{2.10}
\]

This is the step missed by the abelian character in Result 55:
noncommuting q,z projections with the same exponent sums are now
excluded uniformly.

## 3. The residual cyclic subgroup

Because h is not a proper power, (2.10) has a unique spelling

\[
\pi(c)=h^m,
\qquad m\in\mathbb Z.
\tag{3.1}
\]

On a one-dimensional module with

\[
vh=(4/3)v,
\tag{3.2}
\]

the second required eigenvalue in (2.6) is possible exactly when

\[
(4/3)^m=-4\sigma.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Over \(\mathbb Q\), (3.3) never holds. To obstruct a cyclic case it is
enough to make it hold after passing to a nonzero finite commutative
quotient in which 2 and 3 are units. Result 55's all-integer argument
does exactly this for every m except

\[
(\sigma,m)=(+1,1),\quad(-1,0),\quad(-1,1).
\tag{3.4}
\]

Explicitly, after clearing denominators use

\[
\begin{cases}
4^m+4\sigma3^m,&m\ge0,\\
3^{-m}+4\sigma4^{-m},&m<0.
\end{cases}
\tag{3.5}
\]

Outside (3.4), removing all factors 2 and 3 leaves a modulus greater
than one. In that quotient, (3.2)--(3.3) give a nonzero
one-dimensional module vector annihilating (1.3). The three residual
cleared integers are \(16,-3,-8\); because a putative annihilating
module makes 2 and 3 units, none can vanish in a nonzero target ring.

Equations (2.10) and (3.4) prove (0.4).

## 4. Orientations, exact coordinate, and boundary

As in Result 55, conjugating, cyclically reordering, and possibly
inverting the whole changed row puts either A--D target direction and
both carrier orientations into (0.1). The projection in (0.4) is
always taken after that normalization. A D-target product replaces the
written relative conjugator by its inverse, and normalizing a negative
A-orientation flips \(\sigma\).

There is also an exact free-factor coordinate behind the sieve:

\[
\phi:
\quad
x\mapsto z^{-1}xz,\quad
t\mapsto t,\quad
z\mapsto z,\quad
q\mapsto qz.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Its inverse sends \(x\mapsto zxz^{-1}\) and \(q\mapsto qz^{-1}\).
Literal substitution gives

\[
\phi(A)=A,
\qquad
\phi(D)=t^{-1}x.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Thus the A--D pair lies in the rank-three free factor
\(F(x,t,q)\) in this coordinate. On abelianization,

\[
e_z(\phi(c))=e_q(c)+e_z(c),
\qquad
e_q(\phi(c))=e_q(c),
\tag{4.3}
\]

which explains Result 55's exponent hyperplane. Result 56 is stronger:
it retains the full noncommutative q,z projection instead of only
(4.3).

The remaining fibers are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\sigma&\pi(c)\\ \hline
+1&qz^{-1}\\
-1&1,\ qz^{-1}.
\end{array}
\tag{4.4}
\]

They still contain arbitrary x,t kernel insertions and are not
classified here. Arbitrary A--W and W--D products, AK(3), AC, and
stable AC remain open.
