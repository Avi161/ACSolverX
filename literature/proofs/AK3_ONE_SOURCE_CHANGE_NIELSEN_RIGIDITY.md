# One-source-change Nielsen-rigidity reduction

Date: 2026-07-27

Status: **PROVEN REDUCTION**.  The general conditional bridge theorem and
the arbitrary-conjugator nonprimitivity theorem for one changed AK source
are proved.  Admissible Nielsen rigidity for the changed source is the
remaining hypothesis, not a proved fact.

Let

\[
F_0=F(x,y),
\qquad
F=F_0*\langle r\rangle.
\tag{0.1}
\]

For \(V\in F_0\), write

\[
G(V)=F_0/\langle\!\langle V\rangle\!\rangle.
\tag{0.2}
\]

This note abstracts the positive-bridge proof for the original AK sources
and then applies the abstraction after one old-factor row multiplication.

## 1. The exact rigidity property forced by a primitive bridge

### Definition 1.1

An injective endomorphism

\[
\phi:F_0\hookrightarrow F_0
\tag{1.1}
\]

is \(V\)-admissible if:

1. \(\phi_{\mathrm{ab}}\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\); and
2. the natural map induced by \(\phi\),

   \[
   \bar\phi:
   G(V)\longrightarrow
   F_0/\langle\!\langle\phi(V)\rangle\!\rangle,
   \tag{1.2}
   \]

   is an isomorphism.

The word \(V\) has **admissible one-relator Nielsen rigidity** if every
\(V\)-admissible \(\phi\) satisfies

\[
\alpha(\phi(V))=hV^\delta h^{-1}
\tag{1.3}
\]

for some \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(F_0)\), \(h\in F_0\), and
\(\delta=\pm1\).

This is weaker than uniqueness of the Nielsen class among all
two-generator one-relator presentations of \(G(V)\).  It asks only about
relators reached by the unimodular injective self-maps which the bridge
quotient itself forces.

## 2. General positive-bridge theorem

### Theorem 2.1

Suppose \(V\in F_0\) is non-simple and has admissible one-relator Nielsen
rigidity.  If

\[
W=r\,cV^\epsilon c^{-1},
\qquad
\epsilon=\pm1,
\qquad
c\notin\langle r\rangle F_0,
\tag{2.1}
\]

then \(W\) is nonprimitive in \(F\).

### Proof

Assume that \(W\) is primitive and let

\[
q:F\longrightarrow
Q=F/\langle\!\langle W\rangle\!\rangle\cong F_2.
\tag{2.2}
\]

A cyclically reduced conjugate of \(W\) contains \(r\), because its
\(r\)-exponent sum is one.  Freiheitssatz therefore embeds the old factor:

\[
K=q(F_0)\cong F_0.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Put \(v=q(V)\) and \(N=\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle_Q\).  The
coefficient of \(r\) in the abelianization of \(W\) is one, so inclusion
induces

\[
H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(Q;\mathbb Z).
\tag{2.4}
\]

The relation \(W=1\) expresses \(q(r)\) as a conjugate of
\(v^{-\epsilon}\), hence \(Q=KN\).  Moreover,

\[
\langle\!\langle W,V\rangle\!\rangle_F
=
\langle\!\langle r,V\rangle\!\rangle_F.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
K\cap N=\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle_K
\tag{2.6}
\]

and inclusion induces an isomorphism

\[
K/\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle_K
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
Q/\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle_Q.
\tag{2.7}
\]

Identify both \(K\) and \(Q\) with \(F_0\).  The inclusion becomes a
\(V\)-admissible injection \(\phi:F_0\hookrightarrow F_0\).  By (1.3),
postcomposition with an automorphism and an inner automorphism gives an
injective endomorphism \(\eta\) satisfying

\[
\eta(V)=V^\delta,
\qquad \delta=\pm1.
\tag{2.8}
\]

Turner's monomorphism-test theorem applies because \(V\) is non-simple.
If \(\delta=1\), it makes \(\eta\) an automorphism.  If \(\delta=-1\),
then

\[
\eta^2(V)=\eta(V^{-1})=\eta(V)^{-1}=V.
\tag{2.9}
\]

Turner makes \(\eta^2\) an automorphism, which also makes \(\eta\)
surjective.  Thus \(\phi\) is onto and

\[
K=Q.
\tag{2.10}
\]

Now identify \(Q\) with \(F_0\) through \(q|_{F_0}\).  The quotient map
fixes \(F_0\) and sends \(r\) to some \(u\in F_0\), so its kernel is
normally generated by \(ru^{-1}\).  Magnus's normal-closure theorem makes
\(W\) conjugate to \((ru^{-1})^{\pm1}\), whose cyclic free-product
syllable length is at most two.

On the other hand, a shortest representative of the positive double coset
\(\langle r\rangle cF_0\) writes \(c=bda\), with
\(b\in\langle r\rangle\), \(a\in F_0\), and \(d\) beginning in an
\(F_0\)-syllable and ending in a nontrivial \(\langle r\rangle\)-syllable.
If \(d\) has syllable length \(2\ell\), then \(\ell\ge1\), and \(W\) is
conjugate to the cyclically reduced word

\[
r\,d(aV^\epsilon a^{-1})d^{-1}
\tag{2.11}
\]

of syllable length \(4\ell+2\ge6\).  This contradicts the previous
paragraph and proves the theorem. \(\square\)

### Remark 2.2

The non-simple hypothesis cannot be removed.  If \(V\) is primitive,
choose \(U\) so that \((V^\epsilon,U)\) is a basis of \(F_0\) and put

\[
c=Ur^{-1}V^{-\epsilon}.
\tag{2.12}
\]

Then \(c\notin\langle r\rangle F_0\), while

\[
r\,cV^\epsilon c^{-1}
=
rUr^{-1}V^\epsilon rU^{-1}.
\tag{2.13}
\]

The letter \(V^\epsilon\) occurs exactly once in the basis
\((V^\epsilon,U,r)\), so (2.13) is primitive.  Non-simple source rigidity
is therefore doing real work.

## 3. One changed AK source

Let

\[
A=x^3y^{-4},
\qquad
B=xyxy^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

After one old-factor row multiplication, every changed source is, up to
conjugacy and inversion, of one of the forms

\[
V^A_{g,\eta}=A\,gB^\eta g^{-1},
\qquad
V^B_{g,\eta}=B\,gA^\eta g^{-1},
\qquad
g\in F_0,
\quad \eta=\pm1.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Their exponent vectors are

\[
[V^A_{g,+}]=[V^B_{g,+}]=(4,-5),
\tag{3.3}
\]

and

\[
[V^A_{g,-}]=(2,-3),
\qquad
[V^B_{g,-}]=(-2,3).
\tag{3.4}
\]

All four vectors are primitive.  The changed row and the unchanged row
still have determinant one in abelianization, and the row multiplication
preserves the normal closure of the pair.

## 4. No arbitrary old-factor conjugator makes the changed source primitive

We use two standard rank-two facts.

First, for nontrivial cyclically reduced \(P,Q\in F_0\), axis alignment in
the Cayley tree gives

\[
\min_{g\in F_0}\|P gQg^{-1}\|
=
\min_{\substack{P'\text{ a cyclic rotation of }P\\
                 Q'\text{ a cyclic rotation of }Q}}
\|\overline{P'Q'}\|,
\tag{4.1}
\]

where double bars denote cyclic length.  Disjoint axes add twice their
positive bridge distance and cannot minimize; intersecting axes can be
based at a common vertex, producing the rotations on the right.

Second, the Osborne--Zieschang classification says that a cyclically
reduced primitive word with exponent vector \((p,q)\) has cyclic length

\[
|p|+|q|.
\tag{4.2}
\]

For cyclically reduced \(P,Q\), neither of which cancels completely, let
\(L(P,Q^{-1})\) be the maximum length of a common cyclic subword of
\(P\) and \(Q^{-1}\).  Cancellations at the two cyclic seams concatenate
across the chosen cut to one such common factor, and cutting at the ends of
a longest factor realizes it.  Hence

\[
\min_{P',Q'}\|\overline{P'Q'}\|
=
|P|+|Q|-2L(P,Q^{-1}),
\tag{4.3}
\]

where the minimum is over cyclic rotations.

The complete rotation audit for (3.1) has only \(7\cdot6=42\) pairs in
each sign row and gives

| target conjugacy class | exponent vector | minimum cyclic length | primitive length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| \(A gBg^{-1}\) | \((4,-5)\) | \(11\) | \(9\) |
| \(A gB^{-1}g^{-1}\) | \((2,-3)\) | \(9\) | \(5\) |
| \(B gAg^{-1}\) | \((4,-5)\) | \(11\) | \(9\) |
| \(B gA^{-1}g^{-1}\) | \((-2,3)\) | \(9\) | \(5\) |

For a compact hand proof of the two minima, the cyclic length-two subwords
of \(A\) are

\[
xx,\ xY,\ YY,\ Yx.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Those of \(B^{-1}=yxyXYX\) are

\[
yx,\ xy,\ yX,\ XY,\ YX,\ Xy,
\tag{4.5}
\]

The two sets are disjoint but the words share signed letters, so
\(L(A,B^{-1})=1\).  Formula (4.3) gives \(13-2=11\) in the equal-sign
row.  For the opposite-sign row, \(A\) and \(B\) share the length-two
factors \(xY\) and \(Yx\).  Their cyclic length-three subword sets are

\[
\{xxx,xxY,xYY,YYY,YYx,Yxx\}
\tag{4.6}
\]

and

\[
\{xyx,yxY,xYX,YXY,XYx,Yxy\},
\tag{4.7}
\]

which are disjoint.  Thus \(L(A,B)=2\), and (4.3) gives
\(13-4=9\).  Reversing target order does not change cyclic length because
\(PQ\) and \(QP\) are conjugate.

Equations (4.1)--(4.2) and the strict gaps in the table prove:

### Theorem 4.1

For every \(g\in F_0\) and \(\eta=\pm1\), neither word in (3.2) is
primitive.

Because the vectors (3.3)--(3.4) are primitive, none of these words is a
proper power.  A simple element of \(F_2\) lies in a cyclic free factor, so
it is a power of a primitive element.  The exponent vector forces that
power to be \(\pm1\).  Theorem 4.1 therefore also proves:

\[
\boxed{
V^A_{g,\eta}\text{ and }V^B_{g,\eta}
\text{ are non-simple for every }g,\eta.
}
\tag{4.8}
\]

## 5. Exact two-edge obstruction

Start at the normalized stabilized checkpoint \((A,B,r)\).  Make one
row multiplication inside \(F_0\), producing a changed source \(V\) from
(3.2), and then target the r-slot:

\[
r\longmapsto r\,cV^\epsilon c^{-1}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

If \(c\in\langle r\rangle F_0\), the unique-r deletion is the zero-bridge
stable self-loop.  If the bridge is positive, (4.5) and Theorem 2.1 show
that a primitive target in (5.1) forces failure of admissible one-relator
Nielsen rigidity for \(V\).  More explicitly, its primitive quotient must
produce a proper injection

\[
\phi:F_2\hookrightarrow F_2,
\qquad
\phi_{\mathrm{ab}}\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z),
\tag{5.2}
\]

such that the natural map

\[
G(V)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
F_2/\langle\!\langle\phi(V)\rangle\!\rangle
\tag{5.3}
\]

is an isomorphism, but

\[
\phi(V)
\notin
\operatorname{Aut}(F_2)\cdot
\{\text{conjugates of }V^{\pm1}\}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Thus an abstract second presentation of \(G(V)\) is not enough.  The
second Nielsen class must be attained by a proper unimodular self-embedding
whose induced quotient map is the isomorphism.

This closes the primitive-source alternative for every z-free first row
change and isolates (5.2)--(5.4) as its only positive-bridge algebraic
escape.  It does not yet prove admissible rigidity for the family (3.2),
and it does not cover an r-dependent first row change or two successive
source multiplications directly into the r-slot.  AK(3), stable
Andrews--Curtis, and Andrews--Curtis remain open.
