# AK(3) A--D relative-free \(BS(3,4)\) module obstruction

## Statement

Let

\[
B=BS(3,4)=\langle x,y\mid yx^3y^{-1}=x^4\rangle,
\qquad
G=B*\langle z\rangle,
\]

and put

\[
R_3=1+x+x^2,\qquad R_4=1+x+x^2+x^3.
\]

Fix \(\sigma\in\{+1,-1\}\) and \(g\in G\), and set

\[
u=z^{-1}gz.
\]

The group-theoretic input is exact: the natural homomorphism

\[
B*\langle s\rangle\longrightarrow G,
\qquad
b\longmapsto b,\quad s\longmapsto u,
\tag{1}
\]

is injective if and only if \(u\notin B\), equivalently if and only if
\(g\notin zBz^{-1}\). Under these equivalent conditions, there is a
right \(\mathbb Q[G]\)-module containing a nonzero vector \(w\) such
that

\[
w(x^4-1)=0,\qquad
w(yR_3-R_4)=0,\qquad
w(u+\sigma R_4)=0.
\tag{2}
\]

Equivalently, with

\[
t=zxz^{-1},\qquad q=zy,\qquad v=wz^{-1},
\tag{3}
\]

the nonzero vector \(v\) satisfies the original three four-state
relations

\[
v(t^4-1)=0,\qquad
v(g+\sigma(1+t+t^2+t^3))=0,
\tag{4}
\]

\[
v\bigl(q(1+x+x^2)-(1+t+t^2+t^3)z\bigr)=0.
\tag{5}
\]

Thus the corresponding evaluated Fox row is not right-unimodular, and
the relative product cannot be primitive.

The cases \(g\in zBz^{-1}\), equivalently \(u\in B\), are not covered.

## 1. The affine \(BS(3,4)\)-action

Let \(B\) act on \(\mathbb Q\) on the right by

\[
r x=r+1,\qquad r y=\frac34r.
\tag{6}
\]

The inverse action of \(y\) is multiplication by \(4/3\). Therefore

\[
r(yx^3y^{-1})
=\left(\frac34r+3\right)\frac43
=r+4
=rx^4.
\tag{7}
\]

Hence (6) respects the defining relation of \(B\).

Let \(V\) be the \(\mathbb Q\)-vector space of all functions
\(\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q\). Give \(V\) the pushforward permutation
action

\[
(f b)(r)=f(rb^{-1}).
\tag{8}
\]

Thus the support of \(fb\) is the right translate
\(\operatorname{supp}(f)b\).

Let \(w=\mathbf 1_{4\mathbb Z}\). The four translates

\[
4\mathbb Z,\quad4\mathbb Z+1,\quad
4\mathbb Z+2,\quad4\mathbb Z+3
\tag{9}
\]

are disjoint and partition \(\mathbb Z\). Consequently

\[
wx^4=w,\qquad
wR_4=\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z}.
\tag{10}
\]

Also \((4\mathbb Z)y=3\mathbb Z\), and the three translates

\[
3\mathbb Z,\quad3\mathbb Z+1,\quad3\mathbb Z+2
\tag{11}
\]

are disjoint and partition \(\mathbb Z\). Hence

\[
wyR_3=\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Z}=wR_4.
\tag{12}
\]

Both \(w\) and \(W:=wR_4\) are nonzero. They are linearly independent:
if \(aw+bW=0\), evaluation at \(1\) gives \(b=0\), and evaluation at
\(0\) then gives \(a=0\).

## 2. Prescribing the action of \(u\)

On \(E=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{w,W\}\), define

\[
U(w)=-\sigma W,\qquad U(W)=w.
\tag{13}
\]

In the ordered basis \((w,W)\), this is

\[
[U]_E=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
-\sigma&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{14}
\]

Its determinant is \(\sigma\), so it is invertible. Choose a vector
space complement \(V=E\oplus C\), and define \(U\) to be the identity
on \(C\). This gives an explicit linear automorphism of \(V\) with

\[
U(w)+\sigma wR_4=-\sigma W+\sigma W=0.
\tag{15}
\]

The action from Section 1 is a right action of \(B\), and \(U\) is
invertible. The free-product universal property therefore defines a
right action of \(B*\langle s\rangle\) on \(V\), with \(s\) acting as
\(U\). Under the injectivity condition (1), its image is

\[
H=\langle B,u\rangle\le G,
\]

and this action is a well-defined right \(\mathbb Q[H]\)-module
structure in which \(u\) acts as \(U\). Equations (10), (12), and
(15) prove all three relations in (2).

Section 5 proves that (1) is injective exactly when \(u\notin B\).

## 3. Induction to \(G\) preserves the vector

Induce the right \(H\)-module to \(G\):

\[
M=V\otimes_{\mathbb Q[H]}\mathbb Q[G].
\tag{16}
\]

Choose representatives \(T\) for the right cosets \(H\backslash G\),
including the identity representative. Then

\[
\mathbb Q[G]=\bigoplus_{\tau\in T}\mathbb Q[H]\tau
\tag{17}
\]

as a left \(\mathbb Q[H]\)-module. Tensoring (17) with \(V\) shows
that \(M\) is a direct sum of copies of \(V\). In particular, the map

\[
V\longrightarrow M,\qquad a\longmapsto a\otimes1
\tag{18}
\]

is injective. We retain the notation \(w\) for its nonzero image.
The three relations (2) remain valid in \(M\).

## 4. Right-handed return to the four-state relations

Put \(v=wz^{-1}\). Right multiplication by \(z^{-1}\) is invertible,
so \(v\ne0\). Using \(t=zxz^{-1}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
v(t^4-1)
&=wz^{-1}(zx^4z^{-1}-1)\\
&=w(x^4-1)z^{-1}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{19}
\]

Since \(g=zuz^{-1}\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
v\bigl(g+\sigma(1+t+t^2+t^3)\bigr)
&=w\bigl(u+\sigma R_4\bigr)z^{-1}=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{20}
\]

Finally \(q=zy\), and
\(z^{-1}(1+t+t^2+t^3)z=R_4\). Thus

\[
\begin{aligned}
v\bigl(qR_3-(1+t+t^2+t^3)z\bigr)
&=wyR_3-wR_4=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
\]

These are right-module computations: every coefficient stays on the
right of the vector.

In particular, the exact right ideal generated by the three elements
in (4)--(5) is proper: a right Bézout identity would give \(v=0\)
after acting on it.

For completeness, the literal evaluated Fox row from the universal
A--D quotient is

\[
\left(
qR_3+\sigma gt^{-1}z,\;
-R_4(t)-\sigma gt^{-1},\;
\sigma g(t^{-1}-1),\;
1-t^4
\right).
\tag{22}
\]

Equation (19) gives \(vt^{-1}=vt^3\), and hence
\(vR_4(t)t^{-1}=vR_4(t)\). Using (20)--(21), the actions of the four
coordinates in (22) are respectively

\[
\begin{aligned}
vqR_3-vR_4(t)t^{-1}z&=vR_4(t)z-vR_4(t)z=0,\\
-vR_4(t)+vR_4(t)t^{-1}&=0,\\
-vR_4(t)(t^{-1}-1)&=0,\\
v(1-t^4)&=0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{23}
\]

Thus the evaluated Fox row is not right-unimodular over
\(\mathbb Q[G]\). A right Bézout identity over \(\mathbb Z[G]\) would
persist after scalar extension to \(\mathbb Q[G]\), so none exists
there either. The usual necessary Fox condition for a primitive word
now proves the claimed nonprimitivity.

## 5. Torsion-freeness and the exact relative-free range

View

\[
B=\langle x,y\mid yx^3y^{-1}=x^4\rangle
\]

as the HNN extension of the infinite cyclic base
\(\langle x\rangle\), with associated subgroups
\(\langle x^3\rangle\) and \(\langle x^4\rangle\). Its Bass--Serre
action is without inversions. If \(b\in B\) has finite order, the
finite cyclic group \(\langle b\rangle\) fixes a vertex of the
Bass--Serre tree. Hence \(b\) lies in a conjugate of the vertex
stabilizer \(\langle x\rangle\). Since \(\langle x\rangle\cong
\mathbb Z\) is torsion-free, \(b=1\). Thus \(B\) is torsion-free.

Apply the same argument to the Bass--Serre tree of the free product

\[
G=B*\langle z\rangle.
\]

Every finite cyclic subgroup fixes a vertex, and the vertex
stabilizers are conjugates of \(B\) and of
\(\langle z\rangle\). Both are torsion-free, so \(G\) is
torsion-free.

The relative rank-one free-product theorem in
`literature/proofs/RELATIVE_RANK_ONE_FREE_PRODUCT.md` now applies to
(1). In a torsion-free free product \(B*\langle z\rangle\), the map
which fixes \(B\) and sends \(s\) to \(u\) is injective exactly when
\(u\notin B\). Since \(u=z^{-1}gz\),

\[
u\notin B
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
g\notin zBz^{-1}.
\]

Therefore Sections 1--4 obstruct every such noninternal element. If
\(u\in B\), the nontrivial source word \(su^{-1}\) lies in the kernel
of (1); the module construction does not obstruct that case.

## 6. Exact unresolved residue

Let

\[
\kappa:G\longrightarrow F(y,z),
\qquad
\kappa(x)=1,\quad\kappa(y)=y,\quad\kappa(z)=z,
\]

and put \(K=\ker\kappa\) and \(h=zyz^{-1}=qz^{-1}\). Without choosing
bounded representatives, the three Result 56 fibers are

\[
\sigma=+1:\quad g=kh,
\qquad
\sigma=-1:\quad g=k\ \text{or}\ g=kh,
\qquad k\in K.
\tag{24}
\]

The proved class consists of every pair in these fibers with
\(g\notin zBz^{-1}\). The exact unresolved residue for this argument
consists of the internal elements

\[
\boxed{g\in zBz^{-1},\quad\text{equivalently }u\in B.}
\tag{25}
\]

For the internal \(B\)-cases inside the three Result 56 fibers, write

\[
g=zbz^{-1},\qquad b\in B,
\tag{26}
\]

so \(u=b\). Let \(e_y:B\to\mathbb Z\) be the exponent homomorphism
\(e_y(y)=1,\ e_y(x)=0\). Since

\[
h=qz^{-1}=zyz^{-1},
\tag{27}
\]

their translation is

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{Result 56 fiber}&\text{internal case}\\ \hline
\sigma=+1,\ h\text{-fiber}&g=zbz^{-1},\ e_y(b)=1\\
\sigma=-1,\ h\text{-fiber}&g=zbz^{-1},\ e_y(b)=1\\
\sigma=-1,\ 1\text{-fiber}&g=zbz^{-1},\ e_y(b)=0.
\end{array}
\tag{28}
\]

All cases in (28) have \(u=b\in B\), so they belong to the unresolved
residue (25). None is claimed obstructed here.

## 7. Independent replay

`experiments/stable_ac/verify_ad_bs34_module.py` and
`tests/stable_ac/test_ad_bs34_module.py` replay:

1. the affine identity \(yx^3y^{-1}=x^4\);
2. the exact disjoint residue partitions (9) and (11);
3. the automorphism (13)--(14);
4. the substitutions \(y=z^{-1}q\) and
   \(z^{-1}t^iz=x^i\) used in (19)--(21); and
5. the original literal Fox row and its four-state annihilation.

The replay is a check of these displayed identities. It is not used
to infer the relative-free condition for an arbitrary \(u\).

The separate files
`experiments/stable_ac/verify_relative_free_product.py` and
`tests/stable_ac/test_relative_free_product.py` replay the syllable
reductions used by the relative rank-one theorem, including its
\(C_2*C_2\) torsion counterexample. The universal theorem is the
normal-form induction in
`literature/proofs/RELATIVE_RANK_ONE_FREE_PRODUCT.md`, not a conclusion
from those finite fixtures.
