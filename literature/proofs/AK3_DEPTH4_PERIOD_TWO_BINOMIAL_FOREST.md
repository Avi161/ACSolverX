# The three binomial lift operators form a forest

## Status

For the exact period-two lifting equation
\[
d+L_0x_0+L_1x_1+L_2x_2+L_3x_3+L_4x_4=0
\]
in
\[
M=\mathbb Z[Q/\langle c\rangle],
\qquad
Q=C_2*\mathbb Z,
\]
the last three operators are binomials:
\[
L_2=1-X,\qquad
L_3=U^{-1}-t,\qquad
L_4=t-1.
\tag{1}
\]

This note proves that their combined map is injective on finitely supported
vectors:
\[
L_2y_2+L_3y_3+L_4y_4=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
y_2=y_3=y_4=0.
\tag{2}
\]

Consequently every nonzero finitely supported homogeneous lifting syzygy
must involve at least one of the two multi-term source operators
\(L_0,L_1\). This is a structural reduction, not a solution or an
impossibility proof for the full lifting equation.

## 1. Exact quotient coordinates

Write
\[
Q=H\rtimes\langle c\rangle,\qquad
H=F(p,q),
\]
where
\[
p=t,\qquad q=ctc,
\]
and conjugation by \(c\) induces
\[
\alpha(p)=q,\qquad\alpha(q)=p.
\]
Use upper case for inverse letters.

The exact quotient witness has
\[
X=(qPQp)c,\qquad
U=(PPqqP)c.
\tag{3}
\]
Put
\[
G=U^{-1}t^{-1}.
\tag{4}
\]
Semidirect multiplication gives
\[
G=(qPPq)c.
\tag{5}
\]

The three binomial columns have a graph interpretation on the
\(C\)-vertices \(Q/\langle c\rangle\):

\[
L_2e_v=e_v-e_{Xv},
\tag{6}
\]
\[
L_4e_v=e_{tv}-e_v,
\tag{7}
\]
and
\[
L_3e_v=e_{U^{-1}v}-e_{tv}.
\tag{8}
\]
In (8), put \(w=tv\). Its endpoints are \(w\) and
\[
U^{-1}t^{-1}w=Gw.
\]
Thus the unoriented incidence graph of (6)--(8) is the orbit graph for
the left action of
\[
\mathcal K=\langle t,X,G\rangle\le Q
\tag{9}
\]
with the displayed ordered generating set.

## 2. The subgroup is freely generated

Let
\[
\mathbb F_3=\langle a,b,d\rangle
\]
map to \(Q\) by
\[
a\longmapsto t,\qquad
b\longmapsto X,\qquad
d\longmapsto G.
\tag{10}
\]
The parity map sends \(a\) to \(0\) and \(b,d\) to \(1\). Its kernel in
\(\mathbb F_3\) has the Reidemeister--Schreier basis
\[
a,\quad db^{-1},\quad bab^{-1},\quad b^2,\quad bd.
\tag{11}
\]

Using (3)--(5), their images in \(H=F(p,q)\) reduce exactly to
\[
\begin{aligned}
w_1&=p,\\
w_2&=qPPqPqpQ,\\
w_3&=qPQpqPqpQ,\\
w_4&=qPQppQPq,\\
w_5&=qPQppQQp.
\end{aligned}
\tag{12}
\]

Fold the bouquet of the five loops (12) in the free group \(F(p,q)\).
The deterministic Stallings core has
\[
V=4,\qquad E=8
\tag{13}
\]
vertices and unoriented edges. Hence its rank is
\[
E-V+1=5.
\tag{14}
\]

The subgroup generated by (12) is therefore a free group of rank five.
The restriction of (10) maps the free rank-five group with basis (11)
onto that subgroup. A surjection between free groups of the same finite
rank is an isomorphism, so the restriction is injective.

If a word in \(\mathbb F_3\) maps to the identity in \(Q\), it has even
parity and lies in the kernel generated by (11). Injectivity there forces
the original word to be trivial. Therefore (10) itself is injective:
\[
\mathcal K\cong F(t,X,G)
\]
is freely generated of rank three. In particular \(\mathcal K\) is
torsion-free.

## 3. The incidence graph is a forest

The stabilizer in \(Q\) of any \(C\)-vertex is a conjugate of
\(\langle c\rangle\), hence has order two. Since \(\mathcal K\) is
torsion-free,
\[
\mathcal K\cap q\langle c\rangle q^{-1}=1
\qquad(q\in Q).
\tag{15}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal K\) acts freely on every one of its orbits in
\(Q/\langle c\rangle\).

Each orbit graph for the free basis \((t,X,G)\) is consequently a copy of
the Cayley graph of \(F_3\), hence a tree. The complete incidence graph of
(6)--(8) is a disjoint union of these trees.

The boundary of a nonzero finitely supported edge chain in a forest cannot
vanish: the finite supporting subforest has a leaf, and the coefficient on
its unique incident supported edge survives in the boundary. Applied to
(6)--(8), this proves (2).

## 4. Consequence for remote lifting

The degree-two support-escape theorem shows that a successful lift must add
a nonlocal first-layer homogeneous solution. Equation (2) now excludes a
purely binomial remote cycle. Every such nonlocal syzygy must satisfy
\[
(y_0,y_1)\ne(0,0).
\]

Thus remote cancellation is forced to use one of the two source-dependent
multi-term operators:
\[
\begin{aligned}
L_0&=-U^{-1}(A-R)
     -(h_2+U^{-1}h_3)S(A-R),\\
L_1&=(h_2+U^{-1}h_3)(B-S).
\end{aligned}
\]
The surviving mechanism is no longer an arbitrary cycle in the
Bass--Serre graph. It must couple the two fixed source rows to the
binomial forest.
