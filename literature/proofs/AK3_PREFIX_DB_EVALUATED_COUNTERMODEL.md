# The evaluated prefix-DB equations do not force the braid class

Date: 2026-07-25

Status: **PROVEN**. The equations obtained after evaluating a
prefix-\(DB\) three-cross history at its final tail admit a weight-one
killer which is not conjugate to the AK(3) braid endpoint. Consequently
weight, normal generation, and the evaluated \(C_3*C_4\) equations cannot
close the remaining corridor. A quotient-\(B\) Bass--Serre sieve proves
that this explicit solution cannot lift to a legal one-\(z\) target.
General literal one-\(z\) liftability in \(G*\langle z\rangle\) remains
open.

## 1. The necessary evaluated system

Put

\[
R=x^3t^{-4},
\qquad
G=\langle x,t\mid R\rangle,
\qquad
p=xt.
\tag{1.1}
\]

Use the \(DBD\) representative of the final-target-switch pair. Let
\(B^\epsilon\) first target \(D\), producing \(D_1\); let
\(D_1^\eta\) target \(B\), producing \(B_1\); and let \(B_1^\theta\)
target \(D_1\), producing the final target.

Suppose a final orientation \(\delta\in\{1,-1\}\) and conjugation normalize
the third target \(D_2\) in

\[
H=G*\langle z\rangle
\tag{1.2}
\]

so that

\[
D_2^\delta\sim_H z^{-1}e.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Evaluate \(z=e\). Define the evaluated original slots

\[
b=e^{-1}p,
\qquad
d=t^{-1}exe^{-1}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

Multiplication on the opposite side changes a target only by conjugacy.
Intermediate whole-slot conjugations, event bridges, and vertex twists
evaluate to arbitrary elements of \(G\). Thus one can choose conjugate
representatives \(K,C\) of the evaluated \(D_1,B_1\) slots and elements
\(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in G\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
K&=d\alpha b^\epsilon\alpha^{-1},\\
C&=b\beta K^\eta\beta^{-1},\\
1&=K\gamma C^\theta\gamma^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{1.5}
\]

The final equation gives

\[
K=\gamma C^{-\theta}\gamma^{-1}.
\tag{1.6}
\]

With

\[
\rho=\beta\gamma,
\qquad
m=\eta\theta,
\tag{1.7}
\]

substitution into the middle equation gives the two equivalent forms

\[
\boxed{
C=b\rho C^{-m}\rho^{-1},
\qquad
b=C\rho C^m\rho^{-1}.
}
\tag{1.8}
\]

Equations (1.4)--(1.8), the six-row weights, and the fact that \(C\)
normally generates \(G\) are necessary consequences of a genuine
three-cross history. The point of the next section is that they are not
sufficient to force the braid conjugacy class.

## 2. An exact non-braid solution

Let

\[
c=x^3=t^4.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Use the feasible sign row

\[
(\epsilon,\eta,\theta)=(1,1,-1),
\tag{2.2}
\]

and define the following literal words in \(G\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
e&=x^{-6}(xtxt^3xtx),\\
b&=x^{-9}(x^2t^3x^2tx^2),\\
C&=x^{-6}(x^2t^3x^2),\\
\alpha&=xtxt^3xt,\\
\beta&=x^2,\qquad \gamma=1.
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.3}
\]

The initial powers are central:

\[
x^{-6}=c^{-2},
\qquad
x^{-9}=c^{-3}.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Define

\[
d=t^{-1}exe^{-1},
\qquad
K=d\alpha b\alpha^{-1}.
\tag{2.5}
\]

The amalgam normal form in
\(\langle x,t\mid x^3=t^4\rangle\) writes an element as

\[
c^q s_1\cdots s_r
\tag{2.6}
\]

with alternating \(x\)- and \(t\)-syllables. Direct reduction gives

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{word}&\text{amalgam normal form}\\ \hline
e^{-1}p,\ b
&
c^{-3}x^2t^3x^2tx^2\\
K,\ C,\ b\beta K\beta^{-1}
&
c^{-2}x^2t^3x^2\\
KC^{-1}
&
1.
\end{array}
\tag{2.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
b=e^{-1}p,
\qquad
K=d\alpha b\alpha^{-1}=C,
\qquad
C=b\beta K\beta^{-1},
\qquad
1=KC^{-1}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

These are exactly (1.5) in the sign row (2.2). The weights are

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{wt}(e)=7,
\qquad
\operatorname{wt}(b)=0,
\qquad
\operatorname{wt}(C)=1.
}
\tag{2.9}
\]

Thus the solution occupies the feasible tail-weight-\(7\),
survivor-weight-\(1\) row, rather than merely solving an ungraded
quotient equation.

## 3. The survivor is a killer outside the braid conjugacy class

Quotient the center:

\[
\Gamma
=
G/\langle c\rangle
\cong
C_3*C_4
=
\langle X,T\mid X^3=T^4=1\rangle.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The projection of \(C\) is

\[
\bar C=X^2T^3X^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Cyclic reduction combines its two boundary \(X\)-syllables:

\[
\bar C\sim_\Gamma XT^3=XT^{-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Killing (3.3) identifies \(X=T\). The relations \(X^3=T^4=1\)
then force

\[
X=T=1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Hence \(\bar C\) normally generates \(\Gamma\).

Let

\[
Q=G/\langle\!\langle C\rangle\!\rangle.
\tag{3.5}
\]

Since \(Q/\langle c\rangle\) is trivial, \(Q\) is generated by the image
of the central element \(c\); in particular, \(Q\) is cyclic. The
abelianization of \(G\) is \(\mathbb Z\), identified by torus weight, and
\(\operatorname{wt}(C)=1\). Therefore \(Q_{\mathrm{ab}}=1\). A cyclic
group equals its abelianization, so

\[
\boxed{Q=1.}
\tag{3.6}
\]

Thus \(C\) is a genuine weight-one killer of \(G\).

Now put

\[
D_p=t^{-1}(xt)x(xt)^{-1}.
\tag{3.7}
\]

The cyclically reduced alternating form of \(\bar C\) has length \(2\).
The cyclically reduced forms of
\(\overline{D_p}\) and \(\overline{D_p^{-1}}\) both have length \(6\).
Cyclic length is a conjugacy invariant in a free product, so

\[
\boxed{
C\not\sim_GD_p,
\qquad
C\not\sim_GD_p^{-1}.
}
\tag{3.8}
\]

This is consistent with the existence of non-meridional killers in
torus-knot groups. Normal generation and weight do not imply meridional
conjugacy.

## 4. The exact liftability condition

Let

\[
J=z^{-1}e
\tag{4.1}
\]

and let

\[
r_e:H\longrightarrow G,
\qquad
r_e(z)=e
\tag{4.2}
\]

be evaluation. If an oriented target \(T\in H\) satisfies

\[
r_e(T)=1,
\tag{4.3}
\]

then only

\[
T\in\langle\!\langle J\rangle\!\rangle_H
\tag{4.4}
\]

follows. Membership in that normal closure is much weaker than
conjugacy to \(J\).

There is a sharp Bass--Serre formulation. In all feasible rows,
\(\operatorname{wt}(e)\in\{5,7,9\}\), so \(e\ne1\). If additionally

\[
\sigma_z(T)=-1,
\tag{4.5}
\]

then

\[
\boxed{
T\sim_HJ
\iff
\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}(T)=2,
}
\tag{4.6}
\]

where \(\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}\) is cyclically reduced free-product syllable
length. Indeed, a length-two representative with stable exponent \(-1\)
rotates to \(z^{-1}f\) for some \(f\in G\). Equation (4.3) gives
\(e^{-1}f=1\), hence \(f=e\). The converse is immediate.

Equivalently, set

\[
q=ze^{-1}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

Then

\[
H=G*\langle q\rangle,
\qquad
J=e^{-1}q^{-1}e.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The kernel of \(r_e\) is free on

\[
q_g=gqg^{-1},
\qquad
g\in G.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Evaluation remembers only that \(T\) belongs to this free kernel.
Liftability requires its kernel word to be freely conjugate to one basis
letter \(q_g^{-1}\).

## 5. The explicit countermodel is not liftable

For the sign row (2.2), the final exponent calculation gives

\[
\delta=-1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Suppose, for contradiction, that arbitrary unevaluated bridges realize
the data (2.3) as a genuine \(DBD\) history. Quotient \(H\) by the
original

\[
B=z^{-1}p.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This sets \(z=p\) and identifies the quotient with \(G\). Put

\[
A=D[z\mapsto p]=D_p.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The first source factor disappears, so \(D_1\) maps to a conjugate of
\(A\). The second target maps to a conjugate of \(A\). Since
\(\theta=-1\), the third target maps, up to conjugacy and inversion, to

\[
[A,h]=AhA^{-1}h^{-1}
\tag{5.4}
\]

for some \(h\in G\). This conclusion is independent of multiplication
side and includes arbitrary event conjugators and fixed-\(R\) gauges.

On the other hand,

\[
J=z^{-1}e
\longmapsto
p^{-1}e=b^{-1}.
\tag{5.5}
\]

Because \(D_2^{-1}\sim_HJ\), a lift would therefore force

\[
\boxed{
b\sim_G[A,h]
}
\tag{5.6}
\]

for some \(h\in G\).

Project once more to

\[
\Gamma=C_3*C_4.
\tag{5.7}
\]

The explicit normal forms give

\[
\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\bar b)=4,
\qquad
\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}(\bar A)=6.
\tag{5.8}
\]

### Lemma 5.1

For every \(h\in\Gamma\),

\[
\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}([\bar A,h])
\in
\{0,8,10,12\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\ell_{\mathrm{cyc}}([\bar A,h])\ge14.
\tag{5.9}
\]

In particular, this cyclic length is never \(4\).

### Proof

Regard the commutator as the product of \(\bar A\) and a conjugate of
\(\bar A^{-1}\), and compare their axes in the Bass--Serre tree of
\(C_3*C_4\).

If the axes are disjoint at distance \(d\ge1\), the product traverses one
translation segment on each axis and the bridge twice. Therefore its
translation length is

\[
6+6+2d\ge14.
\tag{5.10}
\]

If the axes share an edge, base at an overlap endpoint. Edge stabilizers
are trivial, so the cyclic word is a product \(UV\), where \(U\) is one
of the six syllable rotations of \(\bar A\) and \(V\) is one of the six
rotations of \(\bar A^{-1}\).

If they meet only at a vertex, the residual vertex stabilizer inserts a
twist \(k\) from one of the two factors. It is enough to enumerate the
overcomplete set

\[
k\in\{1,X,X^2,T,T^2,T^3\}
\tag{5.11}
\]

in the words

\[
UkVk^{-1}.
\tag{5.12}
\]

The \(6\cdot6\cdot6=216\) exact alternating reductions have distribution

\[
\boxed{
\{0:18,\ 8:40,\ 10:28,\ 12:130\}.
}
\tag{5.13}
\]

The shared-edge case is the \(k=1\) subcase. Positive overlap and
identical axes are included by the rotations before cyclic reduction.
This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

Equations (5.8)--(5.9) contradict (5.6). Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{the explicit evaluated countermodel (2.3) has no genuine }
H\text{-lift}.
}
\tag{5.14}
\]

This nonlift result includes arbitrary kernel-valued bridge decorations;
it is not restricted to the canonical \(G\)-valued lifts used to display
the evaluated equations.

## 6. Consequence and scope

The countermodel proves that the evaluated equations (1.5), six-row
weights, normal generation, and central-quotient conjugacy do not by
themselves force the braid class. Lemma 5.1 then excludes this particular
countermodel from the true one-\(z\) move space.

Neither statement proves that every liftable prefix-\(DB\) survivor is
conjugate to \(D_p^{\pm1}\). The remaining theorem problem is to apply the
quotient-\(B\) commutator sieve and the free-kernel criterion (4.6) to all
solutions, or to construct a different solution which survives both.

This is **not** an AC obstruction, an AC-inequivalent endpoint, a
stable-AC counterexample, or a completed stable proof.
