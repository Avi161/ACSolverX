# TRAP — a contrast's hit-rate gap can be a LENGTH gap wearing a costume

## What happened

The stable-class matched contrast produced numbers that read as a decisive separation:

| root | members | SPHERICAL (thickenable) | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| AK(2)+z — positive control, class provably trivializable | 27,350 | 14,999 | **54.8%** |
| AK(3)+z — open target | 171,842 | **0** | 0% |

Everything about the experiment's design was sound and was verified: one shared operator
implementation (identity-checked member by member — 4,308 sampled edges on one root, 501
on the other), the same 1,000-pop budget per root, the same audited decision stack and
pinned budgets, and a length cap chosen in the CONSERVATIVE direction (18 vs 16, so the
open target got the strictly larger box and a zero-hit outcome could not be blamed on a
smaller search space).

And the comparison was still not sound, for a reason none of that controls: **the harvest
pops by ascending total length, and the two classes live at different lengths.** AK(2)+z is
trivializable, so its class SHRINKS — the harvest reaches total length 3, the standard
presentation itself, and spends most of its budget at lengths 3–11 where thickenable states
are common. AK(3)+z never produces a member below length 14.

Restricted to the length band the two harvests actually share (14–21):

| | members in band | spherical in band | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| AK(2)+z control | 8,862 | 13 | **0.147%** |
| AK(3)+z target | 31,039 | 0 | 0% |

The control's rate falls from 54.8% to 0.147% — a factor of 370 — the moment length is
held fixed. The honest scale of the residual signal is "0 observed where ~45 would be
expected at the control's in-band rate", not "54.8% versus zero".

## Why it is dangerous

Both numbers are true. The 54.8% figure is a correct statement about the control's class
and would have looked like the project's strongest result. Reporting it as the contrast
would have attributed to THICKENABILITY a separation that is substantially about LENGTH —
and the mechanism runs the wrong way for the conclusion, because being able to reach short
states is itself a consequence of the control being trivializable, i.e. part of what was
supposed to be under test.

## The rules that follow

1. **Match on the confound, not just on the instrument.** Operator identity, budget parity
   and cap parity are necessary and were all present here. They do not make two harvests
   comparable if the harvests occupy different regions of the state space. Report the
   per-length table, always, and lead with the in-band comparison.
2. **When the control can reach the trivial presentation and the target cannot, that IS
   the finding** — state it directly instead of laundering it through a rate.
3. **Never quote a p-value for class members.** They are generated by a move tree, are
   heavily correlated (one parent can spawn thousands of near-identical descendants), and
   an iid Poisson tail is fiction. Quote counts and an "expected at the control's in-band
   rate" figure explicitly labelled as a descriptive scale, not a test statistic.
4. **Audit your own headline before anyone else does.** The length-matched re-analysis
   (`experiments/stable_ac/fable/contrast_length_matched.py`) was written specifically to
   attack the result, and it should be the default second step of any contrast, run in the
   same session that produces the first number.
