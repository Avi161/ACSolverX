# covmeet3 · all124 · audit of the 2026-08-16 vast.ai snapshot

Independent audit of the run downloaded as `covmeet_out (1).zip` (snapshot mtime 2026-08-16 06:58:30 UTC, zip taken ~07:31). Nothing here was read from the run's own `summary.json` — every number is re-derived from the `.snap` bytes by a separate streaming parser, so a bug in `covmeet.py`'s accounting could not launder itself into this document.

## Verdict

**The store is sound.** Integrity, canonicalisation, the triviality invariant, chain replay and the class count all check out, and the merge detector is proven live rather than merely silent. **One real defect** was found and fixed — the run's `summary.json` was stale, which made `verify_covmeet` fail on a perfectly good store.

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `sha256` trailer over the packed body | OK |
| body length after decoding 14,627,414 orbit records | exact, no slack bytes |
| orbits carrying a parent pointer | 14,627,290 — missing exactly the 124 seeds |
| snapshot seed rows vs `aut_min(load_seeds("all124"))` | 124/124 identical, each carrying its own bit |
| 300 random orbits are `aut_min` fixed points | 300/300 |
| 300 random orbits have abelianization det ±1 | 300/300 — an invariant the pipeline never computes |
| 25 chains from deep orbits replayed to their seed, segment by segment | 3,840 segments, all exact |
| class count re-derived from the masks alone (independent union-find) | 124/124, matching the header |
| merge/drop detector fires when a meet exists | `tests/stable_ac/test_covmeet.py` 34/34, incl. two end-to-end merge tests |

Every relator alphabet check, every mask popcount and the frontier bucketing were re-derived the same way. The det ±1 check is the one that would falsify the pipeline rather than the orbit: every CoV step is a Tietze transformation, so every reachable orbit must present the trivial group, and `covmeet.py` never computes a determinant anywhere.

Mask popcount is **1 everywhere**: no orbit was reached by two different seeds, so the 124 cones exactly partition the 14,627,414 orbits. That is the null result, and it is a real null — the detector fires on a constructed meet in the test suite.

## The claim this supports, stated precisely

> No two of the 124 unsolved Aut-classes share a CoV descendant along any route that stays at total length **L ≤ 30**, modulo ~1,071 stragglers at L = 24–30 that the next few waves drain. L = 31 is 63% expanded (1,044,480 expanded / 618,461 open). Nothing below **μ = 13** exists anywhere in the 14.6M-orbit ball; the μ = 13 floor holds, and both μ = 13 orbits belong to `aca_115` — consistent with the 0 drops.

Three things it does **not** say:

1. **Not "these classes are inequivalent."** A cone is a forward-reachability set. A meet is *sufficient* for stable AC-equivalence; disjointness is not evidence of inequivalence. The routes that would join two of these classes may simply climb above L = 31 first.
2. **Not "14 million unsolved presentations."** See below.
3. **Not a statement about most pairs.** See the power analysis below.

## Power: the null is strong for the low-μ cluster and near-vacuous for the rest

Cone sizes are wildly unbalanced — the top 10 cones hold 86.6% of all orbits, the median cone is 1,280 orbits, and 41 of the 124 classes have a cone of ≤ 100 orbits.

The mechanism is **ball position, not scheduler unfairness**. The frontier is global shortest-bucket-first, so at a wavefront of L ≈ 30 a seed that starts at μ = 25 has almost all of its children *above* the wavefront and is barely explored, while a seed at μ = 13 has had 2.4M descendants processed. Shortest-first is not a bug here — it is exactly what buys the "closed through L ≤ 30" statement.

## On "14 million unsolved presentations"

That reading is not what the number is. Four corrections, in order of size:

1. **They are orbits, not presentations.** 14,627,414 distinct `Aut(F₂)`-canonical representatives — each stands for a whole automorphism orbit of concrete presentations, so the number of *presentations* underneath is very much larger still.
2. **None of them is known unsolved.** This pipeline runs **zero search nodes**. It never calls the greedy, never applies an AC or substitution move, and never asks whether anything is AC-trivial. The only inputs whose difficulty is known are the 124 seeds. The other ~14.6M are simply CoV descendants of them, untested in either direction.
3. **They are all presentations of the trivial group** — verified on the sample by abelianization det ±1, and guaranteed structurally, since every CoV step is a Tietze transformation. So "we have 14M unsolved presentations" would be the wrong surprise even if they had been tested: reachable balanced trivial presentations are cheap, and generating them is not the hard part.
4. **The count measures how far the walk went, not a property of the problem.** `REJECT_LEN_UNCAPPED = 10**9` means there is no length ceiling, so the CoV ball around 124 seeds is infinite. At 7.61 children per expansion and ~1.57 new orbits per expansion, the count grows roughly linearly in expansions forever. 14.6M is the odometer reading at 9.3M expansions, and running 72 more hours would print a bigger one without saying anything new about the conjecture.

The number that *is* interesting is the collapse: **803,258,473 raw CoVs → 14,627,414 distinct orbits, 54.9×**. Aut-minimisation is doing an enormous amount of work, and the effective branching after canonicalisation is 1.57 new orbits per expansion, not 7.61. (Do not confuse that 7.61 with the per-state census median of 8 distinct children — the first is a mean over all expansions, the second a median over states.)

## The defect: a stale `summary.json`

The downloaded `summary.json` reported

```
"expanded": 28224,  "discovered": 59056,
"stopped": "time bound 180s reached (smoke)"
```

— the **2026-08-15T01:15 smoke run** — while the snapshot beside it held 9,336,325 / 14,627,414. Cause: `run()` wrote the summary only in its `finally:` block, and `finally` is skipped by SIGKILL, which is exactly how a preempted spot instance dies. The ~25.6 h session that produced this snapshot (05:24:30 → 06:58+) was hard-killed and never got to write one.

Consequence: `verify_covmeet` hard-fails on the summary-vs-snapshot agreement check, and `report_covmeet` refuses to write unless the verifier passes. A healthy 14.6M-orbit store was unreportable because of a bookkeeping file.

**Fixed** in `experiments/stable_ac/cov/meet/covmeet.py`: `_write_summary()` is now called immediately after **every** `save_snapshot()` — the fresh-run seed checkpoint, each periodic checkpoint, and the end-of-run write. Snapshot first, summary second, because the snapshot write is minutes at this size and the summary is milliseconds; the reverse order would leave the summary *ahead* by a whole snapshot write. The disagreement window drops from "the whole session" to the milliseconds between the two writes. `ENGINE_TAG` is deliberately **not** bumped — event semantics and the expansion rule are untouched, and bumping it would make `load()` reject the existing 14.6M-orbit snapshot.

Recovery for a run that already has a stale summary:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m experiments.stable_ac.cov.meet.covmeet /workspace/covmeet_out --seed-set all124
```

`regen_summary()` reads only the snapshot, so what it writes is the store's numbers and not a previous summary's. It needs the RAM to hold the store (~500 B/orbit ≈ 7.3 GB here), which the search box has and this laptop does not — the local copy's summary was instead regenerated offline from the packed bytes and is stamped `regenerated_by`.

Two consequences of that gate are still on disk and are **not** fixed by the code change. [`COVMEET.md`](COVMEET.md) beside this file is still the smoke run's report — it presents 28,224 expanded as the result — because `report_covmeet` never ran on the real store; it carries a staleness banner until someone regenerates it on the box. And the search box's own session, still running the pre-fix code, will keep writing nothing until it is restarted; after a Restart → Run All it re-does at most one snapshot interval (~30 min) and the summary self-heals at the first checkpoint, with no manual `regen_summary` needed.

Pinned by three new tests in `tests/stable_ac/test_covmeet.py` (34/34): a simulated hard kill that skips the `finally` must still leave a summary matching the snapshot; `regen_summary` must repair a stale summary to the real numbers; and it must refuse when there is no snapshot. The kill test was mutation-checked — removing the checkpoint-site call makes it fail with `expanded: summary 0 vs snapshot 1`.

## Throughput, and what 72 more hours buys

Instantaneous rate fell from ~87 to ~57 states/s over the session. That is expected, not a fault: raw CoVs per state rose from ~65 to ~85 as L grew, the snapshot now takes ~174 s to write (so the adaptive cadence has stretched to ~29 min — matching the observed 36-minute gap between `.snap.prev` and `.snap`), and `take_wave` re-sorts the whole shortest bucket on every 4,096-orbit pop. Each additional unit of L costs roughly 1.3–1.6× the last, so a further 72 h lands the ball near **L ≈ 33**. High-μ cone coverage stays thin at that horizon — `aca_82` will still have been asked about a handful of orbits.

If the goal is a statement about *pairs* rather than about the ball, the scheduler is the knob, not the clock: a per-seed quota would trade the clean "closed through L ≤ 30" claim for coverage that is comparable across the 124. That is a design decision, not a bug fix, and is left to the user.

## Reproducing this audit

The audit tooling is deliberately outside the pipeline (a separate 2-bit unpacker, numba-compiled, streaming — the shipped `covmeet.load()` would need ~7 GB for this store). It lives in the session scratchpad rather than in `experiments/`, since it exists to be independent of the code it checks; the checks it duplicates from `verify_covmeet` — self-canonicality, det ±1, segment-by-segment chain replay, mask-derived union-find — are all available there and should be run on the box, where the store fits in RAM:

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python3 -m experiments.stable_ac.cov.meet.verify_covmeet /workspace/covmeet_out --sample 1000
```

That will now pass, because the summary is no longer stale.
