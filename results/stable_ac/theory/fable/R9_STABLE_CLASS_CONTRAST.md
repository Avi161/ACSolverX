# R9 — The stable-class contrast, and the length confound that halves it

STATUS: MEASUREMENT + SELF-AUDIT. Claims addressed: **none**. This document reports data
about two harvests and is deliberately written so that no AC-triviality claim, in either
direction, can be read out of it. Project Red line 3 applies throughout: *search failure
is never evidence of a counterexample.*

## The experiment

One operator, two stabilised roots, matched budgets:

* **positive control** AK(2)+z = ("xxYYY", "xyxYXY", "z") — AK(2) is AC-trivial, so this
  class is provably trivializable and the run is an end-to-end validation of the pipeline;
* **open target** AK(3)+z = ("xxxYYYY", "xyxYXY", "z").

`z` is a live generator in both: moves may entangle it, with no z-inertness bias of any
kind. That is the point — this is the stable move space, which no published computational
method has searched directly.

What was matched, and verified rather than asserted: a single shared operator
implementation (identity-checked member by member — 4,308 sampled edges verified on the
AK(3)+z root, 501 on AK(2)+z, plus 136 and 104 first-pop children respectively); the
seen-set (canonical states, relator permutations × rotations × inversions on cyclically
reduced relators); the priority (total canonical length); the pop budget (exactly 1,000
per root); and the decision stack with round-2 budgets imported verbatim from the audited
runs. Every single-step move composite was re-verified byte-exactly (171,841 of them on
the AK(3)+z side) and 100 sampled chains were replay-verified.

What was NOT matched, deliberately and in the conservative direction: the per-relator
length cap, 18 for AK(3)+z against 16 for AK(2)+z, by the rule "root total + 4". An
absolute match at 16 would have left AK(3)+z only 2 letters of headroom above its
14-letter root against the control's 4, mechanically suppressing child generation and
biasing the open target toward zero hits. Cap 18 makes the **open target's box strictly
larger**, so a zero-hit outcome cannot be blamed on a smaller search space.

## The raw result — and why it must not be quoted this way

| root | members | SPHERICAL | NOT_SPHERICAL | undecided | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AK(2)+z (control) | 27,350 | **14,999** | 12,351 | 0 | 54.8% |
| AK(3)+z (target) | 171,842 | **0** | 171,156 | 686 | 0% |

Cross-root canonical overlap: 0 members (a nonzero overlap would have placed a common
member in both classes and would have been headline-grade on its own).

One confound was checked and cleared. The control's thickenable members are not merely
stabilisations of thickenable rank-2 pairs sneaking in through the z-inert split route:
of the 14,999, only **50** were decided by the split route, while **14,860** were decided
by the rank-3 solver directly on z-entangled states and 89 by census. So the stable
contrast is genuinely about rank-3 states, not the classical contrast in disguise.

A second confound was checked and **was not cleared, and it is the important one.**

## The length confound

The harvest pops by ASCENDING total length, and the two classes do not live at the same
lengths. AK(2)+z is trivializable, so its class shrinks: the harvest reaches total length
**3** — the standard presentation itself — and spends most of its budget at lengths 3–11,
where thickenable states are common. AK(3)+z never produces a member below length 14.

Restricted to the band the two harvests actually share, lengths 14–21:

| | members in band | SPHERICAL in band | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| AK(2)+z (control) | 8,862 | 13 | **0.147%** |
| AK(3)+z (target) | 31,039 | **0** | 0% |

Per length, control spherical counts in band: 12 at length 14, 1 at length 20, zero
everywhere else. The control's rate falls by a factor of 370 the moment length is held
fixed.

**So the honest statement of this experiment is:**

> Under one verified-identical operator at a matched 1,000-pop budget, with the open
> target given the strictly larger length cap, the trivializable control's class yields
> thickenable members at 0.147% within the length band the two harvests share, where
> AK(3)+z's class yields none in 31,039. Scaled by the control's in-band rate, ~45 would
> have been expected. Outside that band the control reaches total length 3 and AK(3)+z
> does not get below 14 — and *that* asymmetry, not the raw 54.8%, is what the run
> actually demonstrates about the two classes.

**No p-value is quoted and none should be.** Class members are generated by a move tree
and are heavily correlated — a single parent can spawn thousands of near-identical
descendants — so an iid Poisson tail would be fiction. The "~45 expected" figure is a
descriptive scale, not a test statistic.

## What this does and does not support

It **does** support: the pipeline works end-to-end in the stable setting (the control
fires, abundantly, and on genuinely z-entangled states); AK(3)'s stable class shows no
thickenable member anywhere in a 171,842-member budgeted corridor; and the corridor
itself is qualitatively different from the control's, because the control's class
descends to the standard presentation within budget and AK(3)+z's does not descend at
all.

It **does not** support any claim that AK(3) is not stably AC-trivial. Bridson and Lishak
exhibit AC-trivializable presentations of ~30 symbols whose shortest trivialization
exceeds 10^10000 moves, and those lower bounds survive stabilization. A budgeted corridor
finding nothing is exactly what a very long trivialization looks like from the inside.
The result is consistent with AK(3) being stably AC-trivial with a trivialization far
beyond any reachable budget, and nothing here distinguishes that from the alternative.

It also inherits the thickenability dependency: "SPHERICAL" here means γ_N = 0, i.e.
orientably thickenable, and the bridge from thickenable to AC-trivializable is
[UNVERIFIED] this session — see `LITERATURE_STATUS.md`. That flag does not affect the
measurement, only what could ever be concluded from a hit.
