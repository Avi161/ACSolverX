## Result

This is a new technique for the codebase. It does not use change of variables, Lemma 11, substitution-and-removal, or an automorphism filter.

The solver state is enriched from a relator tuple to a certified triple `(T, X, X')`:

- `T` is a triangulation of the 3-sphere;
- `X` is a maximal tree in the primal 1-skeleton;
- `X'` is a maximal tree in the dual 1-skeleton.

Lackenby's presentation `P(T,X,X')` has:

- one generator `g_e` for every oriented primal edge `e` not in `X`;
- one relator `r_f` for every oriented triangular face `f` whose dual edge is not in `X'`;
- `r_f` equal to the boundary word of `f`, with edges in `X` deleted.

It is a balanced presentation of the trivial group because the omitted tetrahedron interiors and omitted dual-tree faces form an open 3-ball.

The new actions are:

1. **Dual-Tree Teleport:** keep `T` and `X` fixed and replace `X'` by any other dual maximal tree. This changes which face boundaries are relators.
2. **Pachner Collapse:** perform a legal `4-1` or `3-2` Pachner move, update the two trees by Lackenby's deterministic alignment procedure, and output the exact new presentation `P(T_2,X_2,X'_2)`. A `4-1` collapse can remove three generator-relator pairs at once.

These are presentation transitions generated by 3-dimensional topology. They are not coordinate changes of the initial relator tuple.

## Supermove 1: one dual-tree relator exchange

Let `a` be an edge of the dual graph not in `X'`. Adding `a` to `X'` creates a unique cycle. Choose an edge `b` of `X'` on that cycle and define

`X'' = (X' - {b}) union {a}`.

Let `f_a` and `f_b` be the triangular faces of `T` dual to `a` and `b`. Then the source and exact target are

`P(T,X,X')  ->  P(T,X,X'')`.

Their generators are identical. Their relator sets are identical except that

`r_(f_a)` is removed and `r_(f_b)` is inserted.

The reason is immediate from the presentation constructor: faces dual to tree edges are omitted. In the source, `a` is outside the tree and `b` is inside; in the target their roles are reversed.

Lackenby's Lemma 3.6 proves that, if `T` has `t` tetrahedra, this exact transition requires at most

`C_swap(t) = 4t^2 + 12t - 16`

ordinary Andrews-Curtis moves. No stable-AC conjecture or Lemma 11 is used. The proof slides the attaching map of `f_b` across an annulus in the 3-ball encoded by `X'`, crossing at most `2t-2` triangles. Each triangle crossing is an explicit conjugated relator multiplication plus cancellations.

## Supermove 2: teleport to any dual-tree presentation

The maximal-tree exchange graph of a connected graph is connected. More precisely, Lackenby's Lemma 2.11 converts one dual maximal tree to any specified other one using at most `t-1` edge swaps, because a dual maximal tree contains `t-1` edges.

Therefore, for any two dual maximal trees `X'_A` and `X'_B`, the exact transition

`P(T,X,X'_A)  ->  P(T,X,X'_B)`

has the ordinary AC bound

`C_teleport(t) = (t-1)(4t^2 + 12t - 16)`.

The target family is explicit: it consists of one presentation for every spanning tree of the dual graph. It can be exponentially large in `t`, can be enumerated or sampled with standard spanning-tree algorithms, and is deduplicated only after the presentations have been constructed.

This is the arbitrary-structure part of the technique: within a fixed certified triangulation, the policy may jump to any balanced face-relator system selected by a dual spanning tree, not merely to a word substitution or a free-group automorphic image.

## Verified non-automorphic example

Regina 7.4 recognises the triangulation with isomorphism signature

`fLLQcbcedeeajasdl`

as `S^3`. Its `f`-vector is `(V,E,F,T)=(2,7,10,5)`. Fix primal tree `{0}` and source/target dual trees

`X'_A = {0,1,2,5}`,

`X'_B = {0,2,4,5}`.

This is one dual-tree edge swap: face `4` moves into the tree and face `1` moves out. With generators `x_1,...,x_6`, the exact source is

`P_A = <x_1,...,x_6 |`

`x_3 x_3 x_4^-1,`

`x_3 x_5^-1,`

`x_6 x_1^-1,`

`x_6 x_5 x_2^-1,`

`x_1 x_5^-1 x_6,`

`x_3 x_5^-1 x_1>`.

The exact target is

`P_B = <x_1,...,x_6 |`

`x_4 x_2^-1,`

`x_3 x_3 x_4^-1,`

`x_6 x_1^-1,`

`x_6 x_5 x_2^-1,`

`x_1 x_5^-1 x_6,`

`x_3 x_5^-1 x_1>`.

Thus the move replaces the face-4 relator `x_3 x_5^-1` by the previously omitted face-1 relator `x_4 x_2^-1`; the other five relators are unchanged.

The verification gates are:

- Regina `isSphere()` returns true;
- both presentations are balanced of rank 6;
- their exponent-sum determinants are `-1` and `+1`;
- exhaustive Whitehead descent gives minimum total tuple lengths `6` for `P_A` and `14` for `P_B`;
- an independent Whitehead-graph cut calculation verifies that neither reduced tuple admits a length-decreasing Whitehead automorphism;
- the substitution and graph-cut length formulas agree on 11,200 independently generated cases.

Whitehead minimum length is invariant under `Aut(F_6)`, relator permutation, cyclic relator rotation, and relator inversion. Since `6 != 14`, no free-group automorphism carries `P_A` to `P_B`. This is a rigorous example of a single new supermove reaching a non-automorphic state.

For `t=5`, Lemma 3.6 gives the ordinary AC ceiling

`4(5^2) + 12(5) - 16 = 144`.

## Supermove 3: Pachner collapse

Dual-tree teleport changes the relator structure at fixed rank. Pachner collapse changes the triangulation and can reduce rank.

Lackenby's Proposition 4.2 gives an exact target constructor for each legal Pachner move:

`P(T,X,X')  ->  P(T_2,X_2,X'_2)`.

It also gives a local supplementary-move bound

`S_Pachner(t) = 12t^2 + 108t - 13`,

with every intermediate presentation of length at most `6t+4`.

For a `4-1` move, first use primal and dual tree swaps to put the four-tetrahedron ball into Lackenby's aligned form. Up to the recorded orientation and basepoint choices, the presentation is exactly

`<G,x,y,z | R, x a^-1, y b^-1, z c^-1 y^-1>`,

where each of `a,b,c` is the identity, an old generator, or its inverse. The `4-1` collapse outputs exactly

`<G | R>`.

It deletes three generators and three relators simultaneously. This cannot be an automorphism of the input presentation because the rank changes.

For a `3-2` move, align the primal tree if the disappearing degree-three edge lies in it, align two edges of the dual 3-cycle, and then remove the generator associated with the disappearing edge and its length-at-most-three defining relator. The target is again computed directly as `P(T_2,X_2,X'_2)`.

Both endpoints are thickenable balanced presentations of the trivial group. Let

`B(l) = 2^(2^(2,000,000 l^2))`.

Lackenby's Theorem 1.2 therefore gives the fully elementary stable-AC ceiling

`C_Pachner-SAC(t) = 2 B(6t+4)`

for any one exact Pachner successor: reduce both endpoints to the standard presentation and reverse the target path. This bound is intentionally enormous, while Proposition 4.2 retains the compact local certificate.

## Complete action space

From an enriched state `(T,X,X')`, generate:

1. every legal one-edge dual-tree exchange;
2. sampled or chosen full dual-tree teleports;
3. every legal `3-2` collapse around a degree-three edge;
4. every legal `4-1` collapse around a degree-four vertex;
5. optional inverse `2-3` and `1-4` expansions when escaping a local minimum.

The primary search order should be:

1. rank-decreasing `4-1`;
2. rank-decreasing `3-2`;
3. dual-tree targets with lower relator length or better solver heuristic;
4. topology-expanding moves.

The presentation target is never guessed. It is reconstructed from `(T_2,X_2,X'_2)`, and the move record stores the changed face, tree exchange, and Regina isomorphism signature.

## Entry from an ordinary presentation

The technique applies when a thickenability certificate is available.

For a balanced thickenable trivial presentation `P` of length `l`, Lackenby's construction gives:

1. a thickenable triangular presentation `P'` using at most `5l(n+1)` moves `(0)-(4+)`;
2. a triangulation `T` of `S^3` with at most `2l` tetrahedra;
3. auxiliary trees `(X,X')`;
4. a path of at most `l` additional `(4+)` moves from `P'` to `P(T,X,X')`.

After that one-time lift, dual-tree and Pachner actions have exact presentation outputs.

This domain restriction is real. Converting an arbitrary balanced trivial presentation into a thickenable one is equivalent to the open stable Andrews-Curtis conjecture. The current AK(3) CoV outputs certified non-thickenable in the repository cannot enter this action space without a different thickenable state being found.
