# General-rank cyclic-complement theorem: independent proof audit

**Verdict: valid, for every n >= 1.** The criterion gives a stable AC trivialization with maximum rank n+1, hence one additional generator-relator pair. It does not require an ambient automorphism to be treated as an AC move on an arbitrary presentation. The proof source already states the general-rank extension in its section “The same criterion on the literal MMS02 triple”; this audit makes the kernel, signs, donor use and two-complement boundary explicit. This is an audit of an established sufficient criterion, not a new family or a new solve.

## Statement

Let A1,...,An be words in F(a1,...,an). Suppose their integer exponent matrix E, with these n vectors as columns, has determinant +1 or -1. If there is a word c such that

`<A1,...,An,c> = F(a1,...,an)`,

then the balanced presentation with relators A1,...,An is stably AC-trivial, using at most one extra generator at any time.

The hypotheses already imply that the presented group is trivial: its quotient is generated by the image of c, so is cyclic, while E makes its abelianization zero. This observation is not a substitute for the AC construction below. Without the cyclic-complement hypothesis, unimodularity alone gives only a perfect quotient.

## Proof with exact markings

Set G = F(x1,...,xn,t) and define the epimorphism f:G -> F(a1,...,an) by f(xi)=Ai and f(t)=c. Nielsen reduction of a redundant generating tuple in a free group supplies a free basis

`(W,u1,...,un)`

such that f(W)=1 and f(u1),...,f(un) form a free basis of the target. Nielsen-adjust the ui among themselves to arrange f(ui)=ai. Consequently ker(f) is exactly the normal closure of W: killing W leaves the free group on the ui and the induced map is an isomorphism. Finding an arbitrary kernel element would not establish this property.

The abelianized map has matrix `[E | v]`, where v is the exponent vector of c. Its integer kernel is

`{(-E^-1 v k, k) : k in Z}`.

Because E is unimodular, the displayed vector for k=1 is an integral primitive generator of this kernel. The abelianization of W is also a generator: the full basis `(W,u1,...,un)` changes the map matrix to `[0 | I]`. Hence the t-exponent of W is exactly +1 or -1. Invert W if necessary so that the projection pi killing every xi has `pi(W)=t`. Here F(t) is infinite cyclic, so an exponent statement determines the entire projected word.

Put `h=x1^-1 W`. Then `pi(h t^-1)=1`, so `h t^-1` is a finite product of conjugates of the xi and their inverses. Starting with the standard basis relator tuple, use these first n rows as restored donors to change only the last row:

`(x1,x2,...,xn,t)  ~AC  (x1,x2,...,xn,h)`.

If `h t^-1 = g1 ... gm`, left-donate the factors in the order gm,...,g1. The final last row is `(g1 ... gm)t=h`. Every donor is an original xi row, and is restored after conjugation/inversion. No recipient is used as its own donor. Right-multiply the first row by the last to obtain

`(W,x2,...,xn,h)`.

Let theta be the automorphism sending the marked free basis `(W,u1,...,un)` to `(t,x1,...,xn)`. Let q:G -> F(x1,...,xn) kill t, and let rho rename ai as xi. Their markings give the exact homomorphism identity

`q theta = rho f`.

Apply theta to every word and every conjugator in the preceding ordinary AC path. An automorphism carries each actual AC1/AC2/AC3 step to another step of the same type. This yields a path from the free basis `(theta(x1),...,theta(xn),theta(t))` to

`(t, theta(x2),...,theta(xn), theta(h))`.

Nielsen **row moves on the standard free basis** reach that transformed starting basis; prefix them. This step is legitimate specifically because the starting tuple is a free basis. No unsupported ambient move on an arbitrary tuple is introduced.

Now the literal first row t is a restored donor. Remove every t/T occurrence from each other row, then destabilize that t row and its generator. The surviving words are exactly

`(rho(A2),...,rho(An),rho(A1)^-1)`,

since `theta(h)=theta(x1)^-1 t`. Invert the last row and reorder. Starting from the standard rank-n presentation, this construction used one stabilization, rank-(n+1) ordinary row moves, and one destabilization to reach the original tuple. Reverse it to obtain its stable trivialization. All operations are finite; no bound on the elementary normal-product length is inferred.

## Why two complements do not automatically iterate the proof

For an epimorphism `F(x1,...,xn,t1,t2) -> F_n`, Nielsen reduction supplies a primitive kernel **pair** W1,W2, completed by n marked lifts to a free basis. With matrix `[E | C]`, the integer kernel is

`{(-E^-1 C v, v) : v in Z^2}`.

Projection to the tag coordinates is an isomorphism on this integer kernel. Thus the projected words `p1=pi(W1), p2=pi(W2)` have a unimodular 2-by-2 exponent matrix; kernel-basis Nielsen moves can even normalize that matrix to I. This does **not** say `(p1,p2)=(t1,t2)`, that either projected word is primitive, or that this projected pair has a legal AC trivialization. The rank-one step “exponent +1 implies the word t” fails exactly because F(t1,t2) is nonabelian.

If the original presentation is known trivial, the projected pair normally generates F(t1,t2). Indeed,

`F(t1,t2)/Ncl(p1,p2) = G/Ncl(x1,...,xn,W1,W2) = F_n/Ncl(A1,...,An)`.

For two complements, unimodularity alone does not prove the last quotient trivial: it may be a nontrivial perfect two-generated group. Even when triviality is known, a normally generating unimodular pair need not come with an AC certificate. Assuming that certificate is the missing step, not an abelian dimension calculation.

There is an exact explanation of how much difficulty can survive. For any balanced rank-two tuple `(A1,A2)`, take the two complement images to be the ambient basis a1,a2. In G=F(x1,x2,t1,t2), set

`W1=x1 A1(t1,t2)^-1`, `W2=x2 A2(t1,t2)^-1`.

Then `(W1,W2,t1,t2)` is a free basis, with explicit inverses `xi=Wi Ai(t1,t2)`, and W1,W2 normally generate the kernel. Killing x1,x2 gives exactly

`(p1,p2)=(A1(t1,t2)^-1,A2(t1,t2)^-1)`.

Thus the projected pair can simply be the original AC problem with inverted rows. Primitive kernel words in the larger free group do not force primitive or AC-trivial projected words.

## Useful conditional criterion

For n >= 2, two complements do give a certificate if the projected tag pair `(p1,p2)` has an **ordinary** AC certificate to `(t1,t2)`. Reverse that certificate on the tag rows. Set `hi=xi^-1 Wi` for i=1,2; since `pi(hi)=pi(Wi)=p_i`, correct the tag rows from p_i to hi using only the restored original xj rows. Right multiplication changes x1,x2 to W1,W2. Transport the entire basis-started path through the marked basis map sending W1,W2 to t1,t2, prefix its starting-basis Nielsen row moves, and use those literal tag rows to clear and delete both tags. The survivors are the original relators, with the first two inverted and reordered. Maximum rank is n+2.

A concrete sufficient gate for that tag certificate is that **one projected row is primitive**, together with the unimodular exponent matrix already proved. Straighten that row to a tag generator, clear it from the companion, and the determinant forces the survivor to be the other tag generator to exponent +1 or -1. Transporting the ordinary trivialization back leaves a terminal free basis, which Nielsen row moves reduce. This yields a checkable route without assuming the full rank-two AC conjecture.

More generally, an independently verified stable tag certificate using s additional generators can be lifted with maximum rank n+2+s. Its extra stabilizations must be counted. Neither criterion justifies automatic iteration based solely on join corank, exponent rank, or the existence of a primitive kernel pair.

## Relation to the saved dictionary screen

`rank3_complement_report.json` records 84 complete negative cyclic-complement tests of the independently audited shorter dictionary tuples, 7,672 vertex-pair checks, and zero candidate IDs. This proof audit checks those report counts and their source hashes; it does not rerun or independently certify all graph folds. The theorem therefore supplies no new solve from that screen. Failure of a cyclic complement excludes only this sufficient route for the current literal tuple; it is not an obstruction to stable or ordinary AC triviality and is not invariant under changes to the relators.

For a rank-three dictionary tuple, a future positive would use rank at most four in this explicit theorem. Composing that with the earlier rank-two-to-rank-three dictionary prefix can involve two added pairs relative to the original rank-two input. The theorem's phrase “one stabilization” is relative to its own rank-n input.

## Feasibility of a proper two-word complement probe

A fixed choice such as `C=a1,D=a2^2` is a legitimate bounded candidate: first verify `<A1,A2,C,D>=F2`, obtain an explicit marked Nielsen basis of F4, project its two kernel rows, and apply only independently certified ordinary tag solvers. This is an application of the existing conditional transfer theorem. New coverage would require a shorter certified presentation or solve; changing the complement words alone is not a theorem advance.

Properness of `<C,D>` avoids one immediate ambient-basis return but does not by itself make the projected tag problem new. For example, the natural completion `C=A1^-1 a1,D=A2^-1 a2` has marked lifts `u1=x1 t1,u2=x2 t2`. The kernel basis `Wi=xi Ai(u1,u2)^-1` projects again to `(A1(t1,t2)^-1,A2(t1,t2)^-1)`. Thus such a construction returns the input regardless of whether that complement subgroup is proper. Compare the actual projected pair, including its Aut orbit when available, before claiming a different problem.

The older proper-substitution obstruction does not automatically rule out this route: the condition is `<A1,A2,C,D>=F2`, not containment of `<A1,A2>` in `<C,D>`. The practical missing data are explicit Nielsen-basis inverses and the projected-pair AC certificate. No complement enumeration or tag search was run for this feasibility assessment.
