## Exact algorithm and budget

The first step folds the subgroup generated by `(A,B,x,yy)` and rejects a proper join. A bounded heap then prioritizes total **noncyclic** image length of a marked four-tuple. Its moves are elementary left/right Nielsen row multiplications by another row or its inverse. Every image tuple is normalized using explicitly recorded row inversions and permutations, with the same operations applied to its domain basis in F(r,s,t,u). There is no independent ambient or cyclic conjugation in this search.

Each multiplication candidate costs one word image, and its inversion normalization costs one more. Initial inversion comparisons, terminal sign changes, kernel projections, and each word image in projected comparisons share the same input budget. Empty targets/donors are omitted and image tuples are deduplicated; this is a bounded sufficient search, not a completeness test for possible marked bases. The image-word guard is128 and the domain-basis-word guard is2048; neither fired in the saved panel. Domain witness manipulations and their inverse verification are certificate bookkeeping outside this candidate-image unit.

The serial screen used 0.0961 CPU seconds, excluding independent verification, with50ms cooling per row. The conservative work total is **14984**. `aca_116` used936 images through its first tag checks,32 for the bounded projected followup, and16 to reconstruct the complete Whitehead sweep as an explicit stored witness: **984** total, leaving **16** shared units for the continuation now recorded below. The14 exhausted rows remain at1000. No `y^3` followup was run. A subsequent16-pop continuation may therefore be recorded as984+16 declared heterogeneous units, with no claim that these units have equal computational cost.

## General stable-equivalence proof

Let P=(A,B) be a balanced presentation of the trivial group, let f:F(r,s,t,u)->F(a,b) be onto with f(r)=A,f(s)=B, and suppose a verified free basis `(W1,W2,V1,V2)` satisfies f(Wi)=1 and f(V1)=a,f(V2)=b. Let pi kill r,s, and set Q=(pi(W1),pi(W2)) in F(t,u).

Consider the balanced rank-four presentation

`T=(r,s,W1,W2)`.

Because the marked basis identifies `ker f=Ncl(W1,W2)`, its presented group is

`F(r,s,t,u)/Ncl(r,s,W1,W2) = F(a,b)/Ncl(A,B) = 1`.

Starting from Q, stabilize with unit rows r and s. Each `Wi pi(Wi)^-1` lies in `Ncl(r,s)`, the kernel of the projection killing r,s. A finite normal-product expression in those two literal unit donors therefore corrects the two projected rows to W1,W2, restoring both unit donors. This reaches T by ordinary row operations after two stabilizations. It also proves that Q presents the trivial group.

Let theta send the verified basis `(W1,W2,V1,V2)` to `(z1,z2,a,b)`. Applying that marked ambient change to T gives

`(theta(r),theta(s),z1,z2)`.

The known-trivial-group stable ambient-change lemma makes this a finite stable AC step: each ambient Nielsen map can be implemented by a defining-generator macro, using one temporary helper at a time. This is the only step here that is not claimed ordinary. Its hypothesis is justified by the displayed quotient computation for T; it is not inferred from unimodularity alone.

The actual unit rows z1,z2 now clear their letters from theta(r),theta(s). Killing z1,z2 is exactly the marked map f, so the surviving relators are **(A,B)**, with no omitted sign or endpoint automorphism. Destabilize the two unit rows. Thus Q and P are stably AC-equivalent, with rank-four bridge boundaries and maximum rank at most five for the stated helper implementation of the ambient maps. Reversibility gives both directions. Normal-product expansions are finite but are not emitted; neither an elementary path-length bound nor an ordinary-equivalence statement follows.

For the recorded aca_116 basis, the inverse-basis projections recover the literal P above. The complete bridge data are saved under `stable_equivalence_witness`. This strengthens the earlier conditional solve-only use of the tag pair. It does not imply that all projected pairs are ordinarily AC-equivalent, that the bridge is rank-four internally, or that join corank is invariant under row changes.

## Aut comparison and cheap terminal checks

Q is already minimal in total cyclic length under Aut(F2). The saved eight signed left/right Nielsen maps give lengths

`24,24,24,24,23,23,23,23`,

all greater than21. In rank two, these are exactly the non-inner Type-II Whitehead maps. The other Type-II maps are identities or inner conjugations, while Type-I signed permutations preserve total cyclic length. Whitehead's shortening theorem therefore certifies the minimum21. Since P has length14, P and Q lie in different Aut orbits, even allowing separate cyclic conjugation/inversion and relator permutation. This is an Aut distinction, not a stable-AC distinction.

Both cyclic words of Q have mixed signs in at least one generator, excluding the rank-two cyclic primitive-word form. They have respectively8 and8 cyclic letter changes, so neither is a two-block power word. Their absolute occurrence counts are `(4,5)` and `(7,5)` for x,y; neither has the two stable-letter occurrences required by a literal consecutive-BS donor. These are direct word inspections, not extra primitive searches or negative claims about other AC frames.
