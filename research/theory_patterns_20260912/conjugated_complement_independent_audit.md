# Independent adversarial review: finite conjugated attachments

Date: 2026-09-12. Reviewed source: `conjugated_complement_theory.md`.

**Verdict: PASS.** The equivalence of the three stated existence conditions
and the bound `floor(|R|/2)+floor(|S|/2)` on a sufficient conjugator are valid
under the stated hypotheses: R,S nonempty and cyclically reduced, with their
combined literal alphabet support equal to {x,y}. This review did not assume
the desired conclusion or infer it from the finite experiments.

The result concerns a cyclic **join** complement. It does not prove that
the subgroup has trivial intersection with its complement, nor does it by
itself give an ordinary AC trivialization. No complete negative is obtained
from a capped attachment computation.

## 1. The disconnected rank budget is legitimate

Use graph cycle rank beta(G)=|E|-|V|+number_of_components, counting each
unoriented edge once. For an embedded subgraph Sigma of a connected graph
Delta, even when Sigma is disconnected,

    beta(Sigma) <= beta(Delta).

This is not the false assertion that a subgroup's free-group rank is
monotone under inclusion. It is an assertion about an actual **subgraph**:
its integer cycle chains inject into those of Delta, and neither graph has
two-cells. Thus the excess e=beta(Delta)-beta(Sigma) is nonnegative.

Initially the two disjoint old cycles have total cycle rank2. A connecting
path adds no cycle, and the extra w-loop adds at most one. Consequently e<=1.
If c is empty, record the initial cross-identification immediately and take
the wedged image of the two old cycles as Sigma. Its rank is still2; the
argument continues with the cross-identification already used. If w is empty,
e=0 and no paid internal identification is possible.

## 2. Every folding case preserves the claimed accounting

Keep Sigma as its **image subgraph**, rather than retaining multiplicities
of original edges. Prioritize its own folds. Then:

| operation | change in beta(Delta) | change in beta(Sigma) | change in e |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| open fold wholly inside Sigma |0|0|0|
| closed fold wholly inside Sigma |-1|-1|0|
| fold preserving the embedding of Sigma |0 or-1|0|0 or-1|
| fold identifying two old vertices in the same component |0|+1|-1|
| fold identifying old vertices in different components |0|0|0|

The last two rows require justification. Since Sigma has already been folded,
at least one of the competing arcs lies outside it. The two terminal old
vertices are distinct in Delta because Sigma is embedded. The fold therefore
removes one Delta edge and one Delta vertex: it is open. The image of Sigma
loses one vertex and **no old edge**. Its component count stays fixed in the
internal case and falls by one in the cross case, giving the table.

This also handles the potentially troublesome case where **both competing
arcs are outside Sigma**, with their two terminal vertices inside it. It has
exactly the same accounting. Closed outside folds can spend the budget but
cannot restore it. There can therefore be at most one internal identification.

There is exactly one selected cross-identification, counting a possible
initial c=1 attachment. Images of the two old components cannot merge by an
old-subgraph fold while they remain disjoint: such a fold would require a
common initial vertex. Once connected, their image cannot disconnect.

## 3. Core pruning and full support do not conceal lost old edges

Each old cycle maps to a closed path reading its unchanged cyclically reduced
label. Such a path cannot use a hanging-tree edge: at its furthest visit into
that tree it would immediately retrace an edge, contradicting free reduction.
Thus core pruning never removes an edge belonging to the image of an old
cycle. Tree pruning outside Sigma preserves its image and cannot increase e.

At the end Delta is the full x/y rose. Every letter occurring in either old
cycle still labels its image edge. Full combined support therefore makes the
final image of Sigma the full rose itself. This is the precise point where
the stated support hypothesis is used. The proof must not substitute the
rank of the subgroup generated by R,S for this image-subgraph argument.

## 4. The order of the two selected identifications is harmless

The author's commutation step is correct. The following formalization makes
its scope explicit and rules out dependence on an intermediate folding order.

Choose original-vertex preimages of the selected cross pair and optional
internal pair. On the original disjoint cycles, let E be the **least
label-compatible vertex equivalence relation** containing those pairs. Here
label-compatible means that equivalent origins of equally labelled directed
arcs force equivalent terminal vertices; inverse arcs are included.

Every identification made by an old-subgraph fold is forced by that closure
rule and by the selected pairs already imposed. Conversely the final
old-image quotient is folded and contains both selected identifications, so
its vertex relation contains E. These two inclusions show that the final
old-image graph is exactly the folded quotient generated by the selected
pairs, including deletion of duplicate labelled arcs.

The selected pairs can therefore be imposed in either order. Before the
cross-identification occurs, its endpoints have preimages in different
original cycles. Impose that pair first. In the resulting connected folded
graph, the other pair either already coincides or is a pair of vertices whose
identification and folding give the full rose. The former case needs no extra
generator; the latter is obtained by adjoining the difference of two rooted
path labels. This establishes the required finite attachment.

There are no newly created vertices to obstruct choosing original preimages:
folding and the selected identifications only take vertex quotients.

## 5. Based-word conventions and length bound pass

If p labels a path from the R start to u and q labels a path from the S start
to v, gluing u=v gives the based subgroup

    <R, p q^-1 S q p^-1>.

Consequently the correct convention for the theorem's second generator is
`c=q p^-1`. At the glued basepoint the generators are
`p^-1 R p` and `q^-1 S q`. A cyclic complement w' there transports to
`w=p w' p^-1` at the original R basepoint. All signs in the proposed note
agree with these identities.

A shortest path on an original cycle may use the negative direction. If
R=AB, the paths A and B^-1 to the same vertex give the same rotation BA
after free conjugation. Their difference is a full turn around R and changes
only the generating description of the attached subgroup. Choose shortest
paths independently to obtain

    |c| <= |p|+|q| <= floor(|R|/2)+floor(|S|/2).

The bound is existential. It does not shorten every given conjugator c, and
it puts no bound on the required extra word w. An empty c or w is allowed.
Already-full subgroups can use w=1; allowing a nonempty cyclic generator
instead would not change their positivity. One-vertex folded graphs need
the explicit rose/missing-label cases, as the proposed implementation states.

## 6. Primary-source check and independent finite challenge

[Delgado–Silva, On the lattice of subgroups of a free group: complements and rank](https://gcc.episciences.org/6059/pdf),
Lemma3.13, uses the same old-subgraph-preserving fold strategy for a connected
core. Lemma5.3 supplies the one-vertex-identification complement criterion;
the bouquet exception is separated in Theorem5.4. The two-component extension
above is supplied by the present relative-rank proof, not attributed to their
paper.

`conjugated_complement_independent_check.py` uses a separately written
equivalence-class graph folder, explicit core pruning, and its own complement
and rotation enumeration. It checks10 small full-support pairs, challenges
complete-negative finite-attachment results with all reduced conjugators of
length<=3, and independently checks the shortest-path signs. Results:

- 236 attachment vertex-pair identifications;
- 2,161 direct vertex-pair identifications on longer-conjugator tests;
- 191 attachment sign and length-bound checks;
- no counterexample or failed assertion; elapsed0.1733s.

The records are `conjugated_complement_independent_audit.json`. These finite
checks detect implementation/sign mistakes and small counterexamples; the
unbounded verdict rests on Sections1–5, not extrapolation from the checks.

## 7. Reporting limits

The finite theorem authorizes a complete negative only after **every distinct
attachment is tested completely**, with exact graph-based reuse if desired.
The author's20-row screen is capped at1,000 identifications per row, so its
unfinished rows remain unknown. Arbitrary independent cyclic normalization
of a rooted subgroup's relators is not a valid cache key. Stable simplification
from a positive complement still requires the separate stable-composite
argument and certificate conventions used elsewhere in the project.
