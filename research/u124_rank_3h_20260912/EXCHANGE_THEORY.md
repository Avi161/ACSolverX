# Cancellation-aware definitions and stable generator exchange

All stable conclusions below require a known balanced presentation of the
trivial group. They use exactly Lemma 11 (Substitution and Removal), page 43
of [Shehper et al., arXiv:2408.15332](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15332), with
the scope recorded in the previous investigation's
[`LEMMA11_RESEARCH.md`](../rank_unbounded_20260912/LEMMA11_RESEARCH.md).
An event proves a finite stable composite; it is not an emitted elementary
normal-product expansion. All lengths count every current relator.

## 1. Freely equal templates go beyond literal block replacement

Choose old-generator words `w_1,...,w_k`, fresh generators `z_1,...,z_k`, and
retain all defining relators `D_i=z_i^-1 w_i`. For each old row `R_j`, find
a template `T_j` in the old generators and the helpers whose expansion
`z_i -> w_i` is freely equal to a recorded conjugate or inverse of `R_j`.
The full new tuple is `(D_1,...,D_k,T_1,...,T_n)`.

This is an exact allowed stable composite. Apply Lemma 11 in reverse to add
the definitions. Starting at `T_j`, substitution using the retained `D_i`
changes each helper to its defining word and yields the recorded old row
after free reduction. Reverse that finite sequence to pass from the old row
to `T_j`; other donors can be restored throughout. No occurrence must survive
as a literal block in the final expanded word. The only algebraic requirement
is exact free equality of the complete expansion, which is independently
checked for every row. Several definitions are added sequentially; the
implemented coordinated dictionaries depend only on the preexisting basis.

For example, let `c=[a,b]=abAB`. Then `c b c` freely reduces to `abbAB`.
The two displayed copies of `c` overlap through cancellations in that final
word, so disjoint literal tokenization cannot recover this template.

Unlike a literal substitution, such a template has no universal gain formula
based on disjoint occurrence counts. Its score is the computed sum of lengths
of all defining rows and all normalized templates. Increasing length is allowed
as an intermediate candidate; a later removal can repay it.

## 2. The finite prefix graph is a sound bounded candidate generator

`exchange_templates.py` collects prefixes of each cyclic orientation of every
input row, together with prefixes of both signs of each definition. These
are finitely many vertices of the old free group's Cayley tree. Existing tree
edges are labelled by old generators. A tested edge from `u` to the free
reduction of `u w_i` receives helper label `z_i`, and its reverse receives
`z_i^-1`, when both endpoints are in the vertex set. A shortest graph path
from the empty word to a target orientation gives a template with an exact
expansion witness.

The graph can omit useful cancellation states. Its BFS is shortest only in
the explicitly retained graph. Exhausting this graph's macro-edge scan is
not a proof of globally shortest dictionary representations. The next
construction removes this restriction for a fixed finite dictionary.

## 3. A finite complete geodesic algorithm for a fixed dictionary

The unweighted free-reduction automaton construction is standard: the
Benois theorem gives effective closure of rational languages under free
reduction. A primary-author reference is Bartholdi and Silva,
*Rational subsets of groups*, Section 3.2, Theorem 3.3,
[author manuscript](https://cmup.fc.up.pt/main/sites/default/files/publications/BS1.pdf),
[arXiv:1012.1532](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.1532).
The weighted and lexicographic claims needed here are proved below, rather
than attributed to that unweighted statement.

**Automaton.** Make one root state. For every signed dictionary token, attach
a private directed petal spelling its image in old letters, starting and
ending at the root. Include each old signed generator as a one-edge loop.
The first edge of a petal emits that signed token and carries its weight;
all other petal edges emit nothing and have weight zero. Every closed walk
at the root spells the expansion of exactly its emitted token word, with
the same additive cost. There is no change to the represented element.

For rank `r` and helper images of lengths `m_i`, the automaton has

\[
N=1+2\sum_i(m_i-1),\qquad E=2r+2\sum_i m_i
\]

states and letter edges. In particular, increasing the old rank adds loops,
not an exponential subset-of-alphabet enumeration.

**Freely trivial paths.** For every ordered state pair `(p,q)`, let `F(p,q)`
be the minimum cost of an automaton path whose old-letter label freely
reduces to the empty word. It is generated by these finite rules:

1. `F(p,p)` has an empty path of cost zero.
2. Concatenate a path for `F(p,s)` with one for `F(s,q)`.
3. If `p -a-> s` and `t -a^-1-> q` are letter edges, wrap a path for
   `F(s,t)` between those edges, adding their costs.

These rules are sound because each resulting path is freely trivial. They
are complete because a nonempty freely trivial word has a noncrossing
cancellation matching: its first letter is matched with some inverse
letter, the intervening subword reduces to the identity, and so does the
remaining suffix. Induct on the number of old letters, using wrapping for
the matched pair and concatenation for the suffix. This is a statement about
paths with their intermediate automaton states, not only about labels.

**Finite weighted settlement.** `exchange_v2_geodesic.py` processes tentative
state-pair costs in increasing order. When `(p,q)` is settled, combine it with
already settled compatible pairs and wrap it by inverse-labelled edges.
Never reconsider a settled destination pair. Every derived cost is the sum
of child costs and nonnegative edge costs, so every child is at most the
parent cost. If a settled value were not minimal, take a smallest derivation
of a cheaper path. Its children would already have been settled cheaply
enough, forcing an offer of that cheaper parent before the claimed settlement.
Zero-cost children do not invalidate this argument: either an equally priced
candidate is already correct, or the hypothetical improvement has strictly
smaller cost and all its children precede it. Identity cycles need not occur
in a minimum finite derivation.

There are only `N^2` pairs to settle. Concatenation and inverse-edge wrapping
give at most `2N^3+E^2` candidate deductions before pruning. Every witness is
stored as a **finite tuple of original edge IDs**, constructed from previously
available witnesses. There is no recursive pointer through an epsilon cycle.
These bounds concern deductions, not the cost of copying arbitrarily large
witnesses; no uniform short elementary AC expansion is inferred.

**Reading an exact target.** Given a reduced old word `a_1...a_l`, propagate
minimum-cost paths from the root through a freely trivial closure, an edge
labelled `a_i`, and another closure. End at the root. A path whose free
reduction is the target decomposes into the surviving letters in order,
separated by freely trivial subpaths; the preceding completeness proof
provides all such closures. Conversely any such accepted path freely reduces
to that target. Thus the returned minimum is the globally shortest token
word for this **exact** group element in the fixed dictionary. One target
query uses at most order `l(N^2+E)` closure/letter deductions after saturation.
The saturation is reused for all rows using the same dictionary and objective.

Completion claims are deliberately local. A fully completed saturation and
target read certify a geodesic for that exact target and dictionary. They
do not certify the best dictionary, best conjugate, best stable presentation,
or best stable rank. `exchange_v2_probe.py` chooses an orientation using a
literal score and dictionary-prefix overlap, then records its exact witness.
It does not exhaust the cyclic-orientation problem.

**Work truncation.** A bounded saturation retains only paths actually derived;
a bounded target read retains only valid accepted prefixes completed with
old letters. Either yields a freely verified candidate, with completion false.
The old target itself is always a fallback. Every performed candidate
deduction is counted. Exhausting the budget cannot create a spurious claim
of optimality. No rank or relator-length ceiling is imposed.

## 4. Optimize the removal condition, not just the template's length

To expose an old generator `g`, give a token the lexicographic cost

\[
(\mathbf 1_{\text{token is }g\text{ or }g^{-1}},\;1).
\]

Internal petal edges have cost `(0,0)`. The finite settlement argument above
still applies: lexicographic order on nonnegative integer pairs is a
well-order, is preserved by adding a nonnegative pair, and makes every child
no larger than its parent. All nonempty complete token walks have positive
second coordinate. Thus completion computes the minimum possible number of
old `g` letters, followed by the shortest template attaining that number.
If the result contains exactly one such letter, it supplies a Lemma 11 gate.
There is no appeal to exponent sums as a primitive test or to a free complement.

The objectives can genuinely differ. Define `z=a^3 b^3` and target `a^2 b`.
The ordinary shortest template is still `aab`, length 3 with two `a` letters.
The generator-count objective returns `A z B B`, length 4 with one `a`.
Its expansion freely reduces to `aab`. A zero-`a` representation is impossible
here since every other token has `a` exponent divisible by three, whereas
the target has `a` exponent two. The completed algorithm verifies that the
shortest representation with one `a` has length 4. The two computations
finish within 503 charged deductions each.

This row can occur in a known trivial presentation, for example
`(a^2 b,a^3 b^2)`: the first row gives `b=a^-2`, and the second then gives
`a^-1=1`. This is a proof of the input hypothesis, not a claim that the
exchange beats all simpler routes for that easy presentation.

## 5. A stable exchange across a whole-tuple Whitehead minimum

Let `c=[a,b]` and start with

\[
P=\langle a,b\mid abbAB,\;aabAB\rangle.
\]

These are respectively `c b c` and `a c`. The presentation is trivial:
the second equation gives `c=a^-1`, the first then gives `b=c^-2=a^2`,
and consequently `c=[a,a^2]=1`. Both `a` and `b` are therefore trivial.
Neither relator has a unique old-generator occurrence. The whole-tuple
minimum-cut Whitehead pass checks all four signed multipliers, finds no
strict descent, and completes at total length 10.

Define `z=c` and use the freely equal templates:

\[
(ZabAB,\;zbz,\;az).
\]

This rank-three tuple still has total length 10. Literal compression using
the same definition gives length 12, because it detects only one disjoint
use. The finite prefix graph discovers `zbz` automatically; the complete
weighted automaton also proves it shortest for its chosen exact orientation.

The `az` row permits `a=Z`. Substitute in the other two rows, retaining them:

\[
(ZZbzB,\;zbz).
\]

Their total length is 8. Now `zbz` permits `b=ZZ`; substitution into the
remaining defining row freely reduces it to `Z`. The rank history is
`2 -> 3 -> 2 -> 1 -> 0`, and the full boundary lengths are
`10 -> 10 -> 8 -> 1 -> 0`. All sign choices, expansions and removals are
replayed by the focused checks, and the independent verifier also replayed
the whole trace.

The construction generalizes algebraically: for any integers `p,q,r`, the
pair `(c^p b c^q, a c^r)`, expanded in `a,b`, presents the trivial group.
Its equations give `a=c^-r` and `b=c^-(p+q)`, hence `c=[a,b]=1`. Defining
`z=c` exposes those equations and the commutator of the resulting powers of
`z` reduces to the identity. The displayed case is the verified nonliteral
plateau control; no sweep over this infinite family is claimed.

## 6. Why a one-helper-use pivot must not be pruned

Suppose a compressed pivot contains exactly one old `g` and exactly one
helper `z`. Isolating it gives `g=alpha z^eta beta`, where `eta=±1` and
`alpha,beta` use only the untouched old generators. This is an invertible
ambient basis replacement `theta`, with `z` replacing `g`.

Write the same pivot as `T=A z^delta B`. After applying `theta`, its
defining equation is `Abar z^delta Bbar=1`. Let `wbar=theta(w)` and retain
`D=Z wbar`. Because the old pivot is the expansion of `T`, its ambient image
is `Abar wbar^delta Bbar`. Therefore:

\[
\theta(R)=
\begin{cases}
(\bar A z)D(\bar A z)^{-1},&\delta=+1,\\
\bar A D^{-1}\bar A^{-1},&\delta=-1.
\end{cases}
\]

Recorded inversion/conjugation of the original pivot is incorporated into
these same witnesses. Thus the retained defining row is indeed an ambient
image of the old pivot, up to explicit ordinary relator operations.

The **other rows** prevent a pure ambient-equivalence conclusion. If an old
row had template `T_j`, its ambient image is `T_j(theta(g),wbar)`, whereas
the actual exchange endpoint is `T_j(theta(g),z)`. They differ by substitutions
using the retained `D`, which are genuine ordinary AC moves. The complete
factorization is therefore:

1. Apply the verified ambient basis replacement to the whole old tuple.
2. Conjugate/invert the old pivot to obtain `D`.
3. Use `D` to make the exact substitutions in every other template.

This factorization can avoid the unspecified normal-product part of the
rank-removal lemma in that exchange description. It is an ambient map plus
explicitly realizable ordinary donor operations; it does not by itself
compile the ambient map into a same-rank elementary AC stream.

There is a concrete counterexample to pruning these exchanges at an ambient
minimum. Use the preceding known trivial `P`, still at its Whitehead minimum
of length 10. Define `z=bAB` and use templates `(abz,aaz)`, retaining `ZbAB`.
The pivot `abz` contains one `a` and one `z`. It gives `a=ZB`. Substituting
into both other rows gives

\[
(ZbbzB,\;ZBZBz).
\]

The second row cyclically reduces to `BZB`; the complete normalized endpoint
has total length **8**. Thus a one-helper-use pivot can produce a strict gain
from a whole-tuple ambient minimum. The extra donor substitutions, rather
than the basis replacement alone, supply the gain. Exact code-generated
words and witnesses are retained in the exchange controls; order in a
noncommutative isolating word must never be inferred from exponent arithmetic.

## 8. Derive a hidden definition from a root equation

Frequency-based candidates still guess the defining word before trying its
templates. There is a complementary direction: fix a useful template form
and solve for the defining word exactly. For contexts `U,V` in the current
basis and an integer `p>=2`, seek

\[
R=(wU)^{p-1}wV.
\]

This is equivalent in the free group to

\[
H=R V^{-1}U=(wU)^p.
\]

If `H=q^p`, define `w=qU^-1`; then the template `(zU)^(p-1)zV` expands
exactly to `R`. The identity permits all cancellations between helper images
and contexts. In particular it need not arise from literal repeated blocks
in the reduced word `R`.

All roots of a nonempty free-group word can be extracted without search over
possible root lengths or contents. Freely reduce and write
`H=c h c^-1`, where `h` is cyclically reduced. Write `h=t^k`, where `t` is
the shortest literal period. The valid positive exponents are precisely the
divisors `p>=2` of `k`, and their unique roots are
`q=c t^(k/p) c^-1`. The forward identity is immediate. Conversely, cyclic
reduction of a putative root shows that its power has exactly this periodic
cyclic core, proving that the divisor list is complete. The implementation
uses the linear prefix-function period algorithm and directly replays every
extracted power. The empty `H` case has only the identity root and is skipped;
it does not license enumeration of arbitrary nontrivial roots.

The motivating hidden word is reconstructed automatically. Take
`R=abbAB`, `U=b`, `V=1`. Then

\[
R b=abbA=(abA)^2,
\]

so `q=abA` and `w=qB=abAB=[a,b]`. The resulting pivot is `z b z`, which
contains exactly one old `b` and can remove it by Lemma 11. This derivation
does not rely on the commutator appearing in a repeated-word inventory.

`exchange_root.discover` tests supplied contexts and all signed cyclic row
orientations within a shared work budget. Its defaults use the empty word
and every signed current generator; longer contexts can be supplied. Every
actual root test, including failures, costs one unit. Inverse definitions
share one dictionary plan, while distinct pivot witnesses are retained up
to their canonical template. Plans favor an actual unique old-generator
occurrence in the proposed pivot and score its length together with the
new defining row.

`exchange_root.compile_plan` forces that proven pivot template while applying
the existing cancellation-aware graph to all other rows. This matters because
a shortest alternative template need not preserve the desired elimination
gate. It retains a complete root witness in the stable event and then returns
direct Lemma 11 removal candidates. Roots, forced template expansions,
other-row graph checks and removal attempts are all charged. There is no rank
or relator-length ceiling. Context and work restrictions make the plan
inventory finite, and an exhausted scan proves no stable obstruction.

`exchange_root_checks.py` passes six tests: all divisors of a conjugated sixth
power, absence of spurious commutator roots, hidden commutator reconstruction,
forced-pivot/all-row certificate replay, failed-test budgets, rank 11 with
longer supplied contexts, and corruption rejection. The planted pair yields
nine distinct defining-word plans in 333 root tests. Its bounded complete
probe uses 654 units and returns rank-two length-seven candidates. Those
numbers describe the planted control, not any U124 coverage result.

## 9. Opposite helper signs give a conjugacy equation

The complementary template `z U z^-1 V` expands to `R` exactly when

\[
H=R V^{-1}=wUw^{-1}.
\]

For nontrivial `U`, this equation is decidable by cyclic reduction, and its
entire solution set has an explicit form. Write `U=c u c^-1` and
`H=d h d^-1`, with cyclically reduced cores. A solution exists precisely when
`h` is a cyclic rotation of `u` with the same orientation. If
`u=s t` and `h=t s`, one solution is

\[
w_0=d s^{-1} c^{-1}.
\]

Let `t_U` be the primitive free-group root of `U`, including its conjugating
prefix `c`. Then **all** solutions are

\[
w=w_0 t_U^k,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Indeed, `w_0^-1 w` centralizes `U`. The centralizer of a nonidentity element
in a free group is generated by its primitive root: act on the Cayley tree,
where `U` has a unique translation axis. A commuting element preserves that
axis and its direction and acts by an integral translation. The kernel is
trivial because the free-group action fixes no vertex; the translation
subgroup is cyclic. Its shortest positive generator is exactly the primitive
root already extracted from the cyclic period. This also explains why a
noncyclic source needs its root conjugated back into its original coordinates.

The identity case `U=1` is excluded: its centralizer is the whole free group,
so describing all solutions by powers of a single root would be false.
Cyclic matching does not silently identify a word with its inverse. Every
candidate is tested by the exact equality `w U w^-1 V=R` after free reduction.

`exchange_conjugacy.py` uses finite supplied contexts (empty/signed singletons
by default) and declared shifts `k=0,1,-1`. It charges each actual conjugacy
test and each tested shift, including rejected short definitions. The formula
describes the entire centralizer coset, but the finite shift screen does not
claim to explore it exhaustively. Longer contexts and other finite shift
sets can be supplied without imposing a rank or relator-length ceiling.
The selected opposite-sign pivot is forced during compilation; other rows
are compressed and fully retained, then direct removal candidates are tested.

A checked control uses `w=[a,b]`, `R=w a w^-1 b`, and the pair `(R,aR)`.
It is a known trivial presentation: multiplying the second row by the inverse
of the first gives `a`, and setting `a=1` reduces the first row to `b`.
The input's full canonical length is 21. The conjugacy planner reconstructs
the hidden commutator definition and returns rank-two length-ten candidates
within 1000 charged units. This is a mechanism control, not evidence of
superiority over ordinary AC on this deliberately easy presentation.

Six focused tests check this hidden-definition witness, arbitrary rank and
large IDs, all retained rows and work sums, corrupted conjugators, rejection
of a nonprimitive alleged centralizer root, and noncyclic source coordinates.
In the last test `U=g_11 g_7^2 g_11^-1`, the correct centralizer generator is
`g_11 g_7 g_11^-1`, not `g_7`. Exact coset representatives at shifts
`-2,-1,0,1,2` all replay. These checks use no U124 census search.

Same-sign power equations and opposite-sign conjugacy equations provide two
principled ways to derive a definition from a requested elimination template.
They can feed later higher-rank trajectories, with every previous helper
included among the current generators and every previous defining row
included in the tuple. Their finite failures do not constitute algebraic
obstructions to other templates, context lengths, or coordinated dictionaries.

## 10. Equal-length templates can matter

The completed dictionary algorithm can minimize the additive objective

\[
(\text{number of template tokens},\text{number of old-basis tokens})
\]

lexicographically. This keeps shortest length as the first objective and,
among shortest templates, prefers more uses of the added helpers. Each token
edge has positive first coordinate, so the finite-state proof in Section 3
applies without a separate lexicographic ordering of token strings. This
objective is a deterministic choice among geodesics; it is not a theorem
that this choice has the best future Whitehead or AC reduction.

`exchange_v3_aliases.py` implements this objective using the frozen v2
automaton. It retains the separate elimination objective of v2 as a distinct
route. Completed queries certify optimality for the selected exact oriented
word, not for every conjugate or a future tuple. Truncated queries retain
valid witnesses but make no geodesic claim. The six focused v3 tests and the
independent audit cover neutral aliases, exhaustive tiny queries, work
accounting, full-row replay and repeated saturation.

The saved `aca95` rank-two source of total length 19 supplies a concrete
reason not to discard neutral aliases. With `z=[x,y]`, one length-eight row
has a freely equal length-eight template containing two helper tokens.
The v1 dictionary tuple has total length 18 and subsequently descends to 17
under a whole-tuple Whitehead map; literal compression gives a different
length-18 tuple that is already minimal under whole-tuple automorphisms.
On the selected v2 orientations, v3 instead finds row lengths 7 and 5;
including the length-five defining row gives 17 directly. This exact control
is saved in `exchange_v3_control.json`. It reconstructs a gain first found
by the v1 screen, rather than adding another newly improved census row.

## 11. Finite-index Schreier dictionaries

Choose an old generator `x` in an occurring basis of rank `r`, and an index
`d>=2`. The map to `Z/d` sends `x` to 1 and every other old generator `h`
to 0. Use the coset representatives `1,x,...,x^(d-1)` and the subgroup
dictionary

\[
a=x^d,\qquad b_{i,h}=x^i h x^{-i}\quad(0\leq i<d),
\qquad b_{0,h}=h.
\]

The new generators are introduced triangularly: first `a=x^d`, then
`b_(i,h)=x b_(i-1,h) x^-1` in increasing `i`. There are exactly
`m=1+(d-1)(r-1)` new generators and defining rows. Each new row has length
four except the power row, whose length is `d+1` before any normalization.
The familiar subgroup rank `d(r-1)+1` also follows directly by counting the
non-tree edges of this coset graph. No subgroup theorem is needed for the
legality of the stable transitions: the explicit free-word identities below
and the ordered definitions suffice.

At residue `i`, a letter `h^+-1` emits `b_(i,h)^+-1` without changing the
residue. A positive `x` emits nothing except at `i=d-1`, where it emits `a`;
a negative `x` emits nothing except at `i=0`, where it emits `a^-1`.
The next residue is updated modulo `d`. Every letter step satisfies

\[
x^i\ell=\operatorname{expand}(t)\,x^j.
\]

Multiplying these identities proves that a complete row `R` has the exact
free-group expansion `R=expand(T)x^s`, where `0<=s<d`. To select a negative
residue, use `x^s=a x^(s-d)`, appending `a` to `T`. The implementation chooses
the shorter signed residue, with the exponent sign breaking the `d/2` tie.
In particular, an exponent congruent to `+-1` yields a template containing
exactly one occurrence of old `x` (when its subgroup prefix does not cancel
it, which is automatic because that prefix contains no old `x`). This row
can eliminate `x` by Lemma 11. Counting every retained definition, the
resulting rank is

\[
r+m-1=d(r-1)+1.
\]

For rank two, index 10 therefore gives rank 11 after that single removal.
This is an exact rank calculation, not a claim that the resulting tuple has
minimal rank or length. Further easy removals may remain.

`exchange_schreier.py` compiles the dictionary as a sequence of existing
`defining_template_compression` events, one new helper per event. At each
stage it expands all not-yet-introduced helpers inside the desired final
templates. The next template thus expands, under the one current new
definition, to the previous stage's exact oriented row. Every previously
added defining relator remains a row. Canonicalization witnesses and tracked
row labels connect stages even when normalization permutes their order.
This proves each stage by inverse Lemma 11 and retains the complete tuple
for subsequent removal and replay. The first event also records every
Schreier letter transition, all full old-basis images and all triangular
definitions; the ordinary event records already independently suffice to
verify the stable path.

For input total length `L`, the declared dictionary charge is

\[
1+3L+r+m(r+1)+\frac{m(m-1)}2.
\]

This counts one plan check, two orientation-letter passes, all `L` Schreier
transitions, `r` full-row expansion checks, all `m` definitions and every
retained row's template expansion at every stage. A later removal costs one
more. An infeasible complete dictionary is rejected for one charged plan
check before allocating its helpers. This is a work-budget restriction, not
a fixed ceiling on rank, generator IDs or relator lengths. Individual work
units count candidate or identity checks, not CPU instructions or bytes.

The six planted traces in `exchange_schreier_control.json` use known trivial
pairs `(xy,Cy)`, where `C` is a product of commutators of powers of `x` with
`y`. The row `xy` forces `x=y^-1`, every commutator becomes trivial, and the
second row forces `y=1`. For `C=[x^(d-1),y]^4` the checked results are:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 2 / 15 | 4 / 16 | 3 / 12 | 56 |

| 10 | 2 / 63 | 12 / 56 | 11 / 50 | 268 |

The denser control `C=product_(k=1)^d [x^k,y]` uses every added helper in the

## 12. Choose one Schreier index and descend immediately

For each axis, invert rows with negative axis exponent and rotate at a
minimum prefix height. The resulting height walk is nonnegative. Record
each row's final exponent `e`, maximum prefix height `H`, non-axis letter
count, and signed axis transitions leaving each height. At index `d`, a
positive transition emits the power helper exactly when its starting height
is `d-1 mod d`; a negative transition does so exactly when it starts at
`0 mod d`. These counts and the centered residue give the unreduced
Schreier-template length without building its defining rows.

There is a finite data-derived saturation threshold

\[
D=\max(2,\max_R(H_R+1),2\max_R e_R).
\]

For every `d>=D`, no scanner transition crosses a multiple of `d`, and the
centered residue makes no power-helper correction. Consequently the power
helper occurs only in its own defining row. Every conjugate-letter helper
beyond the largest height used for its old letter occurs only in its own
row and possibly in the next triangular definition. Remove the power helper
and the unused tails in decreasing height order. The remaining tuple is
independent of `d`, with the same old letters and the same retained helper
labels. This proves that indices above `D` add no distinct effective
no-wrap dictionary after those explicit removals. It does not rule out
smaller indices: their power wraps trade more tokens against fewer defining
rows. For axes with row exponents only `0,+-1`, the exponent part of the
threshold is automatic once `d>=2`.

`exchange_schreier_descend.py` considers feasible indices `2..D` by default,
or an explicitly supplied finite index list. It charges one complete
height-profile check per axis/row and one score check per index. Its score
includes every raw defining relator, every raw template and the literal
one-occurrence removal estimate for a singleton-residue pivot. This is a
selection heuristic: normalization can change both row lengths and generator
degrees, so the score is not presented as a certified bound on the normalized
endpoint. The selected plan is built exactly once. Later planning work is
included when checking that the chosen dictionary still fits its reservation.

At a 1000-unit total budget, the default reserves 400 units for immediate
postprocessing. The wrapper tests all old-axis singleton pivots within the
shared remainder and takes the shortest exact resulting tuple, then
alternates whole-tuple Whitehead descent with nonincreasing Lemma-11 peeling.
It returns the resulting path after this descent rather than relying on a
length-prioritized frontier to revisit a long higher-rank intermediate.
Whitehead min-cuts, rejected removals, planner work and all dictionary work
are included in the returned total charge. `indices=(10,), axis=...` can
force a rank-growing plan while retaining the same work reservation.

The three repeated-commutator plants from Section 11 all reach the empty
tuple through forced indices 2, 3 and 10 at 98, 166 and 646 total units,
respectively. The corresponding intermediate ranks after the old-axis
exchange are 3, 4 and 11. The index-10 plant's automatic choice is index 5,
which also solves it. Five focused tests independently replay the complete
paths, verify raw token forecasts, enforce tiny budgets, and check that
indices 3, 5 and 10 on one no-wrap source reduce to exactly equal tuples
after deleting only the unused power and tail definitions. Full forced
traces are in `exchange_schreier_descend_control.json`. These remain planted
mechanism controls, not new census solves.

This route is related to fixed-dictionary compression, but its cost depends
on coordinated definitions. Expanding every `b_(i,h)` directly into old
letters would give total defining length

\[
d+1+(r-1)(d-1)(d+2).
\]

The triangular definitions instead cost `d+1+4(d-1)(r-1)`, a saving of
`(r-1)(d-1)(d-2)` in the full defining tuple. At rank two and index 10,
this is 119 versus 47 defining letters. Every row is still retained; the
saving comes from the exact reuse of earlier helpers. Some helpers may be
used only once in an original row yet participate in this coordinated chain,
so literal-frequency filtering alone would miss the construction.

## 13. Exact collection with commutator corrections

Fix an axis `g`. For each other current generator `h`, let `c_(s,0,h)=h`
and introduce only the needed finite initial segments of the two chains

\[
c_{s,j+1,h}=[g^s,c_{s,j,h}]
=g^s c_{s,j,h}g^{-s}c_{s,j,h}^{-1},\qquad s\in\{+1,-1\}.
\]

Every new generator has its own retained defining relator of length five.
Writing `phi_s(u)=g^s u g^-s`, the defining identity is exactly
`phi_s(c_(s,j,h))=c_(s,j+1,h)c_(s,j,h)`. Repeatedly applying this identity
expresses `g^k h g^-k` in the chain for `s=sign(k)`. The exact token word
has `2^abs(k)` letters before reduction, and its construction uses
`2^abs(k)-1` elementary replacement steps. For example,

\[
g h g^{-1}=c_1h,\qquad
g^2h g^{-2}=c_2c_1c_1h.
\]

There is no commutativity assumption among these tokens; their order is
retained literally. For `h^-1`, invert the entire word, including its order.
Both signed chains are needed because a negative conjugation cannot in
general be expressed by truncating a positive chain.

If an oriented row has axis exponent `e` and its non-axis letters occur at
prefix heights `k_i`, telescoping gives either exact frame

\[
R=\left(\prod_i g^{k_i}h_i^{\epsilon_i}g^{-k_i}\right)g^e
=g^e\left(\prod_i g^{k_i-e}h_i^{\epsilon_i}g^{-(k_i-e)}\right).
\]

`exchange_collect.py` chooses the cheaper frame by its explicit replacement
count, or accepts a forced left/right frame. It inverts negative-exponent
rows and records its cyclic rotation. The right frame rotates at the first
minimum height, while the left frame uses the last minimum; each choice
has an exact conjugation witness. Frame selection is a bounded heuristic,
not optimization over all cyclic representatives or all shared dictionaries.
When `e=1`, the resulting template has exactly one old `g` and immediately
permits a Lemma-11 removal.

The definition inventory consists of every correction needed by the chosen
frames, including all earlier members of its signed chain. Before creating
any large word, the implementation checks the total replacement count
`S=sum_i(2^abs(k_i)-1)` (with shifted heights for a left frame) against the
remaining work. Oversized plans are rejected, not truncated. No assertion
sets a higher commutator to the identity. Each retained row, including all
earlier helper definitions, then passes through a sequence of one-helper
`defining_template_compression` events. These events retain complete free
expansions, signed frames, individual conjugate chunks and normalization
witnesses. Thus the proof uses exactly the same inverse-Lemma-11 argument
as the other coordinated dictionaries, with no nilpotent quotient assumption.

For `m` helpers, rank `r`, total input length `L` and `f` tested frames per
row (`f=2` for the default and `f=1` for a forced side), the accepted
dictionary charge is

\[
1+3fL+S+r+m(r+1)+m(m-1)/2.
\]

The probe additionally counts each axis forecast, every forced-removal
attempt and all subsequent Whitehead min-cuts and peeling attempts. Like
the Schreier wrapper, it reserves 400 units at a total budget of 1000 and
descends immediately after its selected old-axis exchange.

On the Section-5 input, the chosen frames recover the one-helper dictionary
of total length 10, then reach the empty tuple at 85 total charged units.
A larger known-trivial control starts from the four rows

\[
(g h_2,\;[g^3,h_2]^2h_2,\;[g^3,h_3]^2h_3,
\;[g^2,h_4]^2h_4).
\]

The first two rows force `h_2=g=1`, after which the other two force
`h_3=h_4=1`. Its normalized initial length is 27. The collector retains
eight corrections at chain depths 3, 3 and 2, giving rank 12 and length 79;
the old-axis removal gives rank 11 and length 73. Whole-tuple Whitehead
descent and peeling then reach the empty tuple within 612 total units.
This is a verified long higher-rank detour, not a claim that the planted
presentation needs that detour. Both full traces are saved in
`exchange_collect_control.json`. Six focused tests cover signed chains,
all retained rows, arbitrary/gapped large generator IDs, tiny budgets,
left/right differences and rejection before a large-height expansion.

The relation to Schreier generators must be stated at the tuple level.
For a positive chain write `y_i=g^i h g^-i`. Inductively,
`c_j=y_j W_j(y_0,...,y_(j-1))`, so the finite change between matching helper
inventories is triangular and hence a Nielsen automorphism fixing the old
basis. However, transforming the short Schreier defining rows by this map
does not immediately give the short commutator defining rows. Already at
level two, substituting

\[
y_1=c_1h,\qquad y_2=c_2c_1^2h
\]

into `y_2^-1 g y_1 g^-1` gives
`h^-1 c_1^-2 c_2^-1 g c_1 h g^-1`. Obtaining the short row
`c_2^-1[g,c_1]` uses the earlier defining row to replace `g h g^-1` by
`c_1 h`, followed by free reduction and cyclic conjugation. At higher
levels the same induction uses lower defining donors. Negative chains have
the identical argument with `g^-1` in place of `g`.

Thus the direct proven factorization is a triangular ambient helper change
**plus ordinary donor operations on the defining rows**. No pure ambient
equivalence of the complete tuples is claimed. This collector supplies
different exact finite-budget coordinates and donor representatives; it is
not a new stable-equivalence principle beyond the definitions, substitutions,
ambient maps and Lemma-11 removals already under consideration.

failure to improve the initial length. Its summary is retained in
`exchange_collect_rank11_countercontrol.json`; unlike the two preceding
controls, its full path was not serialized or independently audited, so it

confused with a guarantee that a high-rank excursion helps.

## 15. A first-helper collector without unused higher corrections

The useful prefixes admit a direct linear formula. Put `q=g^s`, `b=h^t`
for signed old generators, and introduce only `c=[q,b]`. Then

\[
q^n b q^{-n}=
\begin{cases}
q^{n-1}c(q^{-1}c)^{n-1}b,&n>0,\\
b,&n=0,\\
q^n C(qC)^{-n-1}q b,&n<0.
\end{cases}
\]

The positive formula follows by expanding the telescoping product of the
conjugates of `c`; the negative formula follows in the same way from
`q^-1 b q=(q^-1 C q)b`. Direct free expansion verifies both. Inverting the
entire expression handles `b^-1`. The token count is linear in `abs(n)`:
`3n-1` for positive `n` and `3abs(n)+1` for negative `n`. Consequently no
higher commutator inventory, exponential expansion or higher-chain
preflight is needed to construct this first-helper tuple.

`exchange_collect_prefix.py` uses these formulas inside the same exact left
and right row frames. It tests signed choices of both old generators and
two modes: substitute at all heights, or leave negative-height conjugates
literal. Each old row chooses a shortest canonical template among its two
tested frames, breaking length ties toward more helper tokens. This is a
local finite choice, not a global dictionary-geodesic claim. Every result
contains exactly one new defining row and all original rows. The probe
deduplicates exact tuples, reserves work, and immediately applies Whitehead
descent and nonincreasing removal to a few shortest distinct candidates.
It does not force an old-axis removal when the useful result is already the
first-helper prefix.

The declared compression charge is `2+(f+1)r`: one plan check, one
definition, `f` full frame/identity checks per old row, and one retained-row
expansion check per row. These are formula/row checks rather than the
individual replacement-step units of the full collector. The probe counts
every rejected plan, all tested signed choices and all postprocessing in
the shared work total. Raw CPU comparisons must therefore retain these
different declared unit definitions. Plans are generated lazily, with no
rank, generator-ID or relator-length ceiling.

Six focused tests verify all 72 signed conjugate cases at heights `-4..4`,
linear expansions at heights `+-100`, arbitrary rank and large gapped IDs,
tiny budgets, exact replay, and the saved `aca_43` prefix from length 18 to
16 at eight declared checks. On the planted hidden-commutator example the
prefix arm also finds a rank-three length-nine dictionary and reaches the
empty tuple within 167 total units. These controls do not rerun the saved

Here is an explicit bridge in the saved canonical row frames. The donor
is row zero and remains unchanged. The target begins at

\[
R=S^{-1}=B B A A A b A
\]

and ends at `V=T^-1=B A c A c A c A`. Right-multiply it by the following
three donor conjugates, in order:

\[
R\ \longmapsto\ R\prod_{i=1}^3q_i^{-1}D^{-1}q_i,
\qquad
q_1=B A A b A,\quad q_2=B A b A,\quad q_3=A.
\]

Exact free reduction gives `V`. The target lengths are `7 -> 8 -> 9 -> 8`;
normalizing the full tuple gives precisely the length-22 catalog endpoint.

multiplying the target, and restoring the donor. These are ordinary relator
AC operations; no removal or ambient map is needed for this bridge.

For completeness, the factor construction is a general exact identity, not
a guess based on cancellation. Let `E` expand the helper and let `V` be a
word with `E(V)=R`. For a helper letter at position `i`, let `P_i` be the
expanded prefix before it. The telescoping product for `V R^-1` has one
factor for each helper letter. A positive `c` contributes

\[
(P_iw)D^{-1}(P_iw)^{-1},\qquad w=E(c),
\]

and a negative `C` contributes `P_i D P_i^-1`. Old-letter factors are the
identity. To turn this left product into right donor multiplications of
`R`, use `q_i=(P_iw)^{-1}R` in the positive case and `q_i=P_i^{-1}R` in the
negative case, freely reducing these words. This derives exactly the three
conjugators above. It also explains why an alias change is additional donor
work even when the dictionary itself is fixed: it need not preserve the
ambient-automorphism orbit of the complete relator tuple.

## 18. The general 4r bound is sharp, but a donor move escapes it

The parent-owned `RANK_LENGTH_BOUND.md` proves that a balanced unimodular
tuple with no nonincreasing one-occurrence removal has `L >= 4r`. Its proof
also provides a constructive shortcut below this boundary. If `L < 4r`,
some unique-occurrence pivot already has the nonpositive literal bound

\[
(d(g)-2)(\ell-2)-2\leq0.
\]

Scanning row occurrence counts and global degrees finds such a pivot before
any full substitutions are constructed. Cancellation-sensitive testing is
unnecessary to establish existence below this boundary. At equality, every
inequality in that proof must be tight. Writing `p` for the number of
length-three rows, there must be exactly `p` length-five rows and exactly
`r-2p` length-four rows. The column degrees have the same counts: `p` each
of degrees three and five, and `r-2p` of degree four. Moreover `p>=1`, since
all-even row lengths would make the exponent matrix singular modulo two.
These are useful restrictions on equality cases, not additional search caps.

The bound is attained by a small known-trivial example. With `C=[g,h]`, take

\[
P=(g u^2,\quad h v^2,\quad u C^{-1},\quad v C).
\]

Its rank is four and its total length is 16. On columns `(g,h,u,v)`, its
exponent matrix is upper triangular with diagonal `(1,1,1,1)`. Triviality
also follows directly from the actual relations, not just this determinant:
`u=C`, `v=C^-1`, `g=u^-2` and `h=u^2`, so `g` and `h` commute; hence `C=1`
and all four generators are trivial.

There are exactly four unique-occurrence pivots. Removing `g` or `h` leaves
cyclic row lengths `(3,7,7)`. Removing `u` or `v` leaves `(3,5,9)`. Thus every
such removal increases length from 16 to 17. Each word is freely and
cyclically reduced at the stated length. In particular the rank-three
strengthening to `L>=13` cannot be extended to a strict `L>4r` bound in all
ranks.

One ordinary donor operation immediately escapes. Conjugate the retained
row `vC` by `v`, making `Cv`, and right-multiply `uC^-1` by it. The target
becomes `uv`, while the donor is restored. This drops total length to 13.
The subsequent exact removals of `v,g,h,u` have boundaries

\[
(4,16)\longrightarrow(4,13)\longrightarrow(3,11)
\longrightarrow(2,10)\longrightarrow(1,1)\longrightarrow(0,0).
\]

The initial four failed removals, the ordinary normal-product factor and
all successful removal witnesses are saved in `exchange_rank4_sharp.json`.
The construction uses sparse generator labels including values above
`10^20`; nine explicit candidate checks suffice. The first donor event
verifies without any known-triviality premise, and the later Lemma-11
interpretation uses the direct triviality proof above. This is a sharp
removal bound with a short constructive escape, not an AC counterexample.

Disjoint copies on disjoint alphabets give the same sharp bound at every
rank divisible by four: rank `4m`, length `16m`, known triviality, and every
one-occurrence removal increases total length by one in its own block.
The ordinary donor escape remains available separately in each block.

The same rank-four tuple is also minimal under every ambient automorphism:
all eight signed Whitehead minimum cuts have capacity equal to their vertex
degree. The saved cut data is `exchange_rank4_sharp_aut.json`; the separate
`exchange_rank4_sharp_flows.json` makes this assertion hand-checkable. In the
Whitehead multigraph, the following unit-path families join each positive
letter to its inverse:

| source/sink | capacity-respecting unit paths | total value |
|---|---|---|
| `g,G` | `g U G`; twice `g h G`; `g H u G`; `g H v G` | 5 |
| `h,H` | `h V H`; twice `h g H`; `h G u H`; `h G v H` | 5 |
| `u,U` | `u U`; `u G U`; `u H g U` | 3 |
| `v,V` | `v V`; `v H V`; `v G h V` | 3 |

Within each family, no edge is used more often than its multiplicity. Hence
every separating cut has at least the displayed capacity. The singleton
cut attains that value, which is the corresponding degree. Reversing the
paths treats the four negative multipliers. Thus all Whitehead length
deltas are nonnegative, and Whitehead's length-reduction theorem certifies
global ambient minimality. The explicit donor multiplication therefore
escapes both ambient length minimality and the one-occurrence removal
barrier. Disjoint copies preserve this additional property because their
Whitehead graphs have disjoint vertex sets.

## 19. Retaining high rank while shortening individual relators

The user's later objective values short individual rows even when total
length grows. For that objective, rank reduction is not an acceptance
criterion. A useful tuple order is

\[
(\max_i|R_i|,\quad \#\{i:|R_i|\geq3\},\quad \sum_i|R_i|).
\]

No peeling is required after a successful short-row construction. The
following dictionary and flip operations retain every defining row.

Independent prefix triangulation uses
`E=sum_i max(0,|R_i|-3)` binary helpers on the original rows. A shared binary
dictionary can use fewer. Choose a two-token word `ab`, introduce one fresh
helper `z=ab` with triangle `Zab`, and replace disjoint occurrences of `ab`
or `BA` in signed cyclic representatives of the original rows. Keep all
previous defining triangles unchanged. New definitions reference only old
tokens, so this is an acyclic straight-line dictionary with exact expansion
in the original basis. The source-row rotations and expansions provide the
usual full defining-template certificate.

Let `Delta E` be the actual reduction of excess length on these original
rows after a step. A pair that replaces two useful occurrences can have
`Delta E>=2` while costing one helper. If the process ends with excess zero,
its exact helper count is

\[
H=E_{\rm initial}-\sum_{\rm steps}(\Delta E-1).
\]

Choosing a pair from a row longer than three always permits positive excess
reduction, so the construction needs no more helpers than independent
prefix chains. Shared occurrences can improve this count. This is an exact
finite compression policy, not a claim of optimal grammar size.

For a known-trivial example take `(ababa,aba)`. The second row gives
`b=a^-2`; substituting in the first gives `a^-1=1`. Independent prefix
triangulation of the length-five row needs two helpers. Sharing `z=ab`
instead gives the full tuple `(zza,za,Zab)`: one helper, rank three, maximum
row length three and total length eight. The retained definition is counted.
Its exact event is included in `exchange_triangle_flip_control.json`.

There is also an exact local diagonal flip. Suppose the generator `z`
appears only once in each of two triangular rows, which can be oriented as

\[
(Zab,\quad zcd),
\]

where the signed old letters `a,b,c,d` avoid `z`. Apply the ambient
Nielsen automorphism

\[
z\mapsto azC,\qquad z\mapsto Azc\text{ for its inverse},
\]

fixing every other generator. The two rows become `cZb` and `azd`; cyclic
conjugation gives `(Zbc,azd)`. Every other row is fixed. Thus rank is retained
and the macro's endpoints still have all row lengths at most three. If
`c=B`, the new defining row is the unit `Z`. This is a useful diagonal
change rather than a destabilization.

The endpoint qualification matters: splitting the map into its two
single-sided Nielsen factors may pass through a length-four row. No claim
is made that every elementary intermediate has maximum length three. The
combined map and its inverse are saved and verified on the complete tuple.
