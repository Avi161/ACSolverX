# Independent low-length proof audit

PASS for both statements, with a nonempty freely and cyclically reduced balanced tuple on its occurring basis and determinant ±1. The statements concern literal one-occurrence substitution and cyclic length. Known triviality is an additional premise when interpreting a removal through Lemma 11.

The general bound is **L ≥ 4r** if every singleton removal strictly increases length. Every generator has degree at least three and every row length at least three. A length-three row has its odd exponent support entirely in degree-at-least-five columns: a triple-only row has determinant divisible by three, while a singleton of degree at most four has elimination bound Δ ≤ d−4 ≤ 0. A length-four row has its odd support entirely in degree-at-least-four columns. An odd count is one or three. A singleton of degree at most three has Δ ≤ 2d−6 ≤ 0. If a triple had total degree three, all three occurrences would lie in this row; with only one other letter, cyclic reduction forces the triple to have one sign. The entire exponent column would then be divisible by three.

Let p and q count the length-three and length-four rows. Independence of every subset of exponent rows over F₂ forces at least p degree-at-least-five columns and at least p+q degree-at-least-four columns. Counting occurrences gives L ≥ 3r+2p+q. Counting row lengths gives L ≥ 5r−2p−q. Adding proves 2L ≥ 8r. The two high-degree column sets are nested, so the extra contributions p and p+q are legitimate. Rank one and short rows are already removed by the preliminary argument; the empty tuple is excluded.

The stronger rank-three bound **L ≥ 13** also passes. With L ≤ 12, the preliminary argument reduces the only potentially irreducible tuple to row lengths (3,4,4) or (3,4,5), with the first row g h² and degree of g at least five. Two length-three rows either force total degree at least thirteen or give dependent rows modulo two. Three length-four rows all annihilate the all-ones column modulo two. Length eleven has the degree-three h singleton in a four-row and is immediately removable.

At length twelve the only degree vectors are (6,3,3), (5,3,4), and (5,4,3). Independent enumeration of the unsigned four-row count vectors, subject only to available counts, parity and the elementary singleton bound, gives seven possibilities. Two have all three k occurrences in a four-row and fail the whole-column divisibility-by-three test. The remaining five are exactly the five printed cases. The first of these is eliminated by the same divisibility argument. Thus no unsigned case was omitted.

The determinant formulas and all forced sign implications pass independent scalar checks. The cyclic separation argument in the (3,1,1) five-row forces precisely g² h G k^ε or g² k^ε G h. In both forms solving for h sends g h² to cyclic length three. For the opposite-k four-row, h is a conjugate of G; the two h copies in the first row share cancelling conjugators, giving the claimed 5+7 bound. The simultaneous inversion of g and h preserves the normalized first row after its own inversion and rotation. These transformations preserve both singleton existence and removal length.

The later rank-four sharpness example also passes independently. With C=[g,h], take (g u², h v², u C⁻¹, v C). It is cyclically reduced, has determinant1 and total length16.
