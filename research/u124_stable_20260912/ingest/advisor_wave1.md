# Wave 1 stable-AC legality audit

## BLOCKERS

1. **The 14/14 artifact is not an elementary stable-AC certificate and does
   not solve a U124 row.** It calls `cov.cov_branches`, then compares words.
   It contains no AC1--AC5 replay, no normal-closure witnesses for Lemma 11,
   and no terminal path to the standard pair
   (`tables/ms_template_identities.json`; `code/ms_template_identities.py`).
2. **Prop A is non-effective.** It is a legal *existence* argument if Lemma 11
   is accepted, but its phrases “transform \(z\) to \(z^{-1}w\)” and “remove
   the isolator” must be read as inverse/forward uses of Lemma 11, not as
   AC4/AC5 or as a free Tietze move. Lemma 11 expresses \(w\) as a product of
   \(m\) conjugates of the other relators; \(m\) is finite but unbounded and
   no expression is supplied. Therefore Prop A licenses stable equivalence,
   not a bounded move, move count, or replayable certificate
   (`MU_CRITERION.md`; `LEMMA_11_AND_THE_126_CLASSES.md` §4).

**A. Verdict on Prop A.** No Tietze move is smuggled if S1 is explicitly the
reverse of Lemma 11 and S3--S4 are its forward use. A Tietze observation may
show that the extended presentation is trivial, but cannot itself establish
stable-AC equivalence. Calling either use an elementary stabilization or
charging it linearly would be wrong; the unbounded-\(m\) issue is load-bearing.

## WARNINGS

**B. Trivial-group hypothesis.** Yes: these are known trivial-group
presentations, not presentations of unknown groups. The local source states
that the Miller--Schupp family consists of balanced presentations of the
trivial group (`LEMMA_11_AND_THE_126_CLASSES.md` §4, especially its
“What it needs” paragraph). `data/ms_unsolved_reps/README_aca_124.md` traces
U124 to the 550 unsolved Miller--Schupp cells, while `RECONSTRUCTION.md` and
`THEOREM_CATALOGUE.md` record the subsequent ACA/stable reductions. “Unsolved”
means AC status, not group triviality. Thus Lemma 11's hypothesis is available.
For a proof-grade per-row bundle, however, retain the row-to-MS/ACA provenance;
the campaign files assert this provenance but do not contain 550 elementary
raw-cell-to-representative replays.

**Signs omitted in Prop A.** With \(D=z^{-1}w\), a positive occurrence uses
\((uwv)(v^{-1}D^{-1}v)=uzv\). An inverse occurrence must instead use the
cyclic conjugate \(wz^{-1}\) of \(D\):
\((uw^{-1}v)(v^{-1}wz^{-1}v)=uz^{-1}v\). Likewise, an isolator
\(a^{-1}q\) already defines \(a=q\), whereas \(aq\) must be inverted **and
cyclically rotated** to \(a^{-1}q^{-1}\). `cov.py` handles both signs, but
`MU_CRITERION.md` does not spell them out.

**D. The \(n+12\) statement.** The substitution
\(y\mapsto x^{-2}y\) is an \(\operatorname{Aut}(F_2)\) fact (inverse
\(y\mapsto x^2y\)). The displayed cyclic length is exactly \(n+12\) in the
14 checked cases, and exact `aut_min_len` there says
\(\mu(Q_{n,\delta})=n+12\). The all-\(n\) proposition proves only
\(\mu(Q_{n,\delta})\le n+12\); its claimed exact floor is merely checked on
finite ranges (`MS_TEMPLATE_PROPOSITION.md`; the JSON). If the transformed
pair is used in a stable-AC derivation, the automorphism **must** be realized
or cited via the stable ambient automorphism theorem and the trivial-group
hypothesis. It need not be realized merely to compute an Aut-orbit length.
The unstable pairwise ambient principle is not a theorem
(`ambient-principle-unstable-is-not-a-theorem.md`).

**E. Rank-3/cyclic-complement audit.** I found no fatal illegal move in the
core constructions, but two proof-level omissions should be made explicit.
In Theorem 3.1, “up to rotation and inversion” requires actual AC3/AC1
preconditioning before choosing the displayed spelling; in the positive
isolator case, inversion must be followed by cyclic rotation. Free expansion
alone is harmless. In the cyclic-complement proof, transporting every move
through \(\theta\) is legal because it transports an AC path; it is not an
ambient automorphism asserted as a row move. Prefixing Nielsen **row** moves,
clearing \(t\) with the literal \(t\)-row, and then AC5 are legal. The statement
should also record why its hypotheses imply normal generation: modulo
\(\langle\!\langle A,B\rangle\!\rangle\), the group is generated by \(c\), so
is cyclic, and unimodularity makes its abelianization trivial. Applications
still need explicit Nielsen/Stallings witnesses. Failure of a cyclic
complement is not an obstruction; the AK(2) control in the same file proves
that warning.

## ALLOWED CLAIMS

**C.** The artifact establishes the following, and no more:

- For \(n=2,\ldots,8\) and \(\delta=\pm1\), the implementation's two selected
  CoV branches produce the literal displayed \(Q_{n,\delta}\): 14/14
  free-group identity checks pass.
- For trivial-group inputs, Prop A conditionally upgrades each checked CoV hop
  to existential stable-AC equivalence. This is properly called a
  **structurally justified stable CoV**, not an elementary replay.
- The specific automorphic coordinates have cyclic total \(n+12\), and the
  exact Whitehead computation reports the same orbit minimum in those 14
  cases.
- Eleven archival U124 rows match the tested \(P_{n,\delta}\) family up to the
  ordinary legal conventions recorded in the JSON.

There is **no** ordinary-AC equivalence, elementary stable certificate, or
U124 trivialization here. The relevant U124 endpoints have \(\mu\ge14\), so
the \(\mu\le12\) criterion does not fire.

**F. What must not be claimed next.** Do not claim “U124 solved/removed,”
ordinary AC, a replayable AC1--AC5 certificate, bounded/linear Lemma-11 cost,
an exact all-\(n\) floor \(n+12\), a \(\mu=13\) removal, the unstable ambient
automorphism principle, or an obstruction from failed CoV/corridor/complement
search. Also do not call 124 the exact number of classes; it is an upper bound.
