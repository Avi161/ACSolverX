## 1. What a *family* is

A **family** is a set of census rows that share a relator up to the group
generated by cyclic rotation, relator inversion and the eight signed
permutations of `{x, y}` (`words.SIGNED_PERMS`).  `FAMILY_mine.orbit_key(R) =
min_phi canon_rel(phi(R))` is the canonical representative of that orbit;
`canon_rel` already quotients rotation and inversion, so equality of keys is a
complete invariant of the orbit.

*Checked* (`FAMILY_verify` §A): for 155 words — both relators of all 41 rows
plus a stride sample of the census — `orbit_key` is constant over all
8 signed permutations x inversion x every rotation.  0 failures.

Each family member is put in the **family frame**: apply the signed permutation
that maps its family relator onto the orbit key and take the canonical form of
the other relator, choosing the permutation that makes that companion
lexicographically least.  So every row of a family reads as `(R, W)` with the
same `R`, and the companions `W` are comparable across rows.

The 41 rows have **19 distinct first-relator orbits**.  Row `ac19_56260 =
(YXyXYXyxx, YYXyxYXXyx)` carries *two* of them, so the 41 rows account for 48
family memberships.

## 3. The first surprise: for these families there is almost no census "path" to mine

The brief asks which relator is used as donor first, what the common 2-4 step
prefixes are, and whether paths funnel through hubs.  The honest answer for
these 19 families is that **90 % of the stored certificates are not search
discoveries at all** — they are the backward ball's stored tail, emitted after
a single root lookup at 0 charged units.  The "prefix" of such a certificate is
a property of the offline BFS table, not of the heuristic.

Two readings matter.

* **The family relator is destroyed almost at once.**  The median number of
  moves before `R` stops being one of the two relators is 1 or 2 in 17 of the
  19 families (4 in the other two).  In `YXYxxYXyxYXyx` and `YXYxYXXYXyx`
  *every* solved member's first move rewrites `R` itself.  This is the same
  phenomenon `STALLED_BS_THEORY.md` §5.3 records for the BS family ("every one
  of the 584 solved stalled paths modifies `R`"), and it is the reason section
  7 below reports a negative result for `R`-preserving macros.
* **Companion length does not separate solved from unsolved.**  In
  `YYXXXXYX` the one unsolved companion has `|W| = 9` while solved members go
  up to 13; in `YXXYxYxx` the unsolved companion is the *shortest* class of all
  (`|W| = 9`, solved median 11).  Length is a weak signal at best.

## 4. What *does* separate them: membership in the backward ball

The cap-12 automorphism-closed ball `B(12)` is the set of canonical pairs from
which an engine path to `(x, y)` exists **with every intermediate relator of
length at most 12**.  Every solved family member outside the 341 search solves
is in it; **not one of the 41 residual roots is** (that is exactly what
`ball_root` failing means).

The interesting half is that this is not merely a length effect.  Seventeen of
the 41 roots have both relators of length `<= 12` — `ac19_11753`
`(YYXXYxYXX, YYXXyXYYx)` has `(9, 9)` — and are still outside `B(12)`.  For
those rows, *every* engine path to the trivial pair must at some point push a
relator past length 12.  The 5,000-unit certificates confirm how far past: the
paths for the `YXYXyXXYXyX` family climb to relators of length 17-20 before
descending (section 5).

That reframes the residual: it is not a family of pairs that are far from the
trivial pair, it is a family of pairs whose only routes go **uphill** first,
which is precisely what an `L + 20 S + 2 MK` best-first order pays for last.

## 5. Hubs: the 5,000-unit certificates do funnel

Running the shipped policy `K3p_c12aut` at 5,000 units (five times its census
allowance) solves 26 of the 41 and gives, for the first time, real search paths
for these families.  Intersecting the state sets of the solved members of a
family:

| family | rows solved at 5,000 | merge | common suffix | hub state (lengths) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `YXYXyXXYXyX` | 5 of 5 | `45056` and `62816` agree from their step 1; `65967` joins at its step 4, `66543` at its step 10; `54514` never joins | 45-47 steps | `('YYYYXYYYXyxyxyyyX', 'YYYXYYYXYXYYXYYYXYX')` (17, 19) |
| `YYXXYXYYXXX` | 2 of 2 | after 2 / 3 moves | 36 steps | `('YYYXYXYYXYYYXYXYX', 'YYYYXYXYYYXYXYYYXYXX')` (17, 20) |
| `YYXXYxYxyX` | 2 of 2 | `ac19_58570`'s root is the state `ac19_49255` reaches in 1 move | 50 steps | `('YYXXYxYxyX', 'YXYxyXYxxyX')` (10, 11) |
| `YYXyxYXXyx` | 4 of 5 | `57787` and `65876` merge after 1 / 3 moves; `6554`, `17417` do not join them | 33 steps | `('YXyxYXXyxYx', 'YXyXyXyXYxyxx')` (11, 13) |

So the funnel is real but *local*: members of a family reach a common state in
1-10 moves and then share a long tail — but the hub sits at relator length
17-20, well outside anything the 1,000-unit search reaches under an
`L`-dominated order, and it is not shared across the whole family (`ac19_54514`
leaves its family's hub alone).

## 6. Magnus frames of the family groups

For `G = <x, y | R>` the Magnus/HNN decomposition needs a generator whose
exponent sum in `R` is **zero**: that generator is the stable letter `t`, and
the base is generated by the conjugates `b_i = t^i b t^-i` of the other
generator (checked against `BS(m, m+1) = b^-1 a^m b a^-(m+1)`, where `exp_b = 0`
and `b` is the stable letter — `STALLED_BS_THEORY.md` §1).

When neither exponent sum is zero, one ambient Nielsen shear (`y -> y x^k` or
`x -> x y^m`) can put one of them at zero exactly when one exponent sum divides
the other; that shear is transport (`primitive_patterns.md` §2), not an AC
move.  13 of the 19 families admit such a shear; the last column of the section
2 table records `t`, the transported relator length, the index range `mu..nu`
of the rewriting, and the resulting structure.  `FAMILY_mine.magnus_frame`
computes it and `FAMILY_mine.magnus_word` re-expands the rewriting and checks
it reproduces the transported relator (19/19 where a frame exists).

The three relators named in the brief are worked out in
[`FAMILY_THEORY.md`](FAMILY_THEORY.md) §5; the shape of the answer is that
seven families are mapping tori `F_n ⋊ Z` of a free-group automorphism, four
are strictly ascending HNN extensions of a free group, two have a genuine
one-relator base with cyclic edge groups, and six admit no single-shear frame.

## 7. The `R`-preserving neighbourhood, and where the residual sits in it

An **`R`-preserving move** is a Definition 2.1 substitution that rewrites only
the companion.  `FAMILY_verify` §D expands, for each of the 41 roots, the ball
of canonical pairs reachable by at most 4 such moves with every relator kept at
`root max + 4` symbols, and asks which census rows that ball meets.

* The residual splits into **16 `R`-preserving components**, of sizes
  6, 5, 4, 4, 3, 3, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2 and five singletons.  The 6-component is
  `{45056, 54514, 62816, 65967, 66543, 70845}` — five rows of family
  `YXYXyXXYXyX` plus `ac19_70845`, whose *own* family is `YYXYXyXXyX`; the
  5-component `{14690, 33080, 49255, 50610, 58570}` crosses three families
  (`YXXXyxx`, `YXYxxYXyxYXyx`, `YYXXYxYxyX`).  So
  the components cut across the first-relator families and are the finer, and
  more meaningful, partition.
* **11 of the 41 have a solved census row inside that ball**; 30 do not.
  Chaining through the components lifts this to **20 rows** that reach a solved
  census row by 1-8 `R`-preserving moves (`FAMILY_THEORY.md` Rule W-TRANSPORT,
  all 20 machine-replayed).
* Every neighbour a residual row meets in its 2-move ball, inside its own
  family, is *another residual row*: at depth 2 with the growing-cap variant,
  the family companions met were unsolved in every single case across the six
  largest families.

## 8. Budget, arm and table: the measured matrix

All 41 rows, one thread, decoded and independently replayed by
`harness.run_row`; every solve below is `verified = True`.  "ball" is the
shipped cap-12 automorphism-closed table; "ball+corpus" adds the certificate
corpus of `FAMILY_THEORY.md` §6.  "cascade" is `final_policy_ball.search` with
the shipped `K3p_c12aut` knobs.

| table | arm | @1,000 | @5,000 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| ball | cascade (`K3p_c12aut`) | **0** | 26 |
| ball | plain `S20` | 14 | 22 |
| ball | `aut_edges` (generator arm) | 3 | 25 |
| ball | `ordinary_T` | **23** | 30 |
| ball+corpus | cascade | 30 | **39** |
| ball+corpus | plain `S20` | 22 | 30 |
| ball+corpus | `aut_edges` | 28 | 37 |
| ball+corpus | `ordinary_T` | 28 | 35 |

Three facts fall straight out.

1. **The cascade's allocation, not its budget, is what fails here.**  At 1,000
   units the shipped cascade solves 0 of the 41; the single `ordinary_T` arm on
   the *same table and the same budget* solves 23.  The cascade spends 250 on
   the donor prepass and 300 on plain `S20` and leaves the routed stage 450.
2. **`ordinary_T` is the right arm for this residual** — ordinary substitution
   edges, `w_weight = 1.5`, the BS/two-block/preflight terminals and
   `bs_escape_weight = 4`.  It beats the generator arm 23-3 at 1,000 units.
3. **The union closes the residual.**  `ball+corpus` cascade at 5,000 solves 39;
   the two it misses, `ac19_11753` and `ac19_38222`, are solved by the
   `ordinary_T` arm on the **bare ball** at 1,163 and 1,156 units.  Across the
   eight configurations every one of the 41 rows is solved and verified.

## 9. Reproduction

```bash
PYTHONPATH=. python3 research/residual_20260909/theory/FAMILY_mine.py --build --families --report
PYTHONPATH=. python3 research/residual_20260909/theory/FAMILY_verify.py --all
```

`FAMILY_verify.py --all` rebuilds the corpus from the published census shards
(about 6 s, 112,261 states) and re-runs the 41 rows under the cascade and the
`ordinary_T` arm; `--all --matrix` reproduces the whole section-8 table (two
tables x four arms x two budgets, about 10 minutes on one thread).  The backward
ball is loaded by `FAMILY_mine.load_ball`, which accepts either the `.npz`
compact table or the `.pkl` dict and, if the manifest is mid-migration, checks
the pinned sha256 itself.
