## 0. Conventions

* Alphabet `xXyY`; uppercase is inverse. A **four-block** relator is a
  cyclically reduced cyclic word `R = y^a x^b y^c x^d` with `a,b,c,d != 0`.
  `exp_x(R) = b+d`, `exp_y(R) = a+c`.
* **Ordinary AC moves** are exactly what `certificate_decoder.replay_elementary`
  accepts: `invert(i)`, `swap`, `conjugate(i, single letter)`,
  `multiply(target, source)`. The engine's substitution move
  `(target, jsign, k1, k2)`, `r_i <- rot_{k1}(r_i) . rot_{k2}(r_j^{jsign})`, is a
  conjugated multiplication and expands into those (this is the census's own
  contract).
* **Ambient Nielsen maps** `x->xy, x->xY, y->yx, y->yX` are *transport*
  (`primitive_patterns.md` sec. 2, audit T2), never emitted as AC moves; they are
  recorded as `{'kind':'automorphism'}` steps and the compact decoder transports
  every later conjugator. We write

  * `beta_k`: `x -> x`, `y -> y x^k` (`|k|` copies of `y->yx` or `y->yX`),
  * `alpha_k`: `y -> y`, `x -> x y^k`.
* **Symmetry group** on exponent tuples: generated by
  `sigma(a,b,c,d) = (b,c,d,a)` (the `x<->y` relabelling), `iota(a,b,c,d) =
  (-c,-b,-a,-d)` (relator inversion), `eps_x(a,b,c,d) = (a,-b,c,-d)`,
  `eps_y(a,b,c,d) = (-a,b,-c,d)`; `sigma^2` is rotation of the block cycle.
  Relator swap is handled by quantifying over both relators. Every hypothesis
  below is stated on the tuple and every recognizer tests all readings, so all
  hypotheses are invariant under this group and under relator swap.
* `|abelian_det| = 1` is a *necessary* condition for normal generation and holds
  on every AC19 row (727/727 residual, 72,779/72,779 census). It is assumed
  throughout and is not an extra hypothesis.

## 1. Exact action of a Nielsen map on a four-block relator

**Lemma N0.** `beta_k` inserts `x^k` immediately to the right of every `y` and
`x^{-k}` immediately to the left of every `Y`. Consequently, for
`R = y^a x^b y^c x^d` four-block and `k != 0`:

* if `|a| = |c| = 1`, `beta_k(R)` is again four-block, with tuple
  ```
  ( a ,  b + k([a>0] - [c<0]) ,  c ,  d + k([c>0] - [a<0]) )
  ```
* if `|a| >= 2` or `|c| >= 2`, `beta_k(y^a) = (y x^k)^a` splits into `|a|` y-blocks
  separated by `|a|-1` copies of `x^k`, so `beta_k(R)` has at least
  `2|a| + 2|c| - 2 > 4` blocks.

*Proof.* Direct expansion; `beta_k(y^{-1}) = x^{-k} y^{-1}`. For `|a| = |c| = 1`
the four inserted letters land on the two x-blocks: a `+1` y-block pushes `+k`
into the x-block after it, a `-1` y-block pushes `-k` into the x-block before
it. □

**Corollary N0.1 (block merging).** A single (composite) Nielsen map lowers the
block count of a four-block relator **iff** `|a| = |c| = 1` and `a = c`
(then `k = -sign(a)·b` or `-sign(a)·d` gives `y^{2a} x^{d-b}` resp.
`y^{2a} x^{b-d}`), or the `x<->y` mirror `|b| = |d| = 1`, `b = d`. In particular
`a = -c` (the Baumslag-Solitar shape) is `beta_k`-**invariant**.

*Machine check.* Census-wide: min block count over the four Nielsen images is 2
on 9,502 rows, and every one of them is in the `merge_pm1` family; it is never
2 for `bs_stable_*` or `generic` (`FOURBLOCK_PATHS.md` sec. 4).

**Negative result N2 — the brief's shear formula is wrong.** The brief proposed
that after `y -> y x^k` the relator "becomes `y^a x^{b+ka} y^c x^{d+kc}`". That
is false as a statement about words: by N0 it holds only when `|a| = |c| = 1`,
and even then the correction terms are `k(sign(a)-[c<0])`, not `ka` and `kc`.
What *is* always true is the abelianised version,
`exp_x(beta_k R) = exp_x(R) + k·exp_y(R)`, and the two agree only in the
`|a| = |c| = 1` case. The brief's follow-up ("find `k` with `b + ka = 0`, needs
divisibility") therefore has the wrong obstruction: no divisibility is ever
needed — `|a| = 1` makes `k` an integer automatically — and the real obstruction
is that `|a| >= 2` destroys the block structure outright.

## 2. Rule F1 — the four-block primitive merge  (**PROVED**, sharp)

**Theorem F1.** Let `(R, W)` have `|det| = 1` and let `R` be four-block with
tuple `(a,b,c,d)` satisfying, up to the symmetry group,

> `|a| = |c| = 1`, `a = c`, and `|b - d| = 1`.

Put `s = sign(a)`, and `k = -s·b` if `|b| <= |d|`, else `k = -s·d`. Then
`beta_k(R) = y^{2a} x^{e}` with `|e| = |b-d| = 1`, a relator containing the
generator `x` exactly once, and `(R, W)` is ordinary-AC trivial. The certificate
is `|k| = min(|b|,|d|)` ambient Nielsen steps followed by the proved T3/T4
compiler.

*Proof.* N0/N0.1 gives `beta_k(R) = y^{2a}x^{d-b}` (or `x^{b-d}`), and
`|b-d| = 1` by hypothesis. A cyclic word containing `x` exactly once is, after a
rotation, `y^{r} x^{eps} y^{s}` and hence (T4, `primitive_patterns.md` sec. 4)
primitive with explicit complement `y`. T3 (sec. 3) then trivialises
`(beta_k R, beta_k W)` provided the companion has exponent sum `+-1` in the
complement; in the basis `(u,v) = (beta_k R, y)` the two abelianised rows are
`(1,0)` and `(e_u,e_v)`, so that exponent is `+-det`, and `det` is unchanged by
an ambient automorphism up to sign, hence `+-1`. Transport back with T2. □

**Theorem F1' (sharp converse).** A four-block cyclic word is primitive **iff**
its tuple lies in the symmetry orbit of `(1,b,1,d)` with `|b-d| = 1`.

*Proof.* (<=) above. (=>) A primitive element of `F(x,y)` with exponent sums
`(p,q)`, `gcd(p,q) = 1`, is conjugate — up to inversion and a signed permutation
of the basis — to the Christoffel/mechanical word of slope `q/p`, in which every
run of the minority letter has length 1 and the majority runs have lengths
differing by at most one. Four blocks forces the minority multiplicity to be 2,
i.e. two y-runs of length 1 (`a = c = 1`) and two x-runs with `|b-d| <= 1`;
`gcd(b+d, 2) = 1` makes `b+d` odd, hence `b-d` odd, hence `|b-d| = 1`. □

*Machine check of F1'.* `verify_classification(5)`: all 10,000 four-block tuples
with entries in `[-5,5]\{0}` — the O(1) tuple test, an independent strict
Nielsen descent, and `christoffel_primitive_gate.recognize_canonical` agree on
every one (**64 primitive, 0 mismatches**). Independently, a BFS over the whole
`Aut(F_2)`-orbit of `x` up to cyclic length 16 produces 160 primitive cyclic
words, 24 of them four-block, and **all 24** are of the merge shape.

**Remark (the parity dichotomy).** With `|a| = |c| = 1` and `a = c`,
`|det| = 1` forces `b+d` odd, so `|b-d|` is *always* odd. Four-block words of
the merge shape therefore split cleanly into `|b-d| = 1` (primitive — this rule)
and `|b-d| >= 3` (the torus relator `y^{2a} x^{+-|b-d|}`, section 7). There is no
even case to worry about: `|b-d|` even admits no unimodular companion at all.

## 3. Lemma L1 — consecutivity is forced, not assumed  (**PROVED**)

**Lemma L1.** Let `R` be four-block with tuple `(a,b,c,d)` and `W` any companion
with `|det(R,W)| = 1`.

* If `a + c = 0` then `|b + d| = 1` **and** `|exp_y(W)| = 1`.
* If `b + d = 0` then `|a + c| = 1` **and** `|exp_x(W)| = 1`.

*Proof.* `det = exp_x(R)·exp_y(W) - exp_y(R)·exp_x(W)`. With `exp_y(R) = 0` this
is `(b+d)·exp_y(W) = +-1`, and a product of two integers is `+-1` only if both
are. □

**Corollary L1.1.** Write a four-block relator with `a+c = 0` as
`t x^b t^{-1} x^d`, `t = y^a`. Then the induced relation is
`t x^b t^{-1} = x^{-d}` with `{|b|, |d|} = {m, m+1}` — i.e. a *consecutive*
Baumslag-Solitar shape `BS(m, m+1)`, always, with `m = min(|b|,|d|)`. Neither
`|m-n| != 1` nor `gcd(m,n) > 1` can occur with a unimodular companion, and the
companion's stable-generator exponent test `|exp| = 1` in
`cheap_gates.bs_gate` / `consecutive_bs._recognize` is likewise automatic.

This *proves* what the audit observed empirically ("of the 19 residual roots
carrying a 4-run BS donor, all 19 are already consecutive with `gcd(m,n) = 1`,
and all 19 have companion stable exponent `+-1`"): the count of non-consecutive
or non-coprime four-block BS donors in *any* AC19 population is 0 by
arithmetic. It also pins down where T5 can fail on such a root: only in the
Britton divisibility scan.

*Machine check.* `check_lemmas`: **21,565 / 21,565** census four-block relators
with a vanishing exponent sum satisfy L1 (stable runs: `s=1` 19,827, `s=2`
1,541, `s=3` 149, `s=4` 42, `s=5` 6); **91 / 91** residual ones do (`s=1` 20,
`s=2` 59, `s=3` 5, `s=4` 7 — exactly the audit's table). 0 counterexamples.

## 4. Theorem N1 — the stable-power extension is vacuous  (**PROVED NEGATIVE**)

The brief's candidate (i) asks whether `consecutive_bs`'s requirement of a
single stable letter can be extended to a stable power `y^a`, `|a| >= 2`, via
`y -> y x^k` or by using the companion. It cannot, for a one-line reason.

**Theorem N1.** Let `R` be four-block with `a + c = 0` and `s = |a| >= 2`, and let
`W` be a companion with `|det(R,W)| = 1`. Then `W` is not an element of
`<y^s, x>`. Hence `W` is not even expressible as a word in the stable letter
`t = y^s` and the base `<x>`, and no Britton/HNN reduction of `W` relative to
`t` exists — the entire `consecutive_bs` machinery (recognizer, preflight and
compiler) is inapplicable to every such row.

*Proof.* `exp_y : F(x,y) -> Z` is a homomorphism killing `x` and sending `y^s` to
`s`, so `exp_y(<y^s,x>) = sZ`. By L1, `|exp_y(W)| = 1`, and `s >= 2` does not
divide 1. □

**Corollary N1.1 (the two escape routes are closed too).**
`beta_k` fixes `R` when `a = -c` (N0.1), and no ambient automorphism changes
`|det|`, so the obstruction is invariant under the Nielsen maps. Using the
companion as a donor to modify `R` changes `exp_y(R)` away from 0 and destroys
the vanishing-exponent shape that defines the family. So neither of the brief's
two suggested repairs can work.

**Consequence for the audit's recommendation.** The audit proposes widening the
donor pattern from `b^{-1} ... b` to `b^{-s} ... b^{s}`, noting it would "newly
recognize" 59 (`s=2`) + 5 (`s=3`) + 7 (`s=4`) = 71 residual relators. It would
recognize exactly those 71 donors and **close 0 of them**: in all 71 the
companion's stable-generator exponent is `+-1` (machine-checked), never a
multiple of `s`. The same holds for the 1,738 census relators of that shape.
Widening the donor pattern alone is provably worthless.

The family is not hopeless — but the fix is a different reading of the relator
(section 6), not a bigger stable letter.

## 5. Rule F3 — twin four-block row reduction  (**PROVED**, tiny footprint)

This is the brief's candidate (iii) worked out.

**Theorem F3.** Let `R`, `S` both be four-block, with `|det(R,S)| = 1`, and
suppose some reading (choice of block rotation and of `R^{+-1}, S^{+-1}`) gives
`R ~ (a,b,c,d)` and `S ~ (a,b',c,d')` — the **same y-block exponents in matching
cyclic positions**. Then:

1. `det = (a+c)·((b+d) - (b'+d'))`, so `|a+c| = 1` **and**
   `|(b+d)-(b'+d')| = 1`. In particular `{|a|,|c|} = {1,2}` whenever
   `min(|a|,|c|) = 1`.
2. One ordinary substitution move `S <- rot(S)·rot(R^{-1})` produces, after free
   and cyclic reduction, `D = y^{-c} x^{p} y^{c} x^{q}` with `p = b'-b`,
   `q = d'-d` and `p + q = +-1`.
3. If exactly one of `p, q` is zero, `D` freely reduces to `x^{+-1}`: the new pair
   is `(x^{+-1}, R)`, closed by T3 with `u = x` (delete every `x` from `R`; what
   is left is `y^{a+c} = y^{+-1}`).
4. Otherwise, if `|c| = 1`, then `p` and `q` have opposite signs with
   `{|p|,|q|} = {m, m+1}`, `m = min(|p|,|q|)`, so `D` is literally a
   consecutive `BS(m,m+1)` donor up to signed relabelling / rotation / inversion
   — `bs_gate(general=True)` accepts it — and the companion `R` automatically has
   `|exp_y(R)| = |a+c| = 1`. Closure is then decided by the Britton preflight.
5. Otherwise `|c| >= 2` and the branch is dead by N1.

*Proof.* (1) `exp(R) = (b+d, a+c)`, `exp(S) = (b'+d', a+c)`; expand the
determinant. (2) `R^{-1}S = x^{-d}y^{-c}x^{-b}y^{-a}·y^{a}x^{b'}y^{c}x^{d'}
= x^{-d}y^{-c}x^{b'-b}y^{c}x^{d'}`, which is cyclically
`y^{-c}x^{p}y^{c}x^{q}`; the engine realises it as one move with `jsign = -1`
and the appropriate rotations (the compiler locates the exact `(k1,k2)` by
replay and asserts the result). (3) `p = 0` collapses `y^{-c}y^{c}`, leaving
`x^{q}`, and (1) forces `|q| = 1`. (4) `p+q = +-1` with `p,q != 0` forces
opposite signs and `||p|-|q|| = 1`; with `|c| = 1` the two y-blocks of `D` are
single letters, which is exactly the shape `donor_orientations` enumerates over
its 8 signed `(a,b)` orientations. □

### Compiler and work

Recognizer: `O(|R|+|S|)`, tries all 4x4 readings and prefers
`terminal > bs > dead`. Compiler: 1 substitution move (located by exhaustive
`replay_move` over the at most `4|R||S|` engine moves) + either
`primitive_completion.complete` (branch 3) or `consecutive_bs.collapse`
(branch 4). Measured over the 63 census closures: work min 6, median 21,
mean 27, max 63 — the cheapest rule in this document by an order of magnitude.

## 6. Conjecture C1 — the correct generalization of T5 (**CONJECTURED**)

N1 says the stable *power* reading is dead. The reading that survives is the
classical Magnus one, which always has a stable letter of run length 1.

Let `R` be four-block with `exp_x(R) = 0`, i.e. `R = y^a x^b y^c x^{-b}`,
`b > 0` after a symmetry. Put `y_i = x^i y x^{-i}`; then `R = y_0^{a} y_b^{c}`,
so `G_R = <x,y | R>` is the HNN extension with **stable letter `x`**, base

```
B  =  ( <y_0> *_{y_0^a = y_b^{-c}} <y_b> )  *  F(y_1, ..., y_{b-1}),
A  = <y_0,...,y_{b-1}>,   A' = <y_1,...,y_b>,   x : y_i -> y_{i+1}.
```

* `b = 1`: `B = <y_0,y_1 | y_0^a = y_1^{-c}>` with associated subgroups `<y_0>`,
  `<y_1>` — this **is** `BS(a,-c)`, consecutive by L1. **T5 is exactly this
  case.**
* `b >= 2` with `min(|a|,|c|) = 1`: one base generator is eliminated, `B` is free
  of rank `b`, and `G_R` is an *ascending* (or descending) HNN extension of a
  free group. Britton's lemma still supplies a normal form, and every Britton
  pinch is an exact application of `R`, hence a legal relator substitution.
* `b >= 2` with `min(|a|,|c|) >= 2`: `B` is a genuine amalgam (a torus-knot group
  times a free group) — harder still.

**Conjecture C1.** If `R` is as above with `min(|a|,|c|) = 1` and the companion
`W` (which has `|exp_x(W)| = 1` by L1) Britton-reduces, relative to stable letter
`x` and base `B`, to a single stable letter, then `(R,W)` is ordinary-AC trivial
by the T5 endgame: the reduced companion expresses `x` as a word in `B`,
substituting it into `R` makes `R` a base word, and the resulting base pair
collapses.

*Status: not proved here.* For `b = 1` the endgame is the one-line computation
`a^{m}·a^{-e}·a^{e}·a^{-n} = a^{m-n} = a^{-1}`; for `b >= 2` the corresponding
step lands in a rank-`b` free base and needs a separate argument. What is
established here is (i) that this is the only reading of the family that is not
excluded by N1, and (ii) that the `b = 2`, `(a,c) = (-2,1)` instance already has
a working proof-carrying compiler in the tree — `stable_square.py`, hard-coded
to the donor `YYXXyxx` and disabled by the frozen policy. That donor is
**56 of the 293 residual four-block roots** (and 1,247 solved census rows), by
far the largest residual sub-family with a known algebraic handle.

## 7. The torus branch (**reduction proved, completion blocked on T6**)

By N0.1 and the parity remark of section 2, a `merge_pm1` relator with
`|b-d| >= 3` merges under `beta_k` to `y^{2a} x^{e}`, `|e| = |b-d| >= 3` odd —
the torus relator with `gcd(2,e) = 1` and both exponents `> 1`, hence
non-primitive. The merge itself is proved and bounded (`min(|b|,|d|)` Nielsen
steps). What is missing is the completion:

* T1 (two-block unimodular) needs the *companion* to be two-block as well — 0
  such census rows in this class;
* T6 (torus/amalgam donor, `primitive_patterns.md` sec. 6) is the right theorem
  and **has no implementation anywhere in the tree**, as the audit records.

Population: **7,831 solved census roots** are Aut-equivalent to `y^2 x^k`
(7,277 to `T(2,3)`, 507 to `T(2,5)`, 46 to `T(2,7/9/11)`, 1 to `T(3,4)`), and
**12 of the 293 residual four-block roots** (10 `T(2,5)`, 1 `T(2,3)`,
1 `T(2,7)`). Status: conjectured pending T6.

**What to implement, in order.**

1. **F1 as a root gate (cheap, safe, immediate).** Replace/augment
   `cheap_gates.one_occurrence_donor` with a four-block *primitive* test:
   compute the block tuple (`O(length)`) and accept when it merges with
   `|b-d| = 1`. The compiler is already in the tree — `primitive_completion.
   complete(pair, donor_word=R)` succeeds because a primitive four-block word
   always strictly Nielsen-descends (verified on every one of the 64 primitive
   tuples in the 10,000-tuple sweep, and on 30/30 planted pairs and 400/400
   sampled census hits, all machine-replayed). This turns
   2,725 census rows from "median 263 search units" into a root-level `O(length)`
   test, and it removes a whole class of rows from stage 2's workload. It buys
   nothing on the residual and should be sold as a cost rule, not a coverage rule.
   The more general form of the same idea — enable `christoffel_primitive_gate`
   *together with* `primitive_completion` (the audit's T10 has a gate but no
   compiler; T3 is the compiler) — is strictly stronger and worth measuring next.
2. **Stop pursuing the stable-power widening (N1).** It is the audit's largest
   suggested cheap win on the residual (71 relators) and it is provably worth
   zero rows. Retarget that effort at C1.
3. **C1 / `stable_square` generalization is the only line with real residual
   reach.** 56 of the 293 residual four-block roots carry the single donor
   `YYXXyxx`; `stable_square.py` already proves that instance and is disabled.
   Generalizing it to `R = y^a x^b y^c x^{-b}` with `min(|a|,|c|) = 1` — Britton
   reduction with stable letter `x` over the rank-`b` free Magnus base — is the
   concrete next theorem. Note it must be evaluated on val, not dev.
4. **F3 only if a twin-four-block state can be *reached*.** At the root it is
   0/727. It may be worth testing as a mid-search terminal (the census's own
   paths pass through many pairs of short relators), but that is a policy
   experiment, not a theorem.
5. **T6 remains the largest unimplemented theorem** (7,831 census rows in its
   class, 12 residual). The merge reduction of section 7 makes its input
   available from four-block roots, so implementing T6 would also serve them.
