# Primary-source audit: power words in fixed virtually free groups

## Bottom line

The cited results support the following two decision procedures for a **fixed** finitely generated virtually free group \(G\):

1. Given explicit periods \(p_i\) and **already instantiated**, binary-encoded integer exponents \(x_i\), decide whether \(p_1^{x_1}\cdots p_n^{x_n}=1\) in \(G\).
2. Given power words \(w_0,w_1,\ldots,w_n\), decide whether the element represented by \(w_0\) lies in the subgroup generated by the elements represented by \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\).

They do **not** compute solution sets when the exponents are variables. In particular, neither paper provides a parametric description, a chamber decomposition, shortlex comparisons, or an algorithm returning terminal letters for arbitrary evaluated power products. Some proofs construct compressed free reductions, normalized periods, folded subgroup graphs, and representatives from a fixed finite transversal; those are intermediate artifacts, not the stronger outputs above.

## Exact decision problems and guarantees

### 1. Instantiated power-product equality

The full Lohrey–Weiß paper defines a power word for a fixed finitely generated group \(G\), with fixed finite symmetric generating alphabet \(\Sigma\), as

\[
(p_1,x_1,p_2,x_2,\ldots,p_n,x_n),\qquad p_i\in\Sigma^*,\quad x_i\in\mathbb Z,
\]

where every \(p_i\) is written explicitly and every \(x_i\) is supplied in binary. The question is whether \(p_1^{x_1}\cdots p_n^{x_n}=1\) in \(G\). See the definition on full-version paper p. 23:4 (PDF p. 4); the same definition is on MFCS paper p. 43:4.

- **Theorem 3** (full version, p. 23:6; MFCS **Theorem 2**, p. 43:6): for a finitely generated free group, this decision problem is \(\mathrm{AC}^0\)-Turing-reducible to the ordinary word problem for \(F_2\).
- **Theorem 7** (full version, p. 23:7; MFCS **Theorem 6**, p. 43:6): if \(H\le G\) has finite index, then \(\mathrm{PowerWP}(G)\) is \(\mathrm{NC}^1\)-many-one-reducible to \(\mathrm{PowerWP}(H)\).
- **Corollary 8** (full version, p. 23:7; MFCS **Corollary 7**, p. 43:6): for every finitely generated virtually free group, the power word problem is \(\mathrm{AC}^0\)-Turing-reducible to the ordinary word problem for \(F_2\), hence is in logspace.

Thus equality of two **instantiated** power products \(U,V\) is covered by applying the identity test to \(UV^{-1}\). This last sentence is a direct reduction, not a separately stated theorem in the papers.

**Output:** a Boolean answer (or, at the theorem level, a circuit reduction producing word-problem query/queries). The theorem does not return a reduced representative of the product, a witness explaining cancellation, or a family of exponent tuples.

### 2. Power-compressed subgroup membership

The journal paper fixes a finitely generated group \(G\), a finite alphabet \(\Sigma\), and a fixed surjection \(\pi:F(\Sigma)\to G\). Its input is a list of power words \(w_0,w_1,\ldots,w_n\) over \(\Gamma=\Sigma\cup\Sigma^{-1}\); periods are explicit and nonzero exponents are binary encoded integers. The question is

\[
\pi(w_0)\in\langle \pi(w_1),\ldots,\pi(w_n)\rangle\le G.
\]

See the definition on pp. 1086–1087, especially p. 1087.

- **Theorem 1** (pp. 1100–1101): for a finitely generated free group, the power-compressed subgroup membership problem is decidable in polynomial time.
- **Lemma 11** (pp. 1101–1102): for a **fixed** finitely generated group \(G\) and a **fixed** finite-index subgroup \(H\le G\), the problem for \(G\) polynomial-time reduces to the problem for \(H\).
- **Corollary 2** (p. 1102): for a **fixed** finitely generated virtually free group, the problem is decidable in polynomial time.

Theorem 1 constructs a strongly folded power-compressed graph \(S(A)\), where \(A=\{w_1,\ldots,w_n\}\), and checks whether a straight-line program for the freely reduced query word is accepted by that deterministic compressed automaton (pp. 1100–1101). This proves the yes/no membership result. It does not state that an expression of \(w_0\) as a product of the subgroup generators is returned.

Lemma 11 is also constructive at an intermediate level. With a fixed normal finite-index subgroup and fixed finite transversal \(R\), its **Fact 1** computes, from a power word \(w\), a power word \(w'\) over the subgroup alphabet and \(r\in R\) such that \(w=w'r\) in \(G\) (p. 1101). Its **Claim 1** computes power-word generators for \(H\cap\langle w_1,\ldots,w_n\rangle\) (p. 1102). These artifacts feed a membership instance in \(H\); the theorem's externally specified output remains Boolean.

## Audit of stronger outputs

| Requested property | Present? | Primary-source assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Parametric solution sets in exponent variables | **No** | The exponents are input integers, not unknowns. Lohrey–Weiß discusses group knapsack on p. 23:3 only to explain that a guessed tuple can be checked by a power-word algorithm; it does not compute all tuples, a semilinear/Presburger description, or any other solution-set representation. The subgroup paper likewise takes fully instantiated power words. |
| Canonical reduced word for the evaluated product | **Not as theorem output** | In Lohrey–Weiß, Lemma 13 (full version pp. 23:8–23:9) preprocesses each period into a freely reduced, cyclically reduced, primitive word chosen lexicographically among its cyclic conjugates and inverse conjugates; it produces an equivalent normalized power word, not the freely reduced expansion of the entire product. In Lohrey's Theorem 1 proof, an SLP \(Q\) for \(\operatorname{red}(w_0)\) is computed as an intermediate step using a cited compressed-word result (pp. 1100–1101). Neither gives a canonical normal-form output for arbitrary elements of a virtually free group. |
| Coset representative | **Yes, but only as a fixed-transversal intermediate** | Lohrey–Weiß Theorem 7 fixes a finite set \(R\) of representatives for \(G/H\) and rewrites the input toward an \(H\)-instance (p. 23:7). Lohrey Lemma 11, Fact 1, explicitly computes \(w'\) and \(r\in R\) with \(w=w'r\) (p. 1101). The transversal is fixed in advance and otherwise arbitrary; no shortest, shortlex, or choice-independent representative is promised. The final decision procedures do not expose a coset-representative output. |
| Terminal-letter or first/last-letter output | **No** | No result returns the terminal letter of the reduced value of an arbitrary power word. Lohrey's Lemma 8 contains local prefix/suffix preservation statements under special hypotheses (p. 1096), and the proof later uses that certain locally reduced words start/end with prescribed periods (p. 1098). These are folding invariants, not a terminal-letter algorithm or output guarantee. |
| Shortlex comparison/order | **No** | Neither paper states an algorithm comparing arbitrary group elements in shortlex order or returning a shortlex representative. Both use an arbitrary order on the alphabet and lexicographic minimization among cyclic permutations of a fixed primitive cyclically reduced period (Lohrey p. 1088; Lohrey–Weiß pp. 23:8–23:9). Since all such cyclic permutations have the same length and the comparison is only within that finite conjugacy/inverse-conjugacy set, this is not a shortlex normal form for the whole group element. |
| Chamber decomposition of exponent space | **No** | There is no exponent parameter space to decompose: all exponents are supplied integers. Neither PDF contains a chamber construction or a piecewise region analysis of exponent tuples. |

## Safe citation-level takeaway

For a fixed finitely generated virtually free group \(G\), the literature cited here gives efficient **decision** algorithms for equality of instantiated power products and for subgroup membership of instantiated power words. It also supplies useful constructive reductions (folded compressed graphs, normalized periods, an SLP for a free reduction, finite-transversal decompositions, and generators for an intersection subgroup). It does **not** support a claim that one can extract parametric exponent solutions, canonical shortlex representatives, terminal letters, shortlex comparisons, or chamber decompositions from these theorems.
