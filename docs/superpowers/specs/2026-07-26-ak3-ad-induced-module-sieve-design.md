# AK(3) A--D induced-module sieve design

Date: 2026-07-26

## Objective

Replace Result 55's three abelian exponent classes by three exact fibers
of the projection

\[
\pi:F(x,t,z,q)\to F(q,z),
\qquad x,t\mapsto1.
\]

## Noncommutative Fox row

For

\[
P_\sigma(c)=A\,cD^\sigma c^{-1},
\qquad
g=\pi(c),
\]

the evaluated Fox row over \(\mathbb Z[F(q,z)]\) is

\[
(3q+\sigma gz,\,-4-\sigma g,\,0,\,0).
\]

Put \(h=qz^{-1}\). A right-module vector annihilates this row if

\[
vh=\frac43v,
\qquad
vg=-4\sigma v.
\]

If \(\langle h,g\rangle\) has rank two, then \((h,g)\) is a free basis
of that subgroup. Assigning the two displayed scalars defines a
one-dimensional character, and induction to \(F(q,z)\) supplies the
annihilating module. Therefore primitivity forces \(g\in\langle h\rangle\).

Since h is not a proper power, write \(g=h^m\). The character condition
then reduces to Result 55's scalar equation

\[
(4/3)^m=-4\sigma.
\]

Finite modular witnesses obstruct every m except

\[
(\sigma,m)=(+1,1),\quad(-1,0),\quad(-1,1).
\]

## Proven target

\[
P_\sigma(c)\text{ primitive}
\Longrightarrow
\begin{cases}
\pi(c)=h,&\sigma=+1,\\
\pi(c)\in\{1,h\},&\sigma=-1.
\end{cases}
\]

This is an exact projection sieve, not a claim that the three surviving
fibers contain primitive words.

## Replay

The verifier will freely reduce the q,z projection, decide literal
membership in the cyclic subgroup generated by h, check the cyclic
modular witnesses, and exercise noncommuting words with the same
abelian exponents as the three residual cases.
