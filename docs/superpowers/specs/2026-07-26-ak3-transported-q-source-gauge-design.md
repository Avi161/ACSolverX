# AK(3) transported-q-source gauge design

Date: 2026-07-26

## Objective

Decide the first q-dependent changed source

\[
Q=q\beta(R)=\beta(qR)
\]

at the fixed AK source-slot checkpoint, including arbitrary later
traffic from \(Q\).

## General mechanism

Let \(R_1,\ldots,R_k\) be q-free retained sources, let \(V\) lie in
their joint normal closure, and let \(\beta\) fix q and satisfy
\(\rho\beta=\rho\), where \(\rho\) kills q. At a checkpoint carrying
the coherent sources \(\beta(R_i)\), manufacture

\[
Q=\beta(qV)=q\beta(V).
\]

With \(\delta_V(q)=qV\), the word \(Q\) is the image of q under
\(\tau=\beta\delta_V\). Deleting it induces

\[
\sigma=\rho\delta_V^{-1}\beta^{-1}.
\]

The coherent source slots return literally to \(R_i\). Modulo their
normal closure, \(\sigma\) equals ordinary q-deletion. Every multiplier
from \(\langle\!\langle Q\rangle\!\rangle\) vanishes.

## AK specialization

Take \(V=R=x^3t^{-4}\). Then

\[
Q=q\beta(R)
\]

is obtained by one multiplication of the q-slot by the
\(\beta(R)\)-slot. Its deletion endpoint is

\[
\left(
R,\;
RBR^{-1},\;
t^{-1}zRxR^{-1}z^{-1}
\right).
\]

Conjugating the middle relator by \(R^{-1}\) gives \(B\), and two
conjugates of \(R^{\pm1}\) return the last relator to \(D\).

## Boundary

The proof requires the changed source to have the coherent form
\(\beta(qV)\), the carrier sources to remain distinct until deletion,
\(\rho\beta=\rho\), and \(Q\) to remain unchanged and be deleted first.
It does not cover arbitrary q-dependent source words, incoherent
transport, loss of the carrier normal closure, a second change to \(Q\),
or primitive-pair compression.
