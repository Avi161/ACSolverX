## 06:26:13 UTC · `c2d5197c`

**A pure signed relabel gives bit-identical results — measured, and an earlier claim here was wrong.** The question was whether a candidate that is only a renaming of another must take the same number of AC sub-moves. It must, and it does: grouped **strictly** — the literal image under one of the 8 signed permutations, no rotation and no cyclic reduction, relator order allowed to swap — there are **23 such groups in the subset-60 pool at budget 1,000 and 7 in the ms640 top-3 at 100,000, and all 30 agree exactly on `solved`, `nodes_explored` and `path_length`. Zero disagreements.**

**The disagreements previously attributed to renaming come from a looser grouping.** `words.relabel_key` runs `canon_pair` *before* minimising over the 8 permutations, so a "relabel-equivalent" class is equivalent up to cyclic reduction and rotation as well, and its members are frequently different starting strings of different length — `XyXyXXXyXyx` (total 21) sits in the same class as `YXYXXXYXY` (total 19), and those are genuinely different searches. Of the 24 path-length disagreements in that loose grouping, **22 contain members of different relator lengths** and the other **2 hold four distinct pure-relabel classes each** (pure rotations, e.g. `xYxYXYxYx` against `yxyxyXYXYx` on presentation 228) — so none of the 24 is a pair of pure relabels. [`RESULTS.md`](../results/stable_ac/cov/cov_top3/RESULTS.md) now carries the distinction beside the 169/48/6 figures, which stand for the loose grouping.

The mechanism is now stated with the invariance: a signed permutation is a letterwise bijection, every child-generation predicate in `expand_node_nj` is a length comparison or an inverse test, and roll / concatenation / free reduction all commute with it, so the two search trees are isomorphic. The only non-invariant component is the heap's third key — the packed state bytes, which break `(total, depth)` ties by byte comparison. That is the sole mechanism by which a pure relabel could diverge, and it fired on none of the 30 groups.
